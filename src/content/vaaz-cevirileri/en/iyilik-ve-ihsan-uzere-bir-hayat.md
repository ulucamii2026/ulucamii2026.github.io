<p class="vz-hitap">Dear congregation! My dear brothers and sisters, who know the value of a warm greeting, a smile and a bowl of soup on the cold days of our life in Europe!</p>

<p>Every Friday we listen to the same verse at the end of the khutba; today we begin by unfolding the meaning of that verse:</p>

<figure class="vz-nass vz-ayet"><figcaption class="vz-nass-bas"><span class="vz-nass-tur">Qur’anic verse</span><span class="vz-nass-konu">The command we hear every Friday</span></figcaption><p lang="ar" dir="rtl" class="vz-arapca">إِنَّ اللَّهَ يَأْمُرُ بِالْعَدْلِ وَالْإِحْسَانِ وَإِيتَاءِ ذِي الْقُرْبَىٰ وَيَنْهَىٰ عَنِ الْفَحْشَاءِ وَالْمُنكَرِ وَالْبَغْيِ يَعِظُكُمْ لَعَلَّكُمْ تَذَكَّرُونَ</p><p class="vz-meal">Truly Allah commands justice, ihsan and generosity towards relatives; He forbids indecency, evil and tyranny. Allah admonishes you in this way, so that you may take heed.</p><p class="vz-kaynak">an-Nahl, 16:90</p></figure>

<p>The religion of Islam aims to secure the happiness of the person in this world and the Hereafter; among the paths to that goal, “being good” and “making goodness prevail on earth” come first. Our Lord says: “We wrote in the Psalms, after the Remembrance, that ‘My righteous servants shall inherit the earth.’” (al-Anbiya’, 21:105) So evil was never destined to last; Pharaoh and Nimrod have left the stage, while the struggle of the doers of good continues. (Kur’an Yolu, vol. 3, pp. 703–⁠705)</p>

<p>In verses and hadiths, goodness is called “khayr”, “birr”, “ihsan” and “ma‘ruf”. Looking after the poor without hurting them and guarding their dignity is ihsan; serving one’s parents with a smiling face is ihsan; doing one’s work in the finest way, mindful of the rights of others, is ihsan.</p>

<p>Today we shall speak of the concepts, of those to whom goodness is owed, and of goodness in its hardest form, and at the end we shall make a pledge. Our main sentence: <strong>The believer who lives as if they see Allah carries goodness to those they know and to those they do not; they answer evil with good and the trace they leave lives on after their death.</strong></p>

Khayr, birr and ma‘ruf</h2><p class="vz-bolum-oz">The Qur’an’s words for goodness: khayr, birr, ihsan and ma‘ruf.</p></header>

<figure class="vz-nass vz-ayet"><figcaption class="vz-nass-bas"><span class="vz-nass-tur">Qur’anic verse</span><span class="vz-nass-konu">True goodness</span></figcaption><p lang="ar" dir="rtl" class="vz-arapca">لَّيْسَ الْبِرَّ أَن تُوَلُّوا وُجُوهَكُمْ قِبَلَ الْمَشْرِقِ وَالْمَغْرِبِ وَلَٰكِنَّ الْبِرَّ مَنْ آمَنَ بِاللَّهِ وَالْيَوْمِ الْآخِرِ وَالْمَلَائِكَةِ وَالْكِتَابِ وَالنَّبِيِّينَ</p><p class="vz-meal">Turning your faces towards the east or the west is not righteousness. The truly righteous are those who believe in Allah, the Last Day, the angels, the Book and the prophets; who give from the wealth they love to relatives, orphans, the needy, stranded travellers, those who ask, and those who have lost their freedom; who establish prayer and give zakat. Such people keep their word when they make an agreement; they are patient in hardship, illness and times of war. These are the truthful, and these are the God-fearing.</p><p class="vz-kaynak">al-Baqara, 2:177; excerpt from the verse</p></figure>

<p>“Birr” is among the most comprehensive concepts of the Qur’an: it embraces every kind of goodness, from faith to worship. From the same root, “berr” is a name of Allah (at-Tur, 52:28) and an attribute of the obedient person. (Kur’an Yolu, vol. 1, pp. 263–⁠264)</p>

<p>In another verse our Lord says: “They ask you what they should spend. Say: Whatever wealth you spend should be for parents, relatives, orphans, the needy and travellers. Whatever good you do, Allah knows it well.” (al-Baqara, 2:215) The word “khayr” used here generally means “good, goodness” in the dictionary, as the opposite of evil; it occurs 176 times in the Qur’an, and every good that will benefit a person in the Hereafter is called by this name. (Kur’an Yolu, vol. 1, pp. 335–⁠338)</p>

<figure class="vz-nass vz-hadis"><figcaption class="vz-nass-bas"><span class="vz-nass-tur">Hadith</span><span class="vz-nass-konu">Goodness is beautiful character</span></figcaption><p lang="ar" dir="rtl" class="vz-arapca">الْبِرُّ حُسْنُ الْخُلُقِ وَالإِثْمُ مَا حَاكَ</p><p class="vz-meal">According to what Nawwas b. Sam‘an (may Allah be pleased with him) narrated, our Prophet said: “Goodness (birr) is beautiful character. Sin is what troubles your conscience and what you would not like people to know.”</p><p class="vz-kaynak">Muslim, Birr, 14–⁠15; al-Tirmidhi, Zuhd, 52</p></figure>

<p>According to what Jabir b. ‘Abdillah (may Allah be pleased with him) reported, our Prophet said: “Every good and fine deed is charity.” (al-Bukhari, Adab, 33; Muslim, Zakat, 52)</p>

<p>How beautifully the following hadith shows the many paths of goodness: “Each joint of a person owes charity every day: reconciling two people is charity, helping someone mount their ride is charity, a kind word is charity, every step taken towards prayer is charity, and removing something harmful from the road is charity.” (Muslim, Zakat, 56; al-Bukhari, Jihad, 128)</p>

<p><strong>A scene from our life in Europe:</strong> Ma‘ruf is our common language. Our Belgian neighbour expects from us not a word of Arabic, but a clean doorstep and a smiling face; goodness is the only language that needs no translation.</p>

<header class="vz-bolum vz-devam" id="ihsan" tabindex="-1"><figure class="vz-bolum-sahne"><img class="vz-resim" src="/media/vaazlar/iyilik-ve-ihsan-uzere-bir-hayat/ihsan-gece-namaz-1600.webp" srcset="/media/vaazlar/iyilik-ve-ihsan-uzere-bir-hayat/ihsan-gece-namaz-480.webp 480w, /media/vaazlar/iyilik-ve-ihsan-uzere-bir-hayat/ihsan-gece-namaz-960.webp 960w, /media/vaazlar/iyilik-ve-ihsan-uzere-bir-hayat/ihsan-gece-namaz-1600.webp 1600w" sizes="(max-width: 640px) calc(100vw - 40px), (max-width: 1024px) calc(100vw - 64px), 736px" width="1600" height="905" alt="In a modest home, a person prays towards the qibla in the glow of a night light, seen from the side; there are raindrops on the window and a pale street lamp outside." loading="lazy" decoding="async"><figcaption>Illustrative scene: At the night prayer</figcaption></figure><h3 class="vz-ara-baslik">Ihsan: living as if we see Allah</h3></header>

<p>“Ihsan” means doing a good deed knowingly and in the best way, worshipping Allah with sincerity, and giving more than is due; in short, willingly going beyond the obligatory minimum. One who acts with ihsan is called a “muhsin”.</p>

<p>In the hadith of Gabriel, our Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him) described ihsan as follows:</p>

<figure class="vz-nass vz-hadis"><figcaption class="vz-nass-bas"><span class="vz-nass-tur">Hadith</span><span class="vz-nass-konu">The hadith of Gabriel: What is ihsan?</span></figcaption><p lang="ar" dir="rtl" class="vz-arapca">أَنْ تَعْبُدَ اللَّهَ كَأَنَّكَ تَرَاهُ فَإِنْ لَمْ تَكُنْ تَرَاهُ فَإِنَّهُ يَرَاكَ</p><p class="vz-meal">According to what ‘Umar b. al-Khattab (may Allah be pleased with him) narrated, Gabriel asked, “What is ihsan?” and our Prophet said: “Ihsan is worshipping Allah as if you see Him. Even though you do not see Him, He sees you.”</p><p class="vz-kaynak">al-Bukhari, Iman, 37; Muslim, Iman, 1</p></figure>

<p>Ihsan is beautiful conduct born of deep reverence and devotion to Allah; it is selfless love directed first to one’s parents and then to all people. “They spend in the way of Allah in ease and in hardship, they restrain their anger, and they forgive people. Allah loves those who do their work with excellence.” (Al ‘Imran, 3:134)</p>

<p>The Qur’an sums up this consciousness as follows: “Spend in the way of Allah; do not cast yourselves into danger with your own hands. Do good; Allah loves those who do good.” (al-Baqara, 2:195)</p>

<figure class="vz-sema"><figcaption><strong>Ihsan: living as if we see Allah</strong>The Gabriel hadith: three steps, one direction.</figcaption><ol class="vz-basamak"><li><span class="vz-basamak-no">1</span><strong>Islam</strong><em>submission</em>Outward devotion.</li><li><span class="vz-basamak-no">2</span><strong>Iman</strong><em>affirmation</em>Faith settled in the heart.</li><li><span class="vz-basamak-no">3</span><strong>Ihsan</strong><em>as if seeing</em>Living as if we see Allah.</li></ol><p class="vz-sema-alt">Even if you do not see Him, He sees you.</p></figure>

<figure class="vz-nass vz-hadis"><figcaption class="vz-nass-bas"><span class="vz-nass-tur">Hadith</span><span class="vz-nass-konu">Allah has commanded ihsan in everything</span></figcaption><p lang="ar" dir="rtl" class="vz-arapca">إِنَّ اللَّهَ كَتَبَ الْإِحْسَانَ عَلَى كُلِّ شَيْءٍ</p><p class="vz-meal">According to what Shaddad b. Aws (may Allah be pleased with him) narrated, our Prophet said: “Almighty Allah has commanded ihsan (doing beautifully) in everything. When you kill, kill well! When you slaughter an animal, slaughter well! Let each of you sharpen his blade and spare his animal suffering!”</p><p class="vz-kaynak">Muslim, Sayd, 57; al-Tirmidhi, Diyat, 14</p></figure>

<figure class="vz-bolum-sahne vz-ara-sahne"><img class="vz-resim" src="/media/vaazlar/iyilik-ve-ihsan-uzere-bir-hayat/usta-itkan-1600.webp" srcset="/media/vaazlar/iyilik-ve-ihsan-uzere-bir-hayat/usta-itkan-480.webp 480w, /media/vaazlar/iyilik-ve-ihsan-uzere-bir-hayat/usta-itkan-960.webp 960w, /media/vaazlar/iyilik-ve-ihsan-uzere-bir-hayat/usta-itkan-1600.webp 1600w" sizes="(max-width: 640px) calc(100vw - 40px), (max-width: 1024px) calc(100vw - 64px), 736px" width="1600" height="905" alt="In a small workshop a craftsman carefully sands a chair leg on the workbench; a plain clock without numerals on the wall shows the morning." loading="lazy" decoding="async"><figcaption>Illustrative scene: Doing one’s work with care</figcaption></figure>

<p><strong>A scene from our life in Europe — ihsan in our work is itqan:</strong> It is narrated that our Prophet said: “Allah loves that when one of you does a piece of work, he does it solidly and carefully.” (al-Bayhaqi, Shu‘ab al-Iman; al-Tabarani, al-Mu‘jam al-Awsat) Its chain of narration contains weakness, but its meaning is supported by the authentic hadith above. The craftsman who finishes his work with care even when the boss does not see, the worker at his bench at 08:00, the shopkeeper who does not hide defects — they live by ihsan. As the saying attributed to ‘Ali has it, a person’s worth is measured by doing their work with ihsan. (DIA, “Ihsan”) A Belgian employer sees the itqan in his Muslim worker’s labour before he ever sees his prayer; loyalty to working hours, keeping appointments and giving each task its due are Islam’s silent message here.</p>

<header class="vz-bolum" id="iyilik-kime" tabindex="-1"><figure class="vz-bolum-sahne"><img class="vz-resim" src="/media/vaazlar/iyilik-ve-ihsan-uzere-bir-hayat/komsu-tabak-1600.webp" srcset="/media/vaazlar/iyilik-ve-ihsan-uzere-bir-hayat/komsu-tabak-480.webp 480w, /media/vaazlar/iyilik-ve-ihsan-uzere-bir-hayat/komsu-tabak-960.webp 960w, /media/vaazlar/iyilik-ve-ihsan-uzere-bir-hayat/komsu-tabak-1600.webp 1600w" sizes="(max-width: 640px) calc(100vw - 40px), (max-width: 1024px) calc(100vw - 64px), 736px" width="1600" height="905" alt="Between the doors of two neighbouring brick houses, one hand passes a covered dish of food to the neighbour’s outstretched hand." loading="lazy" decoding="async"><figcaption>Illustrative scene: A plate for the neighbour</figcaption></figure><h2 class="vaaz-bolum-baslik"><span class="vz-no">II.</span> To whom does goodness go? Rings from near to far</h2><p class="vz-bolum-oz">From parents to the traveller: the seven circles counted in the surah an-Nisa’.</p></header>

<figure class="vz-nass vz-ayet"><figcaption class="vz-nass-bas"><span class="vz-nass-tur">Qur’anic verse</span><span class="vz-nass-konu">The circles of goodness</span></figcaption><p lang="ar" dir="rtl" class="vz-arapca">وَاعْبُدُوا اللَّهَ وَلَا تُشْرِكُوا بِهِ شَيْئًا وَبِالْوَالِدَيْنِ إِحْسَانًا وَبِذِي الْقُرْبَىٰ وَالْيَتَامَىٰ وَالْمَسَاكِينِ وَالْجَارِ ذِي الْقُرْبَىٰ وَالْجَارِ الْجُنُبِ وَالصَّاحِبِ بِالْجَنبِ وَابْنِ السَّبِيلِ وَمَا مَلَكَتْ أَيْمَانُكُمْ إِنَّ اللَّهَ لَا يُحِبُّ مَن كَانَ مُخْتَالًا فَخُورًا</p><p class="vz-meal">Worship Allah and associate nothing with Him. Do good to parents, relatives, orphans, the needy, the near neighbour, the distant neighbour, the companion at your side, the traveller, and those under your authority. Allah never loves the self-satisfied and boastful.</p><p class="vz-kaynak">an-Nisa’, 4:36</p></figure>

<p>The verse lists the circles: parents, relatives, orphans, the needy; the near neighbour (the same block of flats), the distant neighbour (the neighbourhood); the companion at your side (work, school, the tram); the traveller (guests, newcomers, refugees); those we are responsible for (employees, apprentices).</p>

<figure class="vz-sema"><figcaption><strong>To whom is goodness owed? From near to far</strong>An-Nisa’, 4:36: Seven circles, one measure.</figcaption><ol class="vz-halka"><li><strong>Parents</strong><span>The same roof, the same table</span></li><li><strong>Relatives</strong><span>Kinship without estrangement</span></li><li><strong>Orphans and the poor</strong><span>Trusts under our care</span></li><li><strong>Near neighbour</strong><span>The same apartment block, the same row of houses</span></li><li><strong>Distant neighbour</strong><span>The neighbourhood and the street</span></li><li><strong>The companion at your side</strong><span>Workmate, classmate, fellow traveller</span></li><li><strong>Traveller</strong><span>Guests, newcomers, refugees</span></li></ol><p class="vz-sema-alt">The neighbour’s religion is not questioned; their right is what counts.</p></figure>

<p>According to what our mother ‘A’isha (may Allah be pleased with her) narrated, our Prophet said: “Gabriel kept advising me about the neighbour so insistently that I thought he would make the neighbour an heir.” (al-Bukhari, Adab, 28; Muslim, Birr, 140) That is why the neighbour is watched over like an heir; their door is knocked on, they are asked how they are, and their plate is never sent back empty.</p>

<figure class="vz-nass vz-hadis"><figcaption class="vz-nass-bas"><span class="vz-nass-tur">Hadith</span><span class="vz-nass-konu">Eating one’s fill while one’s neighbour goes hungry</span></figcaption><p lang="ar" dir="rtl" class="vz-arapca">لَيْسَ الْمُؤْمِنُ الَّذِي يَشْبَعُ وَجَارُهُ جَائِعٌ</p><p class="vz-meal">According to what Ibn ‘Abbas (may Allah be pleased with him) narrated, our Prophet said: “One who sleeps with a full stomach while their neighbour goes hungry is not (truly) a believer.”</p><p class="vz-kaynak">al-Bukhari, al-Adab al-Mufrad, 112</p></figure>

<p><strong>No one asks the neighbour’s religion; they ask after the neighbour’s right.</strong> The hungry neighbour has a right over the well-fed neighbour, the elderly neighbour over the young neighbour, the lonely neighbour over the large family; this right comes before religion and language.</p>

<p class="vz-hitap">My dear brothers and sisters! Before our European neighbour ever opens the Qur’an and reads it, they read our scales, our words, our doorstep and our smile.</p>

<figure class="vz-nass vz-ayet"><figcaption class="vz-nass-bas"><span class="vz-nass-tur">Qur’anic verse</span><span class="vz-nass-konu">Goodness to those who do not fight you</span></figcaption><p lang="ar" dir="rtl" class="vz-arapca">لَّا يَنْهَاكُمُ اللَّهُ عَنِ الَّذِينَ لَمْ يُقَاتِلُوكُمْ فِي الدِّينِ وَلَمْ يُخْرِجُوكُم مِّن دِيَارِكُمْ أَن تَبَرُّوهُمْ وَتُقْسِطُوا إِلَيْهِمْ إِنَّ اللَّهَ يُحِبُّ الْمُقْسِطِينَ</p><p class="vz-meal">Allah does not forbid you from treating kindly and justly those who do not fight you over religion and do not expel you from your homes. Allah loves those who act justly.</p><p class="vz-kaynak">al-Mumtahana, 60:8</p></figure>

<p>Asma’, daughter of Abu Bakr (may Allah be pleased with her), reports: My mother came to me during the period of the Treaty of Hudaybiya, though she was a polytheist. I asked the Messenger of Allah, “Should I keep ties with her?” and he said, “Yes, keep ties with your mother.” (al-Bukhari, Hiba, 29; Muslim, Zakat, 50) It is narrated that the 8th verse of the surah al-Mumtahana was revealed on this occasion: kindness and justice towards those who do not fight are not forbidden.</p>

<p>The measure is this:</p>

<aside class="vz-olcu" aria-label="What goodness to the non-Muslim neighbour?"><span class="vz-olcu-ikon"><svg viewBox="0 0 24 24" fill="none" stroke="currentColor" stroke-width="1.8" stroke-linecap="round" stroke-linejoin="round" aria-hidden="true" focusable="false"><path d="M12 3v17M8 21h8M5 7h14"/><path d="M5 7l-3 6a3 3 0 0 0 6 0z"/><path d="M19 7l-3 6a3 3 0 0 0 6 0z"/></svg></span><p class="vz-kutu-tur">GUIDANCE FROM FIQH</p><p class="vz-kutu-baslik">What goodness to the non-Muslim neighbour?</p><p>Kindness and justice towards non-Muslims who do not fight are not forbidden (al-Mumtahana, 60:8); voluntary charity, gifts, visits, visiting the sick, condolences and aid make no distinction of religion. That voluntary charity may also be given to dhimmi poor is established by the occasion of revelation of al-Baqara 2:272. Zakat, however, is not given to non-Muslims, and the majority view for fitr is the same. (İlmihal; DIA, “Neighbour”; DIA, “Dhimmi”; DIA, “Birr”)</p></aside>

<figure class="vz-bolum-sahne vz-ara-sahne"><img class="vz-resim" src="/media/vaazlar/iyilik-ve-ihsan-uzere-bir-hayat/tramvay-yer-ver-1600.webp" srcset="/media/vaazlar/iyilik-ve-ihsan-uzere-bir-hayat/tramvay-yer-ver-480.webp 480w, /media/vaazlar/iyilik-ve-ihsan-uzere-bir-hayat/tramvay-yer-ver-960.webp 960w, /media/vaazlar/iyilik-ve-ihsan-uzere-bir-hayat/tramvay-yer-ver-1600.webp 1600w" sizes="(max-width: 640px) calc(100vw - 40px), (max-width: 1024px) calc(100vw - 64px), 736px" width="1600" height="905" alt="On a tram, a young traveller with a backpack stands and offers the empty seat to an elderly passenger, pointing to it with their hand." loading="lazy" decoding="async"><figcaption>Illustrative scene: Giving up one’s seat on the tram</figcaption></figure>

<p>On Belgian streets this measure is lived as follows: clearing the snow from an elderly neighbour’s doorstep, putting the bin out on rubbish day, doing the shopping for an elderly neighbour, taking a plate of sweets to a neighbour on the feast, cooking soup in illness, giving an hour’s help with a move, being careful about noise at night. According to what Abu Dharr (may Allah be pleased with him) narrated, our Prophet said: “Smiling at your brother is charity” (al-Tirmidhi, Birr, 36); indeed, even greeting one’s brother with a cheerful face is a goodness not to be belittled. (Muslim, Birr, 144) According to what Jarir b. ‘Abdillah (may Allah be pleased with him) narrated: “Allah shows no mercy to one who shows no mercy to people.” (Muslim, Fada’il, 66; al-Bukhari, Adab, 18) And according to what ‘Adi b. Hatim (may Allah be pleased with him) narrated: “Protect yourselves from the Fire, even with half a date.” (al-Bukhari, Zakat, 10; Muslim, Zakat, 67) When the child of a Jewish family who served the Messenger of Allah fell ill, he visited the child at home and sat at his bedside (al-Bukhari, Jana’iz, 80); making things easy and not driving people away also belong to this religion. (al-Bukhari, ‘Ilm, 11)</p>

<p class="vz-hitap">Islam’s most powerful message in our life in Europe is being a trustworthy and good neighbour.</p>

<header class="vz-bolum" id="kotulugu-sav" tabindex="-1"><figure class="vz-bolum-sahne"><img class="vz-resim" src="/media/vaazlar/iyilik-ve-ihsan-uzere-bir-hayat/cit-baris-1600.webp" srcset="/media/vaazlar/iyilik-ve-ihsan-uzere-bir-hayat/cit-baris-480.webp 480w, /media/vaazlar/iyilik-ve-ihsan-uzere-bir-hayat/cit-baris-960.webp 960w, /media/vaazlar/iyilik-ve-ihsan-uzere-bir-hayat/cit-baris-1600.webp 1600w" sizes="(max-width: 640px) calc(100vw - 40px), (max-width: 1024px) calc(100vw - 64px), 736px" width="1600" height="905" alt="Over a garden fence one hand passes a box of sweets while the other receives it; a cold garden on both sides of the fence and a flowering branch." loading="lazy" decoding="async"><figcaption>Illustrative scene: On both sides of the fence</figcaption></figure><h2 class="vaaz-bolum-baslik"><span class="vz-no">III.</span> Repel evil with what is most beautiful</h2><p class="vz-bolum-oz">The path that turns enmity into friendship: greeting, a plate of food and patience.</p></header>

<figure class="vz-nass vz-ayet"><figcaption class="vz-nass-bas"><span class="vz-nass-tur">Qur’anic verse</span><span class="vz-nass-konu">The enemy becomes a warm friend</span></figcaption><p lang="ar" dir="rtl" class="vz-arapca">وَلَا تَسْتَوِي الْحَسَنَةُ وَلَا السَّيِّئَةُ ادْفَعْ بِالَّتِي هِيَ أَحْسَنُ فَإِذَا الَّذِي بَيْنَكَ وَبَيْنَهُ عَدَاوَةٌ كَأَنَّهُ وَلِيٌّ حَمِيمٌ ۝ وَمَا يُلَقَّاهَا إِلَّا الَّذِينَ صَبَرُوا وَمَا يُلَقَّاهَا إِلَّا ذُو حَظٍّ عَظِيمٍ</p><p class="vz-meal"><em>Good and evil are not equal. Repel (evil) with what is most beautiful; then you will see that the one between whom and you there was enmity has become a warm friend!</em><br><em>None attains this outcome except those who are patient, and none attains it except those who possess great fortune.</em></p><p class="vz-kaynak">Fussilat, 41:34–⁠35</p></figure>

<p>This is goodness in its hardest form: answering with good those who do evil to us. The believer who greets, who shares their plate, who is patient, turns enmity into friendship. This patience is not weakness; it is the work of those who hold a great share of virtue.</p>

<p>Greeting, plate and patience: this is our measure against racist words, unjust complaints, car-park quarrels and insults on the internet.</p>

<figure class="vz-sema"><figcaption><strong>Repel evil with what is most beautiful</strong>Fussilat, 41:34: Read the scales from the heavier side.</figcaption><div class="vz-denge"><div class="vz-kefe"><p class="vz-kefe-ad">Evil</p><ul><li>Racist words and insults</li><li>Unfounded complaints</li><li>Parking and noise rows</li><li>Insults on the internet</li></ul></div><div class="vz-terazi"><svg viewBox="0 0 24 24" fill="none" stroke="currentColor" stroke-width="1.8" stroke-linecap="round" stroke-linejoin="round" aria-hidden="true" focusable="false"><path d="M12 3v17M8 21h8M5 7h14"/><path d="M5 7l-3 6a3 3 0 0 0 6 0z"/><path d="M19 7l-3 6a3 3 0 0 0 6 0z"/></svg></div><div class="vz-kefe"><p class="vz-kefe-ad">What is better</p><ul><li>Greeting and a smile</li><li>A plate of food</li><li>Patience and gentle words</li><li>Silence in answer to hurtful words</li></ul></div></div><p class="vz-sema-alt">Then the enemy becomes a warm friend.</p></figure>

<figure class="vz-nass vz-hadis"><figcaption class="vz-nass-bas"><span class="vz-nass-tur">Hadith</span><span class="vz-nass-konu">Follow a wrong with a good deed</span></figcaption><p lang="ar" dir="rtl" class="vz-arapca">اتَّقِ اللَّهَ حَيْثُمَا كُنْتَ وَأَتْبِعِ السَّيِّئَةَ الْحَسَنَةَ تَمْحُهَا وَخَالِقِ النَّاسَ بِخُلُقٍ حَسَنٍ</p><p class="vz-meal">According to what Abu Dharr (may Allah be pleased with him) narrated, our Prophet said: “Fear Allah wherever you are; when you do wrong, follow it at once with a good deed so that it wipes the wrong away; and behave towards people with beautiful character.”</p><p class="vz-kaynak">al-Tirmidhi, Birr, 55</p></figure>

<p>Let no sinner despair; “Good deeds wipe away evil deeds.” (Hud, 11:114) <strong>Whoever repels evil with what is most beautiful saves both themselves and the other person.</strong></p>

<p>Answering evil with evil is easy; anyone can do it. Repelling evil with good takes patience, but only it breaks the chain of enmity. A greeting, a smile, a plate melts the ice between us and our neighbour, our workmate, even the one who speaks harshly to us on the street.</p>

<header class="vz-bolum vz-devam" id="her-seye-ragmen" tabindex="-1"><h3 class="vz-ara-baslik">Goodness despite everything: the estranged relative, the unfaithful kin</h3></header>

<p>A Companion came to our Prophet and said: “O Messenger of Allah, I have relatives. I keep ties with them, but they cut ties with me. I do good to them, but they do evil to me. I treat them gently, but they treat me harshly.” Upon this, the Messenger of Allah said:</p>

<figure class="vz-nass vz-hadis"><figcaption class="vz-nass-bas"><span class="vz-nass-tur">Hadith</span><span class="vz-nass-konu">Hot ashes</span></figcaption><p lang="ar" dir="rtl" class="vz-arapca">لَئِنْ كُنْتَ كَمَا قُلْتَ فَكَأَنَّمَا تُسِفُّهُمُ الْمَلَّ وَلاَ يَزَالُ مَعَكَ مِنَ اللَّهِ ظَهِيرٌ عَلَيْهِمْ مَا دُمْتَ عَلَى ذَلِكَ</p><p class="vz-meal">According to what Abu Hurayra (may Allah be pleased with him) narrated, our Prophet said: “If you act as you say, your conduct is hard upon them — as if feeding them hot ashes. As long as you carry on, Allah’s help will be with you against them.” (Meaning)</p><p class="vz-kaynak">Muslim, Birr, 22</p></figure>

<aside class="vz-hikaye" aria-label="Mistah and Abu Bakr’s oath"><p class="vz-kutu-tur">A LESSON FROM HISTORY</p><p class="vz-kutu-baslik">Mistah and Abu Bakr’s oath</p><p>Abu Bakr looked after his relative Mistah, who had been left an orphan as a small child, and continued helping him in adulthood because of his poverty. When Mistah became mixed up in the slander of the ifk incident, Abu Bakr swore he would no longer help him. Upon this, the 22nd verse of the surah an-Nur was revealed; Abu Bakr said, “By Allah, I desire Allah’s forgiveness; I prefer it above all else,” broke his oath and carried on helping. (Kur’an Yolu, vol. 4, p. 63) <strong>Goodness is not goodness where it is reciprocated, but where it is cut off.</strong></p><p class="vz-not">Kur’an Yolu, vol. 4, p. 63; DIA, “Mistah b. Uthatha”.</p></aside>

<figure class="vz-nass vz-ayet"><figcaption class="vz-nass-bas"><span class="vz-nass-tur">Qur’anic verse</span><span class="vz-nass-konu">Let them forgive and overlook</span></figcaption><p lang="ar" dir="rtl" class="vz-arapca">وَلْيَعْفُوا وَلْيَصْفَحُوا أَلَا تُحِبُّونَ أَن يَغْفِرَ اللَّهُ لَكُمْ وَاللَّهُ غَفُورٌ رَّحِيمٌ</p><p class="vz-meal">Let not those among you who are generous and well-off swear that they will no longer give to relatives, the needy and those who emigrated in the way of Allah. Let them forgive and overlook; do you not wish that Allah should forgive you? Allah is Most Forgiving, Most Merciful.</p><p class="vz-kaynak">an-Nur, 24:22; excerpt from the verse</p></figure>

<p>According to what Hudhayfa (may Allah be pleased with him) narrated, our Prophet said: “Do not be a spineless follower (imma‘a) who says, ‘If people do good, we do good, and if they do wrong, we do wrong.’ Make it a principle within yourselves to do good to people when they do good, and not to wrong them when they do wrong.” (al-Tirmidhi, Birr, 63) And true maintaining of family ties (silat al-rahim) is this: “One who merely returns the good they receive from relatives is not the true keeper of ties; it is the one who restores the bond when relatives break it.” (al-Bukhari, Adab, 15)</p>

<p><strong>A scene from our life in Europe:</strong> Are there none among us who have not spoken to their sibling for ten years over an inheritance back home, who carry a hurt from the association for years, who cut off a relative for not coming to a wedding? Whoever takes the first step wins: not the one who waits to be called, but the one who calls; not the one who proves themselves right, but the one who reconciles. Let us also say this plainly: this patience is not consent to oppression; seeking one’s right through lawful means is legitimate, for our Lord commands justice (‘adl) together with ihsan. (an-Nahl, 16:90)</p>

If one or both of them reach old age with you, do not even say “uff” to them! Do not scold them! Speak to both of them kind words that win hearts.</em><br><em>Spread over them the wing of mercy and humility. Pray, saying: “My Lord! Show them mercy, just as they raised me with compassion when I was small.”</em></p><p class="vz-kaynak">al-Isra’, 17:23–⁠24</p></figure>

<p>Our Lord gives the glad tidings and the measure together: “If you do good, you do good for yourselves.” (al-Isra’, 17:7) Let our ihsan to them not remain mere words: a visit once a week, a plate of hot food. We blame no one. May our names never be missing from the visitors’ book, and their names never be missing from the book of supplication.</p>

<p>Ihsan flows not only upwards but also downwards. Our Prophet says: “The best of you are the best to their families.” (al-Tirmidhi, Manaqib, 63) A smiling face for one’s spouse and teaching a grandchild a supplication are also ihsan; our French- and Flemish-speaking grandchildren hear Turkish supplications from their grandparents.</p>

<p class="vz-hitap">Ihsan in the family begins as you step through the door: with a greeting, a smile, and never saying even “uff”.</p>

<figure class="vz-nass vz-ayet"><figcaption class="vz-nass-bas"><span class="vz-nass-tur">Qur’anic verse</span><span class="vz-nass-konu">Even an atom’s weight</span></figcaption><p lang="ar" dir="rtl" class="vz-arapca">فَمَن يَعْمَلْ مِثْقَالَ ذَرَّةٍ خَيْرًا يَرَهُ ۝ وَمَن يَعْمَلْ مِثْقَالَ ذَرَّةٍ شَرًّا يَرَهُ</p><p lang="ar" dir="rtl" class="vz-arapca">هَلْ جَزَاءُ الْإِحْسَانِ إِلَّا الْإِحْسَانُ</p><p class="vz-meal">Whoever does an atom’s weight of good shall see it. Whoever does an atom’s weight of evil shall see it. And the recompense of goodness is nothing but goodness itself.</p><p class="vz-kaynak">az-Zalzala, 99:7–⁠8; ar-Rahman, 55:60</p></figure>

<p>Everyone reaps what they sow: “Those who do good in this world shall find good.” (az-Zumar, 39:10) “The Most Merciful will plant love (in hearts) for them.” (Maryam, 19:96) “The righteous shall dwell in the Blissful Garden.” (al-Infitar, 82:13)</p>

<figure class="vz-nass vz-hadis"><figcaption class="vz-nass-bas"><span class="vz-nass-tur">Hadith</span><span class="vz-nass-konu">I was ill, and you did not visit Me</span></figcaption><p lang="ar" dir="rtl" class="vz-arapca">إِنَّ اللَّهَ عَزَّ وَجَلَّ يَقُولُ يَوْمَ الْقِيَامَةِ يَا ابْنَ آدَمَ مَرِضْتُ فَلَمْ تَعُدْنِي ‏ ‏ قَالَ يَا رَبِّ كَيْفَ أَعُودُكَ وَأَنْتَ رَبُّ الْعَالَمِينَ ‏ ‏ قَالَ أَمَا عَلِمْتَ أَنَّ عَبْدِي فُلاَنًا مَرِضَ فَلَمْ تَعُدْهُ أَمَا عَلِمْتَ أَنَّكَ لَوْ عُدْتَهُ لَوَجَدْتَنِي عِنْدَهُ</p><p class="vz-meal">It is narrated from Abu Hurayra (may Allah be pleased with him) that the Messenger of Allah said: “Allah the Exalted will say on the Day of Resurrection: O son of Adam, I was ill and you did not visit Me. Had you visited him, you would have found Me with him…” (continuing with the sections on food and water)</p><p class="vz-kaynak">Muslim, Birr, 43; hadith qudsi (sacred hadith) (excerpt of the narration)</p></figure>

<p>In the words of Yunus Emre: “If you visit one who is ill, / If you give them a sip of water, / Tomorrow it will come to meet you there, / As if you had drunk the wine of God. // If you see one who is destitute, / If you give them a worn garment, / Tomorrow it will come to you there, / As if God had cut His robe for you.” According to the parable attributed to Mawlana, goodness is like the farmer who opens his granary to seed. (DIA, “Yunus Emre”)</p>

<p><strong>A scene from our life in Europe:</strong> A “get well soon” to a fellow countryman in hospital, an hour of tea in the care home, the patience of the farmer sowing seed under the grey Ardennes sky… The farmer does not reap on the day he sows; but one who never sows never reaps. Today’s small kindness is tomorrow’s great friend.</p>

<figure class="vz-nass vz-ayet"><figcaption class="vz-nass-bas"><span class="vz-nass-tur">Qur’anic verse</span><span class="vz-nass-konu">Unless you give from what you love</span></figcaption><p lang="ar" dir="rtl" class="vz-arapca">لَن تَنَالُوا الْبِرَّ حَتَّىٰ تُنفِقُوا مِمَّا تُحِبُّونَ وَمَا تُنفِقُوا مِن شَيْءٍ فَإِنَّ اللَّهَ بِهِ عَلِيمٌ</p><p class="vz-meal">You shall never attain righteousness until you spend from what you love in the way of Allah. Whatever you spend, Allah knows it well.</p><p class="vz-kaynak">Al ‘Imran, 3:92</p></figure>

<p>The commentators list wealth, rank, knowledge and bodily strength among the loved things; charity should be given from what is precious. (Kur’an Yolu, vol. 1, pp. 629–⁠631) When this verse was revealed, Abu Talha gave away Bayruha, his prized garden facing the Prophet’s Mosque; our Prophet said, “What a profitable treasure!” and asked him to distribute it among his relatives. (al-Bukhari, Zakat, 44; Muslim, Zakat, 42) ‘Umar likewise endowed the income of his share at Khaybar for good causes.</p>

<p>In preparation for Tabuk, ‘Umar brought half his wealth, while Abu Bakr brought all of it. To the question “What have you left for your family?” he answered: “I have left them Allah and His Messenger.” ‘Umar said: “After this I shall never compete with you in anything!” (Abu Dawud, Zakat, 40; al-Tirmidhi, Manaqib, 16) In Mecca he had also bought slaves such as Bilal al-Habashi and set them free. Praising seven Companions including Abu Bakr, our Prophet said: “What a fine man Abu Bakr is…” (al-Tirmidhi, Manaqib, 32)</p>

<figure class="vz-nass vz-hadis"><figcaption class="vz-nass-bas"><span class="vz-nass-tur">Hadith</span><span class="vz-nass-konu">Who began today fasting?</span></figcaption><p lang="ar" dir="rtl" class="vz-arapca">‏ مَنْ أَصْبَحَ مِنْكُمُ الْيَوْمَ صَائِمًا ‏</p><p lang="ar" dir="rtl" class="vz-arapca">‏ فَمَنْ تَبِعَ مِنْكُمُ الْيَوْمَ جَنَازَةً ‏</p><p lang="ar" dir="rtl" class="vz-arapca">‏ فَمَنْ أَطْعَمَ مِنْكُمُ الْيَوْمَ مِسْكِينًا ‏</p><p lang="ar" dir="rtl" class="vz-arapca">‏ فَمَنْ عَادَ مِنْكُمُ الْيَوْمَ مَرِيضًا ‏</p><p lang="ar" dir="rtl" class="vz-arapca">‏ مَا اجْتَمَعْنَ فِي امْرِئٍ إِلاَّ دَخَلَ الْجَنَّةَ ‏</p><p class="vz-meal">According to what Abu Hurayra (may Allah be pleased with him) narrated, our Prophet asked: “Who among you began today fasting?”… Abu Bakr said: “I did!”… “Who among you followed a funeral today?”… “Who among you fed a poor person today?”… “And who among you visited a sick person today?”… Upon this he said: “Whoever possesses these qualities shall surely enter Paradise.”</p><p class="vz-kaynak">Muslim, Zakat (1028)</p></figure>

<p>“Do good to others as Allah has done good to you.” (al-Qasas, 28:77) <strong>Whoever gives from what they love finds what they love.</strong></p>

<figure class="vz-nass vz-ayet"><figcaption class="vz-nass-bas"><span class="vz-nass-tur">Qur’anic verse</span><span class="vz-nass-konu">Help one another in goodness and God-consciousness</span></figcaption><p lang="ar" dir="rtl" class="vz-arapca">وَتَعَاوَنُوا عَلَى الْبِرِّ وَالتَّقْوَىٰ وَلَا تَعَاوَنُوا عَلَى الْإِثْمِ وَالْعُدْوَانِ وَاتَّقُوا اللَّهَ إِنَّ اللَّهَ شَدِيدُ الْعِقَابِ</p><p class="vz-meal">… Help one another in goodness and piety, and do not help one another in sin and wrongdoing. Fear Allah, for Allah’s punishment is severe.</p><p class="vz-kaynak">al-Ma’ida, 5:2; excerpt from the verse</p></figure>

<p>Our associations, our funeral funds, our soup days, our blood-donation appointments are all this verse’s answer in our life in Europe. Giving blood is permissible: “Whoever saves a life, it is as if they had saved all humankind.” (al-Ma’ida, 5:32) The volunteer — whom Belgium calls “vrijwilliger / bénévole” — is among us one of those who do good with excellence. It is narrated that: “The best of people are those most beneficial to people.” (al-Tabarani, al-Mu‘jam al-Awsat; good (hasan) according to al-Albani, weak (da‘if) according to the majority)</p>

<p>When a person departs this world, they leave their wealth and rank behind; the deep trace is the hand that strokes the orphan’s head, the word that wins a heart, and the step that mends a broken heart.</p>

<p>Goodness is not money alone: winning a heart, listening to a child, asking after a neighbour, doing one’s work properly — all of these are goodness.</p>

And woe to those into whose hands Almighty Allah has placed the keys to evil!”</p><p class="vz-kaynak">Ibn Majah, Muqaddima, 19 (237); good (hasan) according to al-Albani</p></figure>

To whom do I owe a kind word? Which estrangement will I end?</strong> Let us call the first name that comes to mind today.</p>
