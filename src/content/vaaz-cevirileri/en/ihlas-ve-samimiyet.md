<figure class="vz-nass vz-dua"><figcaption class="vz-nass-bas"><span class="vz-nass-tur">Supplication</span><span class="vz-nass-konu">Praise, blessings and the opening supplication</span></figcaption><p lang="ar" dir="rtl" class="vz-arapca">الْحَمْدُ لِلَّهِ رَبِّ الْعَالَمِينَ، وَالصَّلَاةُ وَالسَّلَامُ عَلَى رَسُولِنَا مُحَمَّدٍ وَعَلَى آلِهِ وَصَحْبِهِ أَجْمَعِينَ</p><p lang="ar" dir="rtl" class="vz-arapca">قَالَ رَبِّ اشْرَحْ لِي صَدْرِي ۝ وَيَسِّرْ لِي أَمْرِي ۝ وَاحْلُلْ عُقْدَةً مِّن لِّسَانِي ۝ يَفْقَهُوا قَوْلِي</p><p class="vz-meal"><em>All praise belongs to Allah, Lord of the worlds. <p class="vz-hitap">Dear congregation! My brothers and sisters, who remember their Lord even where no one sees, and who put intention into the sweat of their brow!</p>

<p>Let us picture two people standing before the donation box at the mosque door. Both drop the same amount into the box at the same moment. Someone standing beside them would see no difference between the two: the money is the same, the box is the same, the outstretched hand is the same. Yet the lines the angels record may be entirely different. The difference cannot be seen with the eye or held in the hand; its name is intention. As the Qur’an explains this unseen difference, it serves us a glass of milk:</p>

<figure class="vz-nass vz-ayet"><figcaption class="vz-nass-bas"><span class="vz-nass-tur">Qur’anic verse</span><span class="vz-nass-konu">Pure milk</span></figcaption><p lang="ar" dir="rtl" class="vz-arapca">نُّسْقِيكُم مِّمَّا فِي بُطُونِهِ مِن بَيْنِ فَرْثٍ وَدَمٍ لَّبَنًا خَالِصًا سَائِغًا لِّلشَّارِبِينَ</p><p class="vz-meal">… so We give you to drink, from within the animal’s belly, between the digested food and the blood, pure milk that is a delight to those who drink it.</p><p class="vz-kaynak">an-Nahl 16:66; excerpt from the verse</p></figure>

<p>Milk is formed in the animal’s belly between the digested food and the blood; it passes through both and takes nothing from either. It comes out as pure milk: clear, sweet, a pleasure to those who drink it. The word ikhlas (purity of intention) comes from the same root as this “pure”: to separate something from whatever has mixed into it, and so to purify it. Worship with ikhlas is worship into which not one drop of any other intention has been mixed. Our Lord also commands our Prophet to say: “My prayer, my worship (of every kind), my life and my death all belong to Allah, Lord of the worlds. He has no partner.” (al-An‘am 6:162-163)</p>

<p><strong>A scene from our life in Europe:</strong> The alarm that rings at five in the morning, the shift at the factory in Aye, the road to school or to the shop… On a rainy winter morning the same question passes through everyone’s heart: “Why am I getting up?” To ask ourselves this question every morning and to renew our intention is to walk the same road and arrive somewhere else.</p>

<p>The main sentence of what we will speak about today is this: <strong>The believer with ikhlas does their work for Allah alone; even when no one sees, they stay the same; praise does not puff them up and blame does not make them give up; inwardly and outwardly they are one.</strong></p>

<p class="vz-hitap">Ikhlas is to mix nothing else into our worship.</p>

Purifying ourselves and turning to Him alone</h2><p class="vz-bolum-oz">Pure devotion: to do worship and good deeds for Allah alone, purified of showing off.</p></header>

<p>In the dictionary, “ikhlas” means to separate something from whatever has mixed into it and so to purify it; as a term, it means to do acts of worship and good deeds for Allah alone, purified of showing off and of concern for gain. (TDV İslâm Ansiklopedisi, “İhlâs”, XXI, 535–⁠537) The Qur’an calls this “pure devotion”:</p>

<figure class="vz-nass vz-ayet"><figcaption class="vz-nass-bas"><span class="vz-nass-tur">Qur’anic verse</span><span class="vz-nass-konu">Pure devotion belongs to Allah alone</span></figcaption><p lang="ar" dir="rtl" class="vz-arapca">أَلَا لِلَّهِ الدِّينُ الْخَالِصُ</p><p class="vz-meal">Let it be known that pure devotion belongs to Allah alone.</p><p class="vz-kaynak">az-Zumar 39:3; excerpt from the verse</p></figure>

<p>Abu Umama al-Bahili (may Allah be pleased with him) relates: A man asked our Prophet, “What do you say of one who fights for reward and for renown?” He was told, “There is nothing for him”; the man repeated the question three times, and the answer was always the same. Then the Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him) said:</p>

<figure class="vz-nass vz-hadis"><figcaption class="vz-nass-bas"><span class="vz-nass-tur">Hadith</span><span class="vz-nass-konu">Only what is pure is accepted</span></figcaption><p lang="ar" dir="rtl" class="vz-arapca">إِنَّ اللَّهَ لاَ يَقْبَلُ مِنَ الْعَمَلِ إِلاَّ مَا كَانَ لَهُ خَالِصًا وَابْتُغِيَ بِهِ وَجْهُهُ</p><p class="vz-meal">Allah accepts only the deed that is done sincerely, for Him alone, and with His pleasure in mind.</p><p class="vz-kaynak">an-Nasa’i, Jihad 24; its chain is good (hasan)</p></figure>

<p>The man is doing something great, yet reward and renown have both mixed into his intention: what decides is not the greatness of the deed but its purity. Even Iblis is compelled to make an exception when he vows to lead everyone astray: “except Your sincere servants among them”. (al-Hijr 15:39-40) Ikhlas is the servant’s refuge from Satan.</p>

<p>If people see our good deed and thank us, is our ikhlas spoiled? No:</p>

<figure class="vz-nass vz-hadis"><figcaption class="vz-nass-bas"><span class="vz-nass-tur">Hadith</span><span class="vz-nass-konu">The believer’s immediate glad tidings</span></figcaption><p lang="ar" dir="rtl" class="vz-arapca">أَرَأَيْتَ الرَّجُلَ يَعْمَلُ الْعَمَلَ مِنَ الْخَيْرِ وَيَحْمَدُهُ النَّاسُ عَلَيْهِ قَالَ تِلْكَ عَاجِلُ بُشْرَى الْمُؤْمِنِ</p><p class="vz-meal">According to the narration of Abu Dharr (may Allah be pleased with him), the Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him) was asked, “What do you say of someone who does good and whom people praise for it?” He said, “That is the believer’s immediate glad tidings.”</p><p class="vz-kaynak">Muslim, Birr 166</p></figure>

<p>To receive appreciation does not spoil ikhlas; what spoils it is doing the deed for appreciation.</p>

<p><strong>A scene from our life in Europe:</strong> Before Eid, the donor list is being written at the mosque association. Three people who have the same amount written down may have three different hearts: one so that others, seeing it, will give too; one quietly; and one so that they will see. We cannot tell from the outside; reading intentions is not for us. But each of us can ask ourselves: “Let them see, or let Allah see?”</p>

<p class="vz-hitap">If appreciation comes, we give thanks; if it does not, we do not turn from our path.</p>

<p>Our Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him) tied the worth of all deeds to a single measure:</p>

<figure class="vz-nass vz-hadis"><figcaption class="vz-nass-bas"><span class="vz-nass-tur">Hadith</span><span class="vz-nass-konu">Deeds are judged by intentions</span></figcaption><p lang="ar" dir="rtl" class="vz-arapca">إِنَّمَا الأَعْمَالُ بِالنِّيَّةِ وَإِنَّمَا لاِمْرِئٍ مَا نَوَى فَمَنْ كَانَتْ هِجْرَتُهُ إِلَى اللَّهِ وَرَسُولِهِ فَهِجْرَتُهُ إِلَى اللَّهِ وَرَسُولِهِ وَمَنْ كَانَتْ هِجْرَتُهُ لِدُنْيَا يُصِيبُهَا أَوِ امْرَأَةٍ يَتَزَوَّجُهَا فَهِجْرَتُهُ إِلَى مَا هَاجَرَ إِلَيْهِ</p><p class="vz-meal">Deeds are judged by intentions, and everyone will have only what they intended. Whoever migrates for Allah and His Messenger, their migration is for Allah and His Messenger. And whoever migrates for some worldly gain they hope to reach, or for a woman they hope to marry, their migration is for whatever they migrated for.</p><p class="vz-kaynak">Muslim, Imara 155; al-Bukhari, Bad’ al-wahy 1</p></figure>

<p>This hadith holds both glad tidings and a warning: the same journey, the same effort; but if the intention differs, so does the place one arrives at.</p>

<p>The glad tidings of intention are so great that our Lord says in a hadith qudsi (sacred hadith): “If My servant intends to do a good deed and does not do it, I write one good deed (reward) for them. But if they do it, I write for them from ten up to seven hundred times the reward. If (My servant) intends to do an evil deed and does not do it, I do not write it down (as a sin). But if they do it, I write one evil deed (sin) against them.” (Muslim, Iman 204) Even the intention to do good is never wasted; only let the intention be pure.</p>

<figure class="vz-sema"><figcaption><strong>The same task, two intentions</strong>al-Bukhari, Bad’ al-wahy 1: Deeds are judged by intentions.</figcaption><div class="vz-denge"><div class="vz-kefe"><p class="vz-kefe-ad">So that they see</p><ul><li>Work</li><li>Charity</li><li>Prayer</li><li><strong>Lost in the void</strong></li></ul></div><div class="vz-terazi"><svg viewBox="0 0 24 24" fill="none" stroke="currentColor" stroke-width="1.8" stroke-linecap="round" stroke-linejoin="round" aria-hidden="true" focusable="false"><circle cx="12" cy="12" r="9"/><path d="M15.6 8.4l-2.1 5.1-5.1 2.1 2.1-5.1z"/><circle cx="12" cy="12" r=".8" fill="currentColor" stroke="none"/></svg></div><div class="vz-kefe"><p class="vz-kefe-ad">For Allah</p><ul><li>Work</li><li>Charity</li><li>Prayer</li><li><strong>Allah’s pleasure</strong></li></ul></div></div><p class="vz-sema-alt">The same task parts in two directions; intention determines the worth of the deed.</p></figure>

<p>Intention is decisive in ordinary matters too. Our Prophet said to Sa‘d ibn Abi Waqqas (may Allah be pleased with him): “There is no expenditure you make seeking the pleasure of Allah for which you will not be rewarded, even the morsel you put into your wife’s mouth.” (al-Bukhari, Iman 41) And on his return from Tabuk, speaking of those who had stayed behind in Medina because of a valid excuse, he said: “Whatever path you crossed and whatever valley you passed, they were with you all along.” (al-Bukhari, Maghazi 82)</p>

<p>Where Allah looks is also clear:</p>

<figure class="vz-nass vz-hadis"><figcaption class="vz-nass-bas"><span class="vz-nass-tur">Hadith</span><span class="vz-nass-konu">Allah looks at your hearts</span></figcaption><p lang="ar" dir="rtl" class="vz-arapca">إِنَّ اللَّهَ لاَ يَنْظُرُ إِلَى صُوَرِكُمْ وَأَمْوَالِكُمْ وَلَكِنْ يَنْظُرُ إِلَى قُلُوبِكُمْ وَأَعْمَالِكُمْ</p><p class="vz-meal">Allah does not look at your appearances or your wealth; He looks only at your hearts and your deeds.</p><p class="vz-kaynak">Muslim, Birr 34</p></figure>

<p>The Qur’an expresses the same truth through the rite of sacrifice: “Neither their flesh nor their blood reaches Allah; what reaches Him is only your piety.” (al-Hajj 22:37)</p>

<figure class="vz-bolum-sahne vz-ara-sahne"><img class="vz-resim" src="/media/vaazlar/ihlas-ve-samimiyet/atolye-ozen-1600.webp" srcset="/media/vaazlar/ihlas-ve-samimiyet/atolye-ozen-480.webp 480w, /media/vaazlar/ihlas-ve-samimiyet/atolye-ozen-960.webp 960w, /media/vaazlar/ihlas-ve-samimiyet/atolye-ozen-1600.webp 1600w" sizes="(max-width: 640px) calc(100vw - 40px), (max-width: 1024px) calc(100vw - 64px), 736px" width="1600" height="900" alt="In a workshop with beams of light coming through the window, a carpenter carefully sands a wooden panel on the bench; a chisel, plane and saw hang on the wall, and a flask stands on the bench." loading="lazy" decoding="async"><figcaption>Illustrative scene: With care even when no one sees</figcaption></figure>

<p><strong>A scene from our life in Europe:</strong> Our coming to live here in Europe was also a hijra, a migration; our fathers set out for the sake of bread, and there is no shame in seeking one’s bread. At the supermarket till, on the building site, on the cleaning shift, raising children at home: if our intention is “lawful provision, bread for my household”, all of it is written in the ledger of worship.</p>

<p class="vz-hitap">Intention is the key that turns an ordinary day into worship.</p>

<p>The name of ikhlas in our dealings with people is sincerity. In Arabic it is called nasiha (sincere goodwill); in the dictionary the word means being pure and sincerely wanting what is good for another. (TDV İslâm Ansiklopedisi, “Nasihat”) According to the narration of Tamim ad-Dari (may Allah be pleased with him), our Prophet said:</p>

<figure class="vz-nass vz-hadis"><figcaption class="vz-nass-bas"><span class="vz-nass-tur">Hadith</span><span class="vz-nass-konu">Religion is sincerity</span></figcaption><p lang="ar" dir="rtl" class="vz-arapca">الدِّينُ النَّصِيحَةُ قُلْنَا لِمَنْ قَالَ لِلَّهِ وَلِكِتَابِهِ وَلِرَسُولِهِ وَلأَئِمَّةِ الْمُسْلِمِينَ وَعَامَّتِهِمْ</p><p class="vz-meal">Religion is sincerity. We asked, “Towards whom?” He said, “Towards Allah, His Book, His Messenger, the leaders of the Muslims, and all Muslims.”</p><p class="vz-kaynak">Muslim, Iman 95</p></figure>

<p>There are five rings: sincerity towards Allah means worshipping Him alone; sincerity towards His Book means reading it and living by it; sincerity towards His Messenger means following his Sunnah; sincerity towards the leaders means telling the truth with courtesy; and sincerity towards the Muslims at large means wishing them well. Jarir ibn ‘Abdullah (may Allah be pleased with him) also relates: “I pledged allegiance to the Messenger of Allah to perform the prayer, to give zakat, and to be sincere towards every Muslim.” (al-Bukhari, Iman 42) Our duty of truthfulness and justice, however, is owed not only to Muslims but to every human being; we say this not as something drawn from this hadith but as required by the general moral measure of our religion.</p>

<p>Sincerity is not a feeling but an attitude. Whoever is sincere towards Allah avoids the forbidden even when no one is watching. Whoever is sincere towards His Book opens it not only at a funeral but within the life they live. Whoever is sincere towards His Messenger carries the Sunnah not only on the tongue but in their character. And whoever is sincere towards their brother or sister watches over them in their absence.</p>

<p>Knowing the weight of this test, our Prophet also prayed for ikhlas. In a supplication that is narrated, though its chain contains weakness, he is reported to have pleaded after his prayers: “O Allah! Our Lord and Lord of all things! Bind me and my family to You with ikhlas at every moment, in this world and the Hereafter. O Lord of majesty and honour!” (Abu Dawud, Witr 25) It is also reported that ‘Umar (may Allah be pleased with him) prayed: “O Allah! Make my deed righteous, make it purely for You, and leave no share in it for anyone.” (Ahmad ibn Hanbal, az-Zuhd)</p>

<p><strong>A scene from our life in Europe:</strong> The board meeting of the mosque association after the evening prayer. Sincerity means putting oneself forward for a task not so that people will call us “chair” but as the congregation’s trust; it means giving an open account of every cent in the till; and if we have a criticism, it means saying it at the meeting, to the person’s face and with courtesy, rather than behind their back. The same sincerity is sought at home, at the kitchen table when we fill in the tax return, when we report our income to the employer, when we make a truthful declaration to the institutions of the country we live in.</p>

<p class="vz-hitap">Sincerity is being able to say behind someone’s back what we say to their face.</p>

<p>Let us now go to a well that the Qur’an describes. Here ikhlas is taught through the silence that follows an act of help.</p>

<figure class="vz-nass vz-ayet"><figcaption class="vz-nass-bas"><span class="vz-nass-tur">Qur’anic verse</span><span class="vz-nass-konu">He helped, then withdrew into the shade</span></figcaption><p lang="ar" dir="rtl" class="vz-arapca">فَسَقَىٰ لَهُمَا ثُمَّ تَوَلَّىٰ إِلَى الظِّلِّ فَقَالَ رَبِّ إِنِّي لِمَا أَنزَلْتَ إِلَيَّ مِنْ خَيْرٍ فَقِيرٌ</p><p class="vz-meal">So Moses watered their animals for them. Then he withdrew into the shade and prayed, “My Lord! I am in need of whatever good You send down to me!”</p><p class="vz-kaynak">al-Qasas 28:24</p></figure>

<aside class="vz-hikaye" aria-label="At the well of Madyan"><p class="vz-kutu-tur">A LESSON FROM HISTORY</p><p class="vz-kutu-baslik">At the well of Madyan</p><p>When Moses (peace be upon him) reached the water of Madyan, he found a crowd of people there watering their animals. A little way off stood two women holding their flocks back. “What is the matter with you two?” he asked. “We cannot water our animals until the shepherds have watered theirs and withdrawn; and our father is a very old man,” they said. That is what they said about their aged father, whom the commentaries mostly identify as Shu‘ayb. Moses watered their animals for them; no one had asked him to, and he expected nothing from anyone. Then he withdrew into the shade and laid his need before his Lord alone. Before long one of the two came walking up to him, shyly: “My father is calling you, to repay you for watering our animals for us,” she said. This invitation was not a return that Moses had asked of people; it was a gift sent by his Lord. <strong>Moses asked nothing of people; his Lord sent it to him.</strong></p><p class="vz-not">al-Qasas 28:23–⁠25.</p></aside>

<p>To do good, not to expect its return from people, and to lay one’s need before one’s Lord alone: these are the three steps of ikhlas. Whoever says thank you does a beautiful thing, of course; but we do not act so that thanks will come. The Qur’an describes the same spirit in the words of the people of Paradise:</p>

<figure class="vz-nass vz-ayet"><figcaption class="vz-nass-bas"><span class="vz-nass-tur">Qur’anic verse</span><span class="vz-nass-konu">Those who feed others for Allah’s pleasure</span></figcaption><p lang="ar" dir="rtl" class="vz-arapca">وَيُطْعِمُونَ الطَّعَامَ عَلَىٰ حُبِّهِ مِسْكِينًا وَيَتِيمًا وَأَسِيرًا ۝ إِنَّمَا نُطْعِمُكُمْ لِوَجْهِ اللَّهِ لَا نُرِيدُ مِنكُمْ جَزَاءً وَلَا شُكُورًا</p><p class="vz-meal">They give food, though they themselves desire it, to the poor, the orphan and the captive. (And they say:) “We feed you only for Allah’s pleasure; we expect from you neither reward nor thanks.”</p><p class="vz-kaynak">al-Insan 76:8–⁠9</p></figure>

<p><strong>A scene from our life in Europe:</strong> Let us picture an aid parcel left at a door: a hand that walks away without ringing the bell, without taking a photograph, without looking back. In real life it is not so easy; years later we are hurt: “I did that for them, and they did not even thank me”, and we bring up the kindness we showed a relative at every gathering. Surah al-Layl describes the one who gives help in these words: “solely to win the pleasure of their Most High Lord, they give help. And in the end they will be well pleased.” (al-Layl 92:19-21)</p>

<p class="vz-hitap">A good deed done for Allah keeps no ledger of what is owed.</p>

<header class="vz-bolum" id="iman-amel" tabindex="-1"><figure class="vz-bolum-sahne"><img class="vz-resim" src="/media/vaazlar/ihlas-ve-samimiyet/kul-ruzgar-1600.webp" srcset="/media/vaazlar/ihlas-ve-samimiyet/kul-ruzgar-480.webp 480w, /media/vaazlar/ihlas-ve-samimiyet/kul-ruzgar-960.webp 960w, /media/vaazlar/ihlas-ve-samimiyet/kul-ruzgar-1600.webp 1600w" sizes="(max-width: 640px) calc(100vw - 40px), (max-width: 1024px) calc(100vw - 64px), 736px" width="1600" height="900" alt="On a stormy day a heap of ash is scattered by the wind on an empty field; the sky is dark with clouds, bare trees stand in the distance, and there are no people." loading="lazy" decoding="async"><figcaption>Illustrative scene: Ashes tossed about by the wind</figcaption></figure><h2 class="vaaz-bolum-baslik"><span class="vz-no">V.</span> Faith is the root of deeds, ikhlas is their soul</h2><p class="vz-bolum-oz">Faith the root, ikhlas the trunk, the righteous deed the fruit.</p></header>

<p>Ikhlas is not a tree by itself; a tree has a root, a trunk and a fruit. The last verse of Surah al-Kahf gathers this into a single sentence:</p>

<figure class="vz-nass vz-ayet"><figcaption class="vz-nass-bas"><span class="vz-nass-tur">Qur’anic verse</span><span class="vz-nass-konu">Two conditions: a righteous deed, a pure intention</span></figcaption><p lang="ar" dir="rtl" class="vz-arapca">فَمَن كَانَ يَرْجُو لِقَاءَ رَبِّهِ فَلْيَعْمَلْ عَمَلًا صَالِحًا وَلَا يُشْرِكْ بِعِبَادَةِ رَبِّهِ أَحَدًا</p><p class="vz-meal">… So whoever hopes to meet their Lord, let them do deeds that benefit both this world and the Hereafter, and let them associate nothing in the worship of their Lord.</p><p class="vz-kaynak">al-Kahf 18:110; excerpt from the verse</p></figure>

<p>The verse sets two conditions: a righteous deed, and associating no one with the worship of one’s Lord. If one is missing, the other cannot stand. Faith is the root, ikhlas the trunk, the righteous deed the fruit; without a root no trunk can be planted, and if the trunk is rotten the fruit comes out rotten too.</p>

<figure class="vz-sema"><figcaption><strong>Faith is the root, ikhlas the trunk, deeds the fruit</strong>al-Kahf 18:110: Let them do righteous deeds and join none with their Lord.</figcaption><ol class="vz-agac-liste"><li><span class="vz-agac-ikon"><svg viewBox="0 0 24 24" fill="none" stroke="currentColor" stroke-width="1.8" stroke-linecap="round" stroke-linejoin="round" aria-hidden="true" focusable="false"><path d="M12 21V8"/><path d="M12 8c-1.8-1-2.5-2.6-2-4.5 1.8.5 2.7 2.4 2 4.5zM12 8c1.8-1 2.5-2.6 2-4.5-1.8.5-2.7 2.4-2 4.5z"/><path d="M12 12.5c-2-.6-3.2-2-3.2-4 2 .2 3.3 1.8 3.2 4zM12 12.5c2-.6 3.2-2 3.2-4-2 .2-3.3 1.8-3.2 4z"/><path d="M12 17c-2-.6-3.2-2-3.2-4 2 .2 3.3 1.8 3.2 4zM12 17c2-.6 3.2-2 3.2-4-2 .2-3.3 1.8-3.2 4z"/></svg></span><div><p class="vz-agac-ust">Visible fruit</p><strong>Righteous deed</strong></div></li><li class="vz-agac-govde"><span class="vz-agac-ikon"><svg viewBox="0 0 24 24" fill="none" stroke="currentColor" stroke-width="1.8" stroke-linecap="round" stroke-linejoin="round" aria-hidden="true" focusable="false"><path d="M12 2.5V5"/><path d="M9 5h6l1.5 3h-9z"/><path d="M7.5 8c-.6 5 1.7 8 4.5 9 2.8-1 5.1-4 4.5-9z"/><path d="M12 10.4c-1 1.4-1 2.6 0 3.5 1-.9 1-2.1 0-3.5z" fill="currentColor" stroke="none"/><path d="M10.5 17.5h3l-.5 2.5h-2z"/></svg></span><div><p class="vz-agac-ust">Supporting trunk</p><strong>Ikhlas</strong></div></li><li class="vz-agac-kok"><span class="vz-agac-ikon"><svg viewBox="0 0 24 24" fill="none" stroke="currentColor" stroke-width="1.8" stroke-linecap="round" stroke-linejoin="round" aria-hidden="true" focusable="false"><rect x="5.2" y="5.2" width="13.6" height="13.6"/><rect x="5.2" y="5.2" width="13.6" height="13.6" transform="rotate(45 12 12)"/><circle cx="12" cy="12" r="2.1" fill="currentColor" stroke="none"/></svg></span><div><p class="vz-agac-ust">Unseen root</p><strong>Faith</strong></div></li></ol><p class="vz-sema-alt">Beside it, ash tossed about by the wind: Ibrahim 14:18.</p></figure>

<p>Surah al-Mulk also asks of us not “many” deeds but “good” deeds: “It is He who created death and life to test which of you is better in conduct.” (al-Mulk 67:2) It is reported that al-Fudayl ibn ‘Iyad explained this “better” as “the purest and the most correct”: pure meaning done for Allah alone; correct meaning in accordance with the Sunnah. Let us look at one more verse:</p>

<figure class="vz-nass vz-ayet"><figcaption class="vz-nass-bas"><span class="vz-nass-tur">Qur’anic verse</span><span class="vz-nass-konu">Ashes tossed about by the wind</span></figcaption><p lang="ar" dir="rtl" class="vz-arapca">مَّثَلُ الَّذِينَ كَفَرُوا بِرَبِّهِمْ أَعْمَالُهُمْ كَرَمَادٍ اشْتَدَّتْ بِهِ الرِّيحُ فِي يَوْمٍ عَاصِفٍ لَّا يَقْدِرُونَ مِمَّا كَسَبُوا عَلَىٰ شَيْءٍ ذَٰلِكَ هُوَ الضَّلَالُ الْبَعِيدُ</p><p class="vz-meal">The deeds of those who reject their Lord are like ashes blown away by the wind on a stormy day. They gain no benefit from what they have earned. That is a deep error.</p><p class="vz-kaynak">Ibrahim 14:18</p></figure>

<figure class="vz-bolum-sahne vz-ara-sahne"><img class="vz-resim" src="/media/vaazlar/ihlas-ve-samimiyet/kaya-toprak-yagmur-1600.webp" srcset="/media/vaazlar/ihlas-ve-samimiyet/kaya-toprak-yagmur-480.webp 480w, /media/vaazlar/ihlas-ve-samimiyet/kaya-toprak-yagmur-960.webp 960w, /media/vaazlar/ihlas-ve-samimiyet/kaya-toprak-yagmur-1600.webp 1600w" sizes="(max-width: 640px) calc(100vw - 40px), (max-width: 1024px) calc(100vw - 64px), 736px" width="1600" height="900" alt="Under a downpour, the thin layer of soil on a flat rock runs down the rock as muddy water and the face of the rock is left bare; the sky is a dark greenish grey, and there are no people." loading="lazy" decoding="async"><figcaption>Illustrative scene: Soil on the rock</figcaption></figure>

<p>This verse describes the state of those who reject their Lord; judgement belongs to Allah. It is not for us to pass judgement on anyone’s Hereafter; what falls to us is to examine the root of our own deeds. Saying “I am a Muslim” should lead not to complacency but to responsibility; for our Lord addresses the believers too: “do not nullify your charity by reminding people of it and by hurting them.” (al-Baqara 2:264)</p>

<p><strong>A scene from our life in Europe:</strong> On a snowy morning we find the pavement in front of our door cleared; it was our Belgian neighbour who did it. We thank them sincerely. Then we turn back to ourselves: is my prayer, my charity, my donation the fruit of a deep-rooted tree, or ash that the wind will scatter?</p>

<p class="vz-hitap">A deed without faith is like a tree without roots, a deed without ikhlas like a body without a soul.</p>

<header class="vz-bolum" id="riya" tabindex="-1"><h2 class="vaaz-bolum-baslik"><span class="vz-no">VI.</span> Showing off (riya’): Hidden shirk</h2><p class="vz-bolum-oz">Showing off is minor shirk; but good is not abandoned out of fear of showing off.</p></header>

<p>Let us now look at the greatest enemy of ikhlas: riya’, that is, showing off — doing worship and good deeds so that people will see and admire them. Our Prophet worried about this above all:</p>

<figure class="vz-nass vz-hadis"><figcaption class="vz-nass-bas"><span class="vz-nass-tur">Hadith</span><span class="vz-nass-konu">What I fear most</span></figcaption><p lang="ar" dir="rtl" class="vz-arapca">إِنَّ أَخْوَفَ مَا أَخَافُ عَلَيْكُمُ الشِّرْكُ الْأَصْغَرُ ، قَالُوا : وَمَا الشِّرْكُ الْأَصْغَرُ يَا رَسُولَ اللهِ ؟ قَالَ : الرِّيَاءُ</p><p class="vz-meal">“What I fear most for you is minor shirk.” They asked, “What is minor shirk, O Messenger of Allah?” He said, “It is riya’, showing off.” He then went on to say that on the Day of Resurrection Allah will say to them, “Go to those you showed off to in the world, and see whether you find any reward with them.”</p><p class="vz-kaynak">Ahmad ibn Hanbal, al-Musnad, V, 428; good (hasan)</p></figure>

<p>Showing off has two siblings: sum‘a, telling of the good one has done so that it will be talked about; and ‘ujb, being so pleased with one’s own deed that one takes credit for it. In a hadith qudsi, Allah says:</p>

<figure class="vz-nass vz-hadis"><figcaption class="vz-nass-bas"><span class="vz-nass-tur">Hadith</span><span class="vz-nass-konu">The most self-sufficient of partners</span></figcaption><p lang="ar" dir="rtl" class="vz-arapca">أَنَا أَغْنَى الشُّرَكَاءِ عَنِ الشِّرْكِ مَنْ عَمِلَ عَمَلاً أَشْرَكَ فِيهِ مَعِي غَيْرِي تَرَكْتُهُ وَشِرْكَهُ</p><p class="vz-meal">Of all partners, I am the one who has least need of partnership. Whoever does a deed and associates someone else with Me in it, I leave both them and what they associated with Me to each other.</p><p class="vz-kaynak">Muslim, Zuhd 46</p></figure>

<figure class="vz-sema"><figcaption><strong>The scales of riya’ and ikhlas</strong>Showing off proves light, ikhlas weighs heavy.</figcaption><div class="vz-denge"><div class="vz-kefe"><p class="vz-kefe-ad">Showing off</p><ul><li>Let them see</li><li>Let them say</li><li>Let them admire</li><li><strong>Proves light</strong></li></ul></div><div class="vz-terazi"><svg viewBox="0 0 24 24" fill="none" stroke="currentColor" stroke-width="1.8" stroke-linecap="round" stroke-linejoin="round" aria-hidden="true" focusable="false"><path d="M12 3v17M8 21h8M5 7h14"/><path d="M5 7l-3 6a3 3 0 0 0 6 0z"/><path d="M19 7l-3 6a3 3 0 0 0 6 0z"/></svg></div><div class="vz-kefe"><p class="vz-kefe-ad">Ikhlas</p><ul><li>Let Allah see</li><li><strong>Weighs heavy</strong></li></ul></div></div><p class="vz-sema-alt">Minor shirk: riya’ (Ahmad ibn Hanbal, V, 428).</p></figure>

<aside class="vz-hikaye" aria-label="The first three to be called to account"><p class="vz-kutu-tur">A LESSON FROM HISTORY</p><p class="vz-kutu-baslik">The first three to be called to account</p><p>Our Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him) related: Among the first to be called to account on the Day of Resurrection will be a man who fell as a martyr. Allah will remind him of His blessings; the man will say, “I fought for Your sake until I was martyred.” He will be told, “You are lying; you fought so that you would be called ‘brave’, and you were called so.” To the second, who taught knowledge and recited the Qur’an, it will be said, “You did it so that you would be called ‘learned’”; and to the third, a person of great wealth, “You did it so that you would be called ‘generous’.” <strong>Martyrdom, knowledge and generosity are all beautiful; what is called to account is not they themselves but the intention behind them: doing it “so that it would be said”.</strong></p><p class="vz-not">Muslim, Imara 152.</p></aside>

<aside class="vz-olcu" aria-label="Good is not abandoned out of fear of showing off"><span class="vz-olcu-ikon"><svg viewBox="0 0 24 24" fill="none" stroke="currentColor" stroke-width="1.8" stroke-linecap="round" stroke-linejoin="round" aria-hidden="true" focusable="false"><path d="M12 3v17M8 21h8M5 7h14"/><path d="M5 7l-3 6a3 3 0 0 0 6 0z"/><path d="M19 7l-3 6a3 3 0 0 0 6 0z"/></svg></span><p class="vz-kutu-tur">GUIDANCE FROM FIQH</p><p class="vz-kutu-baslik">Good is not abandoned out of fear of showing off</p><p>According to a saying attributed to al-Fudayl ibn ‘Iyad: “To abandon a deed for the sake of people is riya’; to do a deed for the sake of people is shirk; ikhlas is Allah’s saving you from both.” That is, to give up prayer or charity out of the fear that “I may become a show-off” is no solution. The Qur’an also finds giving openly beautiful: “If you give charity openly, how good that is!” (al-Baqara 2:271) (al-Qushayri, al-Risala, chapter on ikhlas)</p></aside>

<p><strong>A scene from our life in Europe:</strong> Renewing the car on credit so that people will see it, for the summer drive home; joining the rivalry over jewellery and halls at a wedding… We accuse no one; the seed of this is in the self of every one of us. Let us ask ourselves: “Am I doing this for Allah, or so that they will see?”</p>

<p class="vz-hitap">Fearing show, we do not abandon good; without abandoning it, we mend our intention.</p>

<header class="vz-bolum" id="ekran" tabindex="-1"><figure class="vz-bolum-sahne"><img class="vz-resim" src="/media/vaazlar/ihlas-ve-samimiyet/sofra-telefon-1600.webp" srcset="/media/vaazlar/ihlas-ve-samimiyet/sofra-telefon-480.webp 480w, /media/vaazlar/ihlas-ve-samimiyet/sofra-telefon-960.webp 960w, /media/vaazlar/ihlas-ve-samimiyet/sofra-telefon-1600.webp 1600w" sizes="(max-width: 640px) calc(100vw - 40px), (max-width: 1024px) calc(100vw - 64px), 736px" width="1600" height="900" alt="On a crowded table are rice, a stew, salad, bread and tea glasses; phones held by three hands photograph the food, and no one’s face is visible." loading="lazy" decoding="async"><figcaption>Illustrative scene: Table and phone</figcaption></figure><h2 class="vaaz-bolum-baslik"><span class="vz-no">VII.</span> Ikhlas in the age of the screen: Three questions before sharing</h2><p class="vz-bolum-oz">The like count can become a new sum‘a: ask your intention before you share.</p></header>

<p>Our forebears used to hide their good deeds in the alms-stone (sadaka tashi: an Ottoman stone pillar with a hollow where the giver leaves a coin and the poor person takes it, neither seeing the other); we, however, live with a screen in our hands. The phone is like a pulpit that reaches hundreds of people with a single tap. Selfies taken on umrah and during tawaf, videos of the sacrifice, photographs of the iftar table, stories showing donation receipts… Behind all of them there may be a good intention, and also the sweet tickle of the like count. The like count can become a new sum‘a. When our Prophet announced that on the day when there is no shade but Allah’s shade, Allah will shade seven kinds of people, he also named this one:</p>

<figure class="vz-nass vz-hadis"><figcaption class="vz-nass-bas"><span class="vz-nass-tur">Hadith</span><span class="vz-nass-konu">While the right hand gives, the left does not know</span></figcaption><p lang="ar" dir="rtl" class="vz-arapca">وَرَجُلٌ تَصَدَّقَ أَخْفَى حَتَّى لاَ تَعْلَمَ شِمَالُهُ مَا تُنْفِقُ يَمِينُهُ</p><p class="vz-meal">According to the narration of Abu Hurayra (may Allah be pleased with him), our Prophet described one of the seven kinds of people whom Allah will shade with His own shade, on the day when there is no shade but His, in these words: “…the one who gives charity and hides it so well that their left hand does not know what their right hand has given.”</p><p class="vz-kaynak">al-Bukhari, Adhan 36; Zakat 16</p></figure>

<p>Is sharing, then, forbidden? No. When a poor group came, the charity brought by a Companion from the Ansar became an example to the others, and our Prophet said, “Whoever starts a good practice in Islam will have their own reward and the reward of those who do it after them.” (Muslim, Zakat 69) To announce an aid campaign, to call others to good, to share useful knowledge is beautiful. The measure is intention and courtesy: let us not show the faces of those who receive help or of children, let us not touch anyone’s privacy, let us not write a caption saying “look what I have done”. Surah Luqman also reminds us: “remember that Allah loves no one who is carried away by pride and full of self-admiration.” (Luqman 31:18)</p>

<figure class="vz-sema"><figcaption><strong>Three questions before sharing</strong>Guidance from fiqh</figcaption><ol class="vz-zincir"><li><span class="vz-zincir-no">1</span><strong>For whom am I sharing this?</strong>For Allah, or for applause?</li><li><span class="vz-zincir-no">2</span><strong>Whom might it hurt, and whose privacy does it touch?</strong>Have I thought of the dignity of those who receive help, and of the children?</li><li><span class="vz-zincir-no">3</span><strong>Would I still have done it if I had not shared it?</strong>Can I say that I would have done it even if no likes had come?</li></ol><p class="vz-sema-alt">If all three are clear, share; if one is doubtful, wait.</p></figure>

<p><strong>A scene from our life in Europe:</strong> In the family WhatsApp group, a photograph of our own good deed slips in among the pictures of the evening table; perhaps it goes to relatives waiting back home with an air of “look what we are doing here”. The test is even stronger for our young people: the worth of a good deed can begin to be measured by the number of followers. But our measure is not followers; it is the pleasure of the Truth, Allah.</p>

<p class="vz-hitap">The screen shows everyone; Allah sees even what never reaches the screen.</p>

<header class="vz-bolum" id="gizli-amel" tabindex="-1"><figure class="vz-bolum-sahne"><img class="vz-resim" src="/media/vaazlar/ihlas-ve-samimiyet/sadaka-tasi-1600.webp" srcset="/media/vaazlar/ihlas-ve-samimiyet/sadaka-tasi-480.webp 480w, /media/vaazlar/ihlas-ve-samimiyet/sadaka-tasi-960.webp 960w, /media/vaazlar/ihlas-ve-samimiyet/sadaka-tasi-1600.webp 1600w" sizes="(max-width: 640px) calc(100vw - 40px), (max-width: 1024px) calc(100vw - 64px), 736px" width="1600" height="900" alt="At night, in an old street beside wooden houses, a stone pillar stands in a secluded corner; the paving stones gleam in the light of a street lamp, and there are no people." loading="lazy" decoding="async"><figcaption>Illustrative scene: The alms-stone</figcaption></figure><h2 class="vaaz-bolum-baslik"><span class="vz-no">VIII.</span> Where no one sees: The blessing of hidden deeds</h2><p class="vz-bolum-oz">The reward of a hidden deed is also hidden.</p></header>

<p>Allah’s finest gift to His servant is the one He gives to the deed that no one knows of. The Qur’an describes those who rise at night to plead with their Lord, and announces to them the reward kept hidden for them:</p>

<figure class="vz-nass vz-ayet"><figcaption class="vz-nass-bas"><span class="vz-nass-tur">Qur’anic verse</span><span class="vz-nass-konu">A hidden reward for a hidden deed</span></figcaption><p lang="ar" dir="rtl" class="vz-arapca">تَتَجَافَىٰ جُنُوبُهُمْ عَنِ الْمَضَاجِعِ يَدْعُونَ رَبَّهُمْ خَوْفًا وَطَمَعًا وَمِمَّا رَزَقْنَاهُمْ يُنفِقُونَ ۝ فَلَا تَعْلَمُ نَفْسٌ مَّا أُخْفِيَ لَهُم مِّن قُرَّةِ أَعْيُنٍ جَزَاءً بِمَا كَانُوا يَعْمَلُونَ</p><p class="vz-meal">Their bodies forsake their beds to worship and call upon their Lord in fear and hope, and they spend for Allah’s sake from what We have provided for them. No one can know what joys are kept in store for them as a reward for what they have done.</p><p class="vz-kaynak">as-Sajda 32:16–⁠17</p></figure>

<p>The reward of a hidden deed is also hidden: “no one can know.” Our Prophet said:</p>

<figure class="vz-bolum-sahne vz-ara-sahne"><img class="vz-resim" src="/media/vaazlar/ihlas-ve-samimiyet/gece-namazi-1600.webp" srcset="/media/vaazlar/ihlas-ve-samimiyet/gece-namazi-480.webp 480w, /media/vaazlar/ihlas-ve-samimiyet/gece-namazi-960.webp 960w, /media/vaazlar/ihlas-ve-samimiyet/gece-namazi-1600.webp 1600w" sizes="(max-width: 640px) calc(100vw - 40px), (max-width: 1024px) calc(100vw - 64px), 736px" width="1600" height="900" alt="At night, in a plain room where moonlight and neighbouring rooftops can be seen through the window, a man stands in prayer on the carpet, seen from the side; a lamp is lit on the side table." loading="lazy" decoding="async"><figcaption>Illustrative scene: The night prayer</figcaption></figure>

<figure class="vz-nass vz-hadis"><figcaption class="vz-nass-bas"><span class="vz-nass-tur">Hadith</span><span class="vz-nass-konu">The night prayer</span></figcaption><p lang="ar" dir="rtl" class="vz-arapca">أَفْضَلُ الصَّلاَةِ بَعْدَ الْفَرِيضَةِ صَلاَةُ اللَّيْلِ</p><p class="vz-meal">After the obligatory prayer, the most virtuous prayer is the night prayer.</p><p class="vz-kaynak">Muslim, Siyam 202; excerpt from the hadith</p></figure>

<figure class="vz-nass vz-hadis"><figcaption class="vz-nass-bas"><span class="vz-nass-tur">Hadith</span><span class="vz-nass-konu">The unassuming servant</span></figcaption><p lang="ar" dir="rtl" class="vz-arapca">إِنَّ اللَّهَ يُحِبُّ الْعَبْدَ التَّقِيَّ الْغَنِيَّ الْخَفِيَّ</p><p class="vz-meal">Allah loves the servant who is pious, rich in heart and unassuming.</p><p class="vz-kaynak">Muslim, Zuhd 11</p></figure>

<p>Our forebears kept this subtlety alive with alms-stones: in the secluded corners of Ottoman mosques stood these stones, where benefactors left their charity and those in need took what they needed without being hurt. (TDV İslâm Ansiklopedisi, “Sadaka”, XXXV, 383–⁠384; “Taş”, XL, 140–⁠144)</p>

<aside class="vz-hikaye" aria-label="Food carried by night"><p class="vz-kutu-tur">A LESSON FROM HISTORY</p><p class="vz-kutu-baslik">Food carried by night</p><p>According to a report, ‘Ali, the son of Husayn, was known as “Zayn al-‘Abidin”, the adornment of those who worship, because of his abundant prostrations in prayer. By night he would carry food on his back and leave it for the poor of Medina. Some families did not know who the help came from; when Zayn al-‘Abidin died the help stopped, and only then did they understand who their benefactor had been. <strong>A good deed done for Allah, even if it is never mentioned, is not lost.</strong></p><p class="vz-not">TDV İslâm Ansiklopedisi, “Zeynelâbidîn”, XLIV, 365–⁠366; after adh-Dhahabi, Siyar, IV, 393.</p></aside>

<figure class="vz-bolum-sahne vz-ara-sahne"><img class="vz-resim" src="/media/vaazlar/ihlas-ve-samimiyet/sessiz-hizmet-1600.webp" srcset="/media/vaazlar/ihlas-ve-samimiyet/sessiz-hizmet-480.webp 480w, /media/vaazlar/ihlas-ve-samimiyet/sessiz-hizmet-960.webp 960w, /media/vaazlar/ihlas-ve-samimiyet/sessiz-hizmet-1600.webp 1600w" sizes="(max-width: 640px) calc(100vw - 40px), (max-width: 1024px) calc(100vw - 64px), 736px" width="1600" height="900" alt="In the kitchen of a hall, a man, seen from behind, washes plates and tea glasses in the sink; empty tables and chairs can be seen beyond the serving window." loading="lazy" decoding="async"><figcaption>Illustrative scene: Silent service</figcaption></figure>

<p><strong>A scene from our life in Europe:</strong> Paying the mosque’s bill without having one’s name written down, sending a student a scholarship without giving one’s name, praying two rak‘as at night and telling no one… These are our alms-stones. “Which good deed today is known only to my Lord?”</p>

<p class="vz-hitap">May everyone have one deed known only to their Lord.</p>

<header class="vz-bolum" id="sidk" tabindex="-1"><figure class="vz-bolum-sahne"><img class="vz-resim" src="/media/vaazlar/ihlas-ve-samimiyet/esnaf-terazi-1600.webp" srcset="/media/vaazlar/ihlas-ve-samimiyet/esnaf-terazi-480.webp 480w, /media/vaazlar/ihlas-ve-samimiyet/esnaf-terazi-960.webp 960w, /media/vaazlar/ihlas-ve-samimiyet/esnaf-terazi-1600.webp 1600w" sizes="(max-width: 640px) calc(100vw - 40px), (max-width: 1024px) calc(100vw - 64px), 736px" width="1600" height="900" alt="A shopkeeper in an apron, face not visible, reaches for tomatoes beside an old pan scale; vegetable crates and paper bags stand behind." loading="lazy" decoding="async"><figcaption>Illustrative scene: The shopkeeper’s scales</figcaption></figure><h2 class="vaaz-bolum-baslik"><span class="vz-no">IX.</span> Sidq: Being the same within and without</h2><p class="vz-bolum-oz">Intention, word and deed looking in the same direction.</p></header>

<p>The face of ikhlas that shows in word and conduct is sidq: truthfulness, that is, being the same within and without. The Qur’an addresses the believers:</p>

<figure class="vz-nass vz-ayet"><figcaption class="vz-nass-bas"><span class="vz-nass-tur">Qur’anic verse</span><span class="vz-nass-konu">Be with the truthful</span></figcaption><p lang="ar" dir="rtl" class="vz-arapca">يَا أَيُّهَا الَّذِينَ آمَنُوا اتَّقُوا اللَّهَ وَكُونُوا مَعَ الصَّادِقِينَ</p><p class="vz-meal">O you who believe! Be mindful of Allah and be with the truthful.</p><p class="vz-kaynak">at-Tawba 9:119</p></figure>

<p>Scholars of Islamic ethics describe sidq in six steps: truthfulness in speech, in intention, in resolve, in holding firm to one’s resolve, in deed, and in one’s religious states. (Kur’an Yolu, commentary on verse 119 of at-Tawba) That is, sidq is not only avoiding lies; it is intention, word and deed looking in the same direction. The Qur’an also warns us against saying what we will not do: “Why do you say what you do not do? Saying what you do not do is most hateful in Allah’s sight.” (as-Saff 61:2-3)</p>

<p>The story of the Bedouin is among the finest examples of sidq:</p>

<figure class="vz-nass vz-hadis"><figcaption class="vz-nass-bas"><span class="vz-nass-tur">Hadith</span><span class="vz-nass-konu">The one who was true to Allah</span></figcaption><p lang="ar" dir="rtl" class="vz-arapca">إِنْ تَصْدُقِ اللَّهَ يَصْدُقْكَ</p><p lang="ar" dir="rtl" class="vz-arapca">صَدَقَ اللَّهَ فَصَدَقَهُ</p><p class="vz-meal">According to what Shaddad ibn al-Had (may Allah be pleased with him) related, a Bedouin man brought the share of spoils that had fallen to him in battle to our Prophet and said, “I did not follow you for this; I followed you so that I might be struck by an arrow right here, die, and enter Paradise.” Our Prophet said, “If you are true to Allah, Allah will be true to you.” Soon afterwards the man was brought in, struck exactly where he had pointed. Our Prophet said, “He was true to Allah, and Allah was true to him.”</p><p class="vz-kaynak">an-Nasa’i, Jana’iz 61</p></figure>

<p>Our Prophet also lists the marks of being one thing within and another without: “When they speak, they lie; when they promise, they break their word; when they are entrusted with something, they betray it.” (al-Bukhari, Iman 24) We read these traits not to accuse anyone but to examine our own selves. The Qur’an speaks of hypocrisy (nifaq) in grave terms (an-Nisa’ 4:145), but straight afterwards it leaves the door open: “Except for those who repent and set their conduct right, who hold fast to Allah and devote their religion to Him alone: they are the exception.” (an-Nisa’ 4:146) Ikhlas is to appear as one is, and to be as one appears; the saying that in our culture is attributed to Mawlana, “Either appear as you are, or be as you appear”, expresses this too.</p>

<figure class="vz-bolum-sahne vz-ara-sahne"><img class="vz-resim" src="/media/vaazlar/ihlas-ve-samimiyet/beyan-masasi-1600.webp" srcset="/media/vaazlar/ihlas-ve-samimiyet/beyan-masasi-480.webp 480w, /media/vaazlar/ihlas-ve-samimiyet/beyan-masasi-960.webp 960w, /media/vaazlar/ihlas-ve-samimiyet/beyan-masasi-1600.webp 1600w" sizes="(max-width: 640px) calc(100vw - 40px), (max-width: 1024px) calc(100vw - 64px), 736px" width="1600" height="900" alt="In lamplight, beside a tea glass and a calculator at a table, a hand fills in blank forms with a pencil; through the window, brick houses in the evening can be seen." loading="lazy" decoding="async"><figcaption>Illustrative scene: Truthfulness at the declaration desk</figcaption></figure>

<p><strong>A scene from our life in Europe:</strong> The lines we put on a CV, the figures on a tax return, the “halal” sign in the shop and the truth about the goods sold… Our children notice inconsistency at once: if a father who says on the phone “tell them Dad isn’t home” says on Friday “do not lie”, which will the child believe? We say this not to accuse but to look at ourselves.</p>

<p class="vz-hitap">The believer is the same person when no one is looking.</p>

<header class="vz-bolum" id="bereket" tabindex="-1"><figure class="vz-bolum-sahne"><img class="vz-resim" src="/media/vaazlar/ihlas-ve-samimiyet/magara-isik-1600.webp" srcset="/media/vaazlar/ihlas-ve-samimiyet/magara-isik-480.webp 480w, /media/vaazlar/ihlas-ve-samimiyet/magara-isik-960.webp 960w, /media/vaazlar/ihlas-ve-samimiyet/magara-isik-1600.webp 1600w" sizes="(max-width: 640px) calc(100vw - 40px), (max-width: 1024px) calc(100vw - 64px), 736px" width="1600" height="900" alt="A beam of light enters through the opening mouth of a dark cave; a green valley is visible outside, and there are no people." loading="lazy" decoding="async"><figcaption>Illustrative scene: Light at the mouth of the cave</figcaption></figure><h2 class="vaaz-bolum-baslik"><span class="vz-no">X.</span> The blessing of ikhlas: The charity that fell to a thief, the prayer that opened the cave</h2><p class="vz-bolum-oz">The charity of a pure intention is not lost; a righteous deed saves us on the day of distress.</p></header>

<p>The blessing of ikhlas is seen both in this world and in the Hereafter. Our Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him) tells us two stories: one is the story of a charity, the other the story of a prayer.</p>

<aside class="vz-hikaye" aria-label="The charity that fell to a thief"><p class="vz-kutu-tur">A LESSON FROM HISTORY</p><p class="vz-kutu-baslik">The charity that fell to a thief</p><p>A man said, “I will certainly give a charity”; he went out with his charity and, without knowing it, placed it in the hand of a thief. In the morning people were saying, “A thief has been given charity!” The man said, “O Allah, praise be to You,” and decided to give charity again; this time his charity fell into the hand of a woman involved in wrongdoing. The people talked again, and the man praised Allah again. On the third night he placed his charity in the hand of a rich man; this time too he said, “Praise be to You, that I was able to give to a thief, to a woman involved in wrongdoing, and to a rich man.” It was said to him: “As for the charity you gave to the thief, perhaps he will give up stealing because of it; the woman may become chaste; and the rich man may take a lesson and spend from what Allah has given him.” <strong>The charity of one whose intention is pure is not lost, even if it falls in the wrong place.</strong></p><p class="vz-not">al-Bukhari, Zakat 14; Muslim, Zakat 78.</p></aside>

<figure class="vz-bolum-sahne vz-ara-sahne"><img class="vz-resim" src="/media/vaazlar/ihlas-ve-samimiyet/anne-baba-sut-1600.webp" srcset="/media/vaazlar/ihlas-ve-samimiyet/anne-baba-sut-480.webp 480w, /media/vaazlar/ihlas-ve-samimiyet/anne-baba-sut-960.webp 960w, /media/vaazlar/ihlas-ve-samimiyet/anne-baba-sut-1600.webp 1600w" sizes="(max-width: 640px) calc(100vw - 40px), (max-width: 1024px) calc(100vw - 64px), 736px" width="1600" height="900" alt="In a bedroom with a window, an elderly woman and man sleep in bed; a standing man, seen from behind, waits at their heads with a bowl in his hand." loading="lazy" decoding="async"><figcaption>Illustrative scene: Milk for the parents</figcaption></figure>

<aside class="vz-hikaye" aria-label="Three people in the cave"><p class="vz-kutu-tur">A LESSON FROM HISTORY</p><p class="vz-kutu-baslik">Three people in the cave</p><figure class="vz-nass vz-hadis"><p lang="ar" dir="rtl" class="vz-arapca">فَإِنْ كُنْتَ تَعْلَمُ أَنِّي فَعَلْتُ ذَلِكَ ابْتِغَاءَ وَجْهِكَ، فَافْرُجْ لَنَا فُرْجَةً نَرَى مِنْهَا السَّمَاءَ</p></figure><p>Three people from earlier communities were caught in the rain on a journey and took shelter in a cave; a rock that broke away from the mountain blocked its mouth. They said, “Think of the righteous deeds you have done for Allah, and pray by means of them; perhaps He will grant deliverance.” The first was a man who had stood by his elderly parents’ heads all night with a bowl of milk so as not to wake them. The second was one who, when about to approach the woman he loved, turned away from sin at the warning “Fear Allah”. The third was one who multiplied the wage his worker had forgotten and handed over all of it rightfully. Each prayed, “O Allah! If You know that I did this seeking Your pleasure, open for us a gap so that we may see the sky”; with each prayer the rock moved aside a little more, and in the end the cave was fully opened. <strong>What will save us on the day we are in distress is only the deed that we do for Allah alone.</strong></p><p class="vz-not">al-Bukhari, Adab 5; Muslim, Dhikr 100.</p></aside>

<p>Let us read these three deeds in today’s language: serving elderly parents; turning away from sin when no one is looking; handing over a worker’s due in full, even increased. Let us ask ourselves: <strong>If I were trapped in the cave, which of my deeds would I present to my Lord?</strong></p>

<figure class="vz-sema"><figcaption><strong>Out of the cave: three deeds</strong>al-Bukhari, Adab 5; Muslim, Dhikr 100.</figcaption><ol class="vz-kartlar"><li><span class="vz-kart-ikon"><svg viewBox="0 0 24 24" fill="none" stroke="currentColor" stroke-width="1.8" stroke-linecap="round" stroke-linejoin="round" aria-hidden="true" focusable="false"><path d="M8 4h8l-.9 5.6c-.3 2 .1 3.6 1 5.6L15 20H9l-1.1-4.8c.9-2 1.3-3.6 1-5.6z"/><path d="M6 21h12"/></svg></span><strong>Serving parents</strong><span class="vz-kart-metin">Waited until dawn.</span></li><li><span class="vz-kart-ikon"><svg viewBox="0 0 24 24" fill="none" stroke="currentColor" stroke-width="1.8" stroke-linecap="round" stroke-linejoin="round" aria-hidden="true" focusable="false"><path d="M12 3l7 2.8V11c0 4.8-3 8.2-7 10-4-1.8-7-5.2-7-10V5.8z"/><path d="M8.8 11.8l2.3 2.3 4.3-4.6"/></svg></span><strong>Chastity</strong><span class="vz-kart-metin">Turned back, fearing Allah.</span></li><li><span class="vz-kart-ikon"><svg viewBox="0 0 24 24" fill="none" stroke="currentColor" stroke-width="1.8" stroke-linecap="round" stroke-linejoin="round" aria-hidden="true" focusable="false"><ellipse cx="12" cy="6.5" rx="6" ry="2.4"/><path d="M6 6.5v4c0 1.3 2.7 2.4 6 2.4s6-1.1 6-2.4v-4"/><path d="M6 10.5v4c0 1.3 2.7 2.4 6 2.4s6-1.1 6-2.4v-4"/><path d="M6 14.5v3c0 1.3 2.7 2.4 6 2.4s6-1.1 6-2.4v-3"/></svg></span><strong>The worker’s due</strong><span class="vz-kart-metin">Grew it and paid in full.</span></li></ol><p class="vz-sema-alt">In your hour of need, which deed will you offer your Lord?</p></figure>

<p><strong>A scene from our life in Europe:</strong> To serve our elderly parents often, as far as our circumstances allow, in our home or near it; to turn away from sin when no one sees us — alone in a hotel room, on a business trip, with only the phone in our hands; to hand over in full at the month’s end the due of a worker who works off the books… None of it is applauded, none of it is photographed.</p>

<p class="vz-hitap">The capital for a day of hardship is the deed done for Allah alone in days of ease.</p>

<header class="vz-bolum" id="sonuc" tabindex="-1"><h2 class="vaaz-bolum-baslik"><span class="vz-no">XI.</span> Conclusion: With a pure heart</h2><p class="vz-bolum-oz">Our ikhlas pledge: an intention for every task, staying the same even when no one sees.</p></header>

<p>Let us conclude with this glad tiding of our Prophet:</p>

<figure class="vz-nass vz-hadis"><figcaption class="vz-nass-bas"><span class="vz-nass-tur">Hadith</span><span class="vz-nass-konu">The one who says it purely from the heart</span></figcaption><p lang="ar" dir="rtl" class="vz-arapca">أَسْعَدُ النَّاسِ بِشَفَاعَتِي يَوْمَ الْقِيَامَةِ مَنْ قَالَ لاَ إِلَهَ إِلاَّ اللَّهُ، خَالِصًا مِنْ قَلْبِهِ أَوْ نَفْسِهِ</p><p class="vz-meal">The person who will most deserve my intercession on the Day of Resurrection is the one who sincerely says from their heart, “La ilaha illa llah.”</p><p class="vz-kaynak">al-Bukhari, ‘Ilm 33</p></figure>

<p>The tongue says the word; but to say it “from the heart, purely” is ikhlas. According to Dhu n-Nun al-Misri, the marks of ikhlas are three: that praise and blame are the same to a person, that they forget to look at the deeds they have done, and that they do not think of themselves as having earned a reward for them. (TDV İslâm Ansiklopedisi, “İhlâs”, XXI, 535–⁠537)</p>

<p>Let us gather what we have discussed today at a glance: let us see ikhlas, its allies and its foes once more in the diagram “Ikhlas and its relatives” in the first section.</p>

<p>Now let us turn all of this into a pledge that we will hang on the door of our heart:</p>

<figure class="vz-sema"><figcaption><strong>The Ikhlas Pledge — on the door of our heart</strong>Seven articles, a pledge for seven days.</figcaption><ol class="vz-cerceve-orta vz-sira"><li><span class="vz-cerceve-no">1</span><strong>I will begin every task with an intention.</strong></li><li><span class="vz-cerceve-no">2</span><strong>I will stay the same even when no one sees.</strong></li><li><span class="vz-cerceve-no">3</span><strong>Every week I will do one good deed in secret.</strong></li><li><span class="vz-cerceve-no">4</span><strong>I will not throw my kindness in anyone’s face.</strong></li><li><span class="vz-cerceve-no">5</span><strong>Before I share, I will ask my intention.</strong></li><li><span class="vz-cerceve-no">6</span><strong>My inside and my outside will be one.</strong></li><li><span class="vz-cerceve-no">7</span><strong>I will not be puffed up by praise nor give up under blame.</strong></li></ol></figure>

<figure class="vz-sozler" id="ihlas-sozlesmesi"><figcaption><span>Let us hang it on the door of our heart</span><strong>The Ikhlas Pledge</strong></figcaption><ol class="vz-soz-liste"><li><strong>I will begin every task with an intention.</strong> When I go to work, to the table, to the mosque, I will say, “For Allah’s pleasure.”</li><li><strong>I will stay the same even when no one sees.</strong> With no boss, no customer, no camera present, I will do my work and my worship with the same care.</li><li><strong>Every week I will do one good deed in secret.</strong> Without having my name written down, without taking a photograph, without telling anyone.</li><li><strong>I will not throw my kindness in anyone’s face.</strong> I will not bring up what I have given, and I will not sulk if no thanks come.</li><li><strong>Before I share, I will ask my intention.</strong> I will not carry my worship and my good deeds onto the screen to show off, and I will not show the face of the one who receives help.</li><li><strong>My inside and my outside will be one.</strong> I will be the same at home and on the tax return as I am in the mosque; I will not say what I have not done.</li><li><strong>I will not be puffed up by praise nor give up under blame.</strong> Every evening I will ask, “For whom did I do it today?”, and close the day with a supplication for ikhlas.</li></ol><p class="vz-sozler-alt"><a href="/vaazlar/en/ikhlas-and-sincerity.pdf#page=13">Printable version: PDF, page 13</a></p></figure>

<p><strong>The believer with ikhlas does their work for Allah alone; even when no one sees, they stay the same; praise does not puff them up and blame does not make them give up; inwardly and outwardly they are one.</strong> As we leave this Friday prayer, let us ask three questions: <strong>For whom did I do it? Would I have done it if no one had seen? Would I have given up if no thanks had come?</strong> This week let us do our first small hidden good deed: telling no one, taking no photograph, so that only our Lord knows.</p>

<header class="vz-bolum" id="hatim-niyazi" tabindex="-1"><figure class="vz-bolum-sahne"><img class="vz-resim" src="/media/vaazlar/ihlas-ve-samimiyet/cemaat-niyaz-1600.webp" srcset="/media/vaazlar/ihlas-ve-samimiyet/cemaat-niyaz-480.webp 480w, /media/vaazlar/ihlas-ve-samimiyet/cemaat-niyaz-960.webp 960w, /media/vaazlar/ihlas-ve-samimiyet/cemaat-niyaz-1600.webp 1600w" sizes="(max-width: 640px) calc(100vw - 40px), (max-width: 1024px) calc(100vw - 64px), 736px" width="1600" height="900" alt="Seven men kneel side by side on the carpet in the white-arched hall of a mosque, praying with open palms." loading="lazy" decoding="async"><figcaption>Illustrative scene: The congregation in supplication</figcaption></figure><h2 class="vaaz-bolum-baslik">Closing the sermon here in Europe: the congregation’s supplication</h2><p class="vz-bolum-oz">We open our hands and our hearts to our Lord.</p></header>

<p class="vz-hitap">My dear brothers and sisters! Let us open our hands and our hearts to our Lord.</p>

<figure class="vz-nass vz-dua"><figcaption class="vz-nass-bas"><span class="vz-nass-tur">Supplication</span><span class="vz-nass-konu">Seeking refuge, praise and blessings</span></figcaption><p lang="ar" dir="rtl" class="vz-arapca">أَعُوذُ بِاللَّهِ مِنَ الشَّيْطَانِ الرَّجِيمِ</p><p lang="ar" dir="rtl" class="vz-arapca">الْحَمْدُ لِلَّهِ رَبِّ الْعَالَمِينَ، وَالصَّلَاةُ وَالسَّلَامُ عَلَى رَسُولِنَا مُحَمَّدٍ وَعَلَى آلِهِ وَصَحْبِهِ أَجْمَعِينَ</p><p class="vz-meal">I seek refuge with Allah from the evil of the accursed Satan. All praise belongs to Allah, Lord of the worlds. May blessings and peace be upon our Prophet Muhammad, his family and all his Companions.</p><p class="vz-kaynak">Praise: al-Fatiha 1:2. <div class="vz-dua-metni"><p>O Allah! Purify our intentions, cleanse our hearts of showing off and of calculations of gain, and grant that we do even our smallest good deed for Your pleasure alone.</p><p>O Allah! Our Lord and Lord of all things! Bind us and our families to You with ikhlas at every moment, in this world and the Hereafter; make our deeds righteous and our intentions pure.</p></div>

<figure class="vz-nass vz-dua"><figcaption class="vz-nass-bas"><span class="vz-nass-tur">Supplication</span><span class="vz-nass-konu">Seeking refuge with Allah from hidden shirk</span></figcaption><p lang="ar" dir="rtl" class="vz-arapca">اللَّهُمَّ إِنِّي أَعُوذُ بِكَ أَنْ أُشْرِكَ بِكَ وَأَنَا أَعْلَمُ، وَأَسْتَغْفِرُكَ لِمَا لا أَعْلَمُ</p><p class="vz-meal">It is reported that our Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him) taught Abu Bakr (may Allah be pleased with him) this supplication: “O Allah! <div class="vz-dua-metni"><p>Our Lord! Protect us from showing off, from self-admiration, and from throwing our kindness in people’s faces; and forgive whatever we have mixed, knowingly or unknowingly, into our deeds.</p><p>O Allah! Make our French- and Dutch-speaking children and grandchildren among Your servants whose words and hearts are true, the same within and without; make them love ikhlas, and make us a good example to them.</p><p>O Allah! Reward with Your mercy those who do good without making their names known, and our volunteers who serve our mosque in silence; grant healing to our sick, peace to our elderly, and forgiveness to our departed.</p><p>O Allah! Grant peace and security to the country we live in, help the oppressed of the earth, and make us among Your servants who are useful and trustworthy to our neighbours and to all humanity.</p><p>Our Lord!
