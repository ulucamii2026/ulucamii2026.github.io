<header class="vz-bolum"><h2 class="vaaz-bolum-baslik" id="iftitah" tabindex="-1">Opening: Faith is a tree</h2></header>

<p class="vz-hitap">Dear congregation! My cherished brothers and sisters, who guard their faith here in Europe like a trust, and who strive to set a good example for their children and their neighbours!</p>

<p>Our neighbour, our colleague or our child’s teacher does not ask us, “How many articles of faith are there?” But every day they watch: Do we keep our word? Do we do our work properly? How do we speak when we are angry? <strong>Our European neighbour reads our lives before ever opening and reading the Qur’an.</strong> Our Lord likens faith and the good word to a tree with a firm root:</p>

<figure class="vz-nass vz-ayet"><figcaption class="vz-nass-bas"><span class="vz-nass-tur">Qur’anic verse</span><span class="vz-nass-konu">A tree with a firm root and branches in the sky</span></figcaption><p lang="ar" dir="rtl" class="vz-arapca">أَلَمْ تَرَ كَيْفَ ضَرَبَ اللَّهُ مَثَلًا كَلِمَةً طَيِّبَةً كَشَجَرَةٍ طَيِّبَةٍ أَصْلُهَا ثَابِتٌ وَفَرْعُهَا فِي السَّمَاءِ ۝ تُؤْتِي أُكُلَهَا كُلَّ حِينٍ بِإِذْنِ رَبِّهَا</p><p class="vz-meal">Have you not seen what kind of parable Allah has given? He likens a good word to a good tree, its root firm and its branches in the sky. That tree yields its fruit at all times, by the leave of its Lord.</p><p class="vz-kaynak">Ibrahim, 14:24–25; excerpt from the verses</p></figure>

<p>The root of a tree lies unseen beneath the soil, but its fruit is there for everyone to see. The root of faith, too, is in the heart; only Allah knows it. Its fruit, however, becomes visible in our prayer, our words, our business dealings, our families and our neighbourliness. Today we shall speak about the qualities of the <strong>complete believer</strong> described by the Qur’an and our Prophet, that is, the believer whose faith has matured and come into fruit. <strong>Faith has its roots in the heart and its fruit in life.</strong></p>

<figure class="vz-nass vz-ayet"><figcaption class="vz-nass-bas"><span class="vz-nass-tur">Qur’anic verse</span><span class="vz-nass-konu">What believers believe in</span></figcaption><p lang="ar" dir="rtl" class="vz-arapca">آمَنَ الرَّسُولُ بِمَا أُنزِلَ إِلَيْهِ مِن رَّبِّهِ وَالْمُؤْمِنُونَ كُلٌّ آمَنَ بِاللَّهِ وَمَلَائِكَتِهِ وَكُتُبِهِ وَرُسُلِهِ لَا نُفَرِّقُ بَيْنَ أَحَدٍ مِّن رُّسُلِهِ وَقَالُوا سَمِعْنَا وَأَطَعْنَا غُفْرَانَكَ رَبَّنَا وَإِلَيْكَ الْمَصِيرُ</p><p class="vz-meal">The Messenger of Allah and the believers have believed in what was sent down to him from his Lord. Each one of them believes in Allah, His angels, His books and His messengers. They said, “We make no distinction between any of His messengers,” and “We have heard and we obey. We seek Your forgiveness, our Lord, and to You is the return.”</p><p class="vz-kaynak">al-Baqara, 2:285</p></figure>

<p>My dear brothers and sisters! <strong>Faith</strong> is to affirm with the heart the definitively known teachings that our Prophet brought from Allah, and to accept without hesitation whatever he has told us. A person who declares this acceptance with the tongue is called a <strong>believer</strong>. One who rejects it is called a disbeliever, and one who says “I believe” with the tongue but does not believe in the heart is called a hypocrite.</p>

<p>Sunni scholars distinguish between the foundation of faith and its perfection: righteous action is not a part of faith; therefore a believer who sins does not leave faith. But action nourishes faith, strengthens it and brings it to maturity. A tree that has a root but bears no fruit has not withered; it is waiting for its gardener’s labour. <strong>Faith saves through its foundation and raises through its perfection.</strong></p>

<figure class="vz-nass vz-hadis"><figcaption class="vz-nass-bas"><span class="vz-nass-tur">Hadith</span><span class="vz-nass-konu">The branches of faith</span></figcaption><p lang="ar" dir="rtl" class="vz-arapca">الْإِيمَانُ بِضْعٌ وَسَبْعُونَ أَوْ بِضْعٌ وَسِتُّونَ شُعْبَةً، فَأَفْضَلُهَا قَوْلُ لَا إِلَهَ إِلَّا اللَّهُ، وَأَدْنَاهَا إِمَاطَةُ الْأَذَى عَنِ الطَّرِيقِ، وَالْحَيَاءُ شُعْبَةٌ مِنَ الْإِيمَانِ</p><p class="vz-meal">Faith has seventy-odd branches, or sixty-odd branches. The highest of them is the saying “La ilaha illa Allah”, and the lowest is removing something harmful from the road. Modesty is also a branch of faith.</p><p class="vz-kaynak">Muslim, Iman, 58 (35)</p></figure>

<p>The highest branch of faith is tawhid (declaring Allah’s oneness); the lowest is removing an obstacle from the road. So faith is a broad way of life, reaching from the word of tawhid in the heart to a stone on the pavement. In another hadith our Prophet ties the perfection of faith to brotherhood: “None of you is a (complete) believer until he wants for his brother what he wants for himself.” (al-Bukhari, Iman, 7; Muslim, Iman, 71) The scholars understood the words “is not a believer” here as negating not the foundation of faith but <strong>its perfection</strong>. (al-Nawawi, Sharh Muslim, II, 16)</p>

<figure class="vz-sema"><figcaption><strong>The branches of faith</strong>A broad way of life, reaching from the word of tawhid in the heart to a stone on the pavement.</figcaption><ol class="vz-sube"><li><span class="vz-sube-ust">Highest</span><strong>La ilaha illa Allah</strong>Tawhid in the heart and on the tongue</li><li><span class="vz-sube-ust">In between</span><strong>Seventy-odd branches</strong>Modesty is also a branch of faith</li><li><span class="vz-sube-ust">Lowest</span><strong>Removing something harmful from the road</strong>Moving a stone off the pavement is also a branch of faith</li></ol></figure>

<p>The Qur’an too says of those whose hearts tremble when Allah is mentioned, who perform the prayer and who spend from their provision, “They are the true believers.” (al-Anfal, 8:2–4) Now let us take up the qualities of this true believer one by one.</p>

<header class="vz-bolum"><figure class="vz-bolum-sahne"><img class="vz-resim" src="/media/vaazlar/kamil-muminin-ozellikleri-imanin-meyvesi/husu-1600.webp" srcset="/media/vaazlar/kamil-muminin-ozellikleri-imanin-meyvesi/husu-480.webp 480w, /media/vaazlar/kamil-muminin-ozellikleri-imanin-meyvesi/husu-960.webp 960w, /media/vaazlar/kamil-muminin-ozellikleri-imanin-meyvesi/husu-1600.webp 1600w" sizes="(max-width: 640px) calc(100vw - 40px), (max-width: 1024px) calc(100vw - 64px), 736px" width="1600" height="893" alt="In the prayer hall of a simple, light-filled mosque, a middle-aged man in socks stands on a red carpet, seen from behind; his hands are folded below his chest and his head is slightly bowed as he prays; ahead of him are a plain mihrab with no inscription and a wooden minbar." loading="lazy" decoding="async"><figcaption>Illustrative scene: Prayer with khushu‘</figcaption></figure><h2 class="vaaz-bolum-baslik" id="namazda-husu" tabindex="-1"><span class="vz-no">II.</span> The complete believer has khushu‘ in prayer</h2><p class="vz-bolum-oz">Surah al-Mu’minun begins with prayer and ends with prayer; all the other qualities find their place around prayer.</p></header>

<figure class="vz-nass vz-ayet"><figcaption class="vz-nass-bas"><span class="vz-nass-tur">Qur’anic verse</span><span class="vz-nass-konu">The believers who attain salvation</span></figcaption><p lang="ar" dir="rtl" class="vz-arapca">قَدْ أَفْلَحَ الْمُؤْمِنُونَ ۝ الَّذِينَ هُمْ فِي صَلَاتِهِمْ خَاشِعُونَ</p><p class="vz-meal">The believers have certainly attained salvation: those who live in a state of deep reverence in their prayers.</p><p class="vz-kaynak">al-Mu’minun, 23:1–2</p></figure>

<p>Surah al-Mu’minun begins its description of the believers who attain salvation with prayer, and ends with prayer again. In between it lists turning away from idle talk, zakat, chastity, and honouring trusts and covenants. (al-Mu’minun, 23:1–11) The qualities of the complete believer are, as it were, framed by prayer:</p>

<figure class="vz-sema"><figcaption><strong>Al-Mu’minun 1–11: A life that begins and ends with prayer</strong>The qualities of the complete believer are, as it were, framed by prayer.</figcaption><div class="vz-cerceve"><p class="vz-cerceve-bant"><span class="vz-cerceve-no">1</span><strong>They pray with humble devotion</strong><em>verse 2</em></p><ol class="vz-cerceve-orta"><li><span class="vz-cerceve-no">2</span><em>verse 3</em><strong>They turn away from idle talk</strong></li><li><span class="vz-cerceve-no">3</span><em>verse 4</em><strong>They pay zakat</strong></li><li><span class="vz-cerceve-no">4</span><em>verses 5–7</em><strong>They guard their chastity</strong></li><li><span class="vz-cerceve-no">5</span><em>verse 8</em><strong>They honour trusts and covenants</strong></li></ol><p class="vz-cerceve-bant"><span class="vz-cerceve-no">6</span><strong>They guard their prayers</strong><em>verse 9</em></p></div><p class="vz-sema-alt">Glad tidings: they will inherit the Paradise of Firdaws (verses 10–11)</p></figure>

<p><strong>Khushu‘</strong> is the bowing of the heart in reverence, knowing that it stands before Allah, and the stilling of the body in that same reverence. Prayer is the act of worship in which a servant is closest to his Lord; but it is not enough merely to perform it, the real matter is to attain khushu‘ within the prayer. A prayer performed while our mind is on shopping, our eyes on the clock and our heart on the world may discharge the obligation, yet it struggles to bring the heart to life.</p>

<p>The way to khushu‘ is clear: to pray on time and without haste, to learn the meaning of what we recite, to stay a moment longer in prostration, to silence the phone before prayer, and to begin with the opening takbir saying to ourselves, “Perhaps this is my last prayer.” Khushu‘ is not gained in an instant; it grows a little more with every prayer.</p>

<figure class="vz-nass vz-hadis"><figcaption class="vz-nass-bas"><span class="vz-nass-tur">Hadith</span><span class="vz-nass-konu">Allah looks at hearts and deeds</span></figcaption><p lang="ar" dir="rtl" class="vz-arapca">إِنَّ اللَّهَ لَا يَنْظُرُ إِلَى صُوَرِكُمْ وَأَمْوَالِكُمْ، وَلَكِنْ يَنْظُرُ إِلَى قُلُوبِكُمْ وَأَعْمَالِكُمْ</p><p class="vz-meal">Allah does not look at your outward appearance or your wealth; rather, He looks at your hearts and your deeds.</p><p class="vz-kaynak">Muslim, Birr, 34 (2564)</p></figure>

<p>Here in Europe, prayer is sometimes squeezed into a break at work, a school recess or a car park on a journey. Speaking politely with a manager so as to observe the prayer time at work, spreading the prayer mat in a clean corner where there is no prayer room, and treasuring every prayer we can perform in congregation: this is the effort of the complete believer. <strong>If prayer stands at the centre of our lives, all the other qualities find their place around it.</strong></p>

<figure class="vz-nass vz-ayet"><figcaption class="vz-nass-bas"><span class="vz-nass-tur">Qur’anic verse</span><span class="vz-nass-konu">The character that prayer brings</span></figcaption><p lang="ar" dir="rtl" class="vz-arapca">اتْلُ مَا أُوحِيَ إِلَيْكَ مِنَ الْكِتَابِ وَأَقِمِ الصَّلَاةَ إِنَّ الصَّلَاةَ تَنْهَىٰ عَنِ الْفَحْشَاءِ وَالْمُنكَرِ</p><p class="vz-meal">Recite what has been revealed to you from the Book, and perform the prayer with care. Without doubt, the prayer keeps one from shameless acts and from wrongdoing.</p><p class="vz-kaynak">al-‘Ankabut, 29:45; excerpt from the verse</p></figure>

<p>A prayer performed with khushu‘ does not end in prostration; it is carried into a person’s life. Just as we place our heads in prostration, so our hands will prostrate as well and will not reach for what is forbidden; our feet will prostrate as well and will not walk towards evil. It is not enough for our tongue to remember Allah; that tongue will not lie, will not backbite, will not set people against one another. <strong>If we pray yet wrong other people, it means we have not yet gained what prayer should have brought us.</strong></p>

<figure class="vz-nass vz-ayet"><figcaption class="vz-nass-bas"><span class="vz-nass-tur">Qur’anic verse</span><span class="vz-nass-konu">Be upright, as you have been commanded</span></figcaption><p lang="ar" dir="rtl" class="vz-arapca">فَاسْتَقِمْ كَمَا أُمِرْتَ وَمَن تَابَ مَعَكَ وَلَا تَطْغَوْا إِنَّهُ بِمَا تَعْمَلُونَ بَصِيرٌ</p><p class="vz-meal">Be upright, as you have been commanded, together with those who have turned back to the right path at your side! And do not stray beyond bounds. Allah sees very well what you do.</p><p class="vz-kaynak">Hud, 11:112</p></figure>

<p>The Companion Sufyan ibn ‘Abdullah (may Allah be pleased with him) said, “O Messenger of Allah! Tell me something about Islam such that I need not ask anyone else after you.” Our Prophet replied, “Say, ‘I believe in Allah’, and then be upright.” (Muslim, Iman, 62) <strong>Istiqama</strong> is the harmony of belief, word and conduct. There is no duplicity in the life of the complete believer: he is not one person in the mosque and another at work, one person in front of others and another when alone.</p>

<figure class="vz-sema"><figcaption><strong>Istiqama: Belief, word and conduct are in harmony</strong>There is no duplicity in the life of the complete believer.</figcaption><ol class="vz-uclu"><li><strong>Belief</strong>Affirmation in the heart</li><li><strong>Word</strong>True and good</li><li><strong>Conduct</strong>The same everywhere</li></ol><p class="vz-sema-alt">The same person in the mosque and in the marketplace.</p></figure>

<aside class="vz-hikaye" aria-label="“Then where is Allah?”"><p class="vz-kutu-tur">A lesson from a Companion</p><p class="vz-kutu-baslik">“Then where is Allah?”</p><p>As ‘Abdullah ibn ‘Umar (may Allah be pleased with him) passes a shepherd tending sheep, he asks, “Shepherd, is there a sheep (for slaughter)?” The shepherd says, “Its owner is not here.” To test him, Ibn ‘Umar says, “You could say a wolf ate it,” whereupon the shepherd raises his head to the sky: “Then where is Allah?” Ibn ‘Umar says, “By Allah, I am more deserving of saying ‘Where is Allah?’”; he buys both the shepherd and the sheep, sets the shepherd free and gives him the sheep as a gift.</p><p class="vz-not">al-Haythami, Majma‘ al-Zawa’id, IX, 350; al-Bayhaqi, Shu‘ab al-Iman. A report transmitted from a Companion, not a hadith.</p></aside>

<p>To be able to say “Allah sees” where no one else sees is the essence of istiqama. Allah sees us at home, at work, in the shops and markets, and in front of a screen, just as He sees us in the mosque.</p>

<figure class="vz-nass vz-ayet"><figcaption class="vz-nass-bas"><span class="vz-nass-tur">Qur’anic verse</span><span class="vz-nass-konu">The ear, the eye and the heart are accountable</span></figcaption><p lang="ar" dir="rtl" class="vz-arapca">وَلَا تَقْفُ مَا لَيْسَ لَكَ بِهِ عِلْمٌ إِنَّ السَّمْعَ وَالْبَصَرَ وَالْفُؤَادَ كُلُّ أُولَٰئِكَ كَانَ عَنْهُ مَسْئُولًا</p><p class="vz-meal">Do not go after what you have no knowledge of! <p>My dear brothers and sisters! Every part of the body that Allah has given us is a trust, and every trust will be accounted for. The complete believer knows that the tongue, a small piece of flesh, can drag a person to disaster in this world and in the hereafter. Our Prophet, taking hold of his own tongue, said to Mu‘adh ibn Jabal (may Allah be pleased with him), “Restrain this!”, and told him that what drags people face-down into the Fire is, more often than not, the harvest of their tongues. (al-Tirmidhi, Iman, 8)</p>

<p>The complete believer also guards his eyes. Our Lord commands believing men and believing women to keep their eyes away from what is forbidden. (al-Nur, 24:30–31) Today the eye is tested not only in the street but also on the screen in the palm of our hand. Every image, every piece of news and every video that reaches us with a single tap is written to the account of our eyes and our hearts. <strong>Being able to turn the screen face-down is also a matter of faith.</strong></p>

<p>Here in Europe this also has a dimension of language: the more languages we know, the greater our responsibility. Whether we speak Turkish, French, Dutch or German, what our Lord asks of us is the same: speech that is true, good and fitting.</p>

<figure class="vz-nass vz-ayet"><figcaption class="vz-nass-bas"><span class="vz-nass-tur">Qur’anic verse</span><span class="vz-nass-konu">The flesh of one’s dead brother</span></figcaption><p lang="ar" dir="rtl" class="vz-arapca">يَا أَيُّهَا الَّذِينَ آمَنُوا اجْتَنِبُوا كَثِيرًا مِّنَ الظَّنِّ إِنَّ بَعْضَ الظَّنِّ إِثْمٌ وَلَا تَجَسَّسُوا وَلَا يَغْتَب بَّعْضُكُم بَعْضًا أَيُحِبُّ أَحَدُكُمْ أَن يَأْكُلَ لَحْمَ أَخِيهِ مَيْتًا فَكَرِهْتُمُوهُ وَاتَّقُوا اللَّهَ إِنَّ اللَّهَ تَوَّابٌ رَّحِيمٌ</p><p class="vz-meal">You who believe! Avoid most suspicion, for some suspicion is sin. Do not pry into one another’s private affairs and do not backbite one another. Would any one of you like to eat the flesh of his dead brother? <p>The verse lines up three evils like a chain: a chain that begins with suspicion, grows by searching out faults, and ends with speaking behind a brother’s back. Our Lord likens backbiting to eating the flesh of one’s dead brother, because the person who is backbitten is not there and cannot defend himself.</p>

<figure class="vz-nass vz-hadis"><figcaption class="vz-nass-bas"><span class="vz-nass-tur">Hadith</span><span class="vz-nass-konu">What is backbiting?</span></figcaption><p lang="ar" dir="rtl" class="vz-arapca">أَتَدْرُونَ مَا الْغِيبَةُ؟ قَالُوا: اللَّهُ وَرَسُولُهُ أَعْلَمُ. قَالَ: ذِكْرُكَ أَخَاكَ بِمَا يَكْرَهُ</p><p class="vz-meal">“Do you know what backbiting is?” The Companions said, “Allah and His Messenger know best.” Our Prophet said, “It is mentioning your brother in a way he would dislike.” When it was asked, “What if what I say is true of my brother?”, he replied, “If it is true of him, you have backbitten him; if it is not, you have slandered him.”</p><p class="vz-kaynak">Muslim, Birr, 70 (2589); excerpt from the narration</p></figure>

<p>Our Prophet told us that the truly bankrupt person is the one who comes on the Day of Resurrection with prayer, fasting and zakat, but who has insulted one person, slandered another and consumed another’s property; his good deeds are given away to those he wronged, and when his good deeds run out, he is made to carry their sins. (Muslim, Birr, 59) <strong>Backbiting transfers the capital for the hereafter that we have built up with so much effort into someone else’s account.</strong></p>

<aside class="vz-hikaye" aria-label="A remark in a group chat"><p class="vz-kutu-tur">An illustrative scene</p><p class="vz-kutu-baslik">A remark in a group chat</p><p>In a group chat, a voice recording about someone’s private life starts to circulate; everyone adds a sentence. One of our elderly brothers writes a short note: “Our brother is not here; let us not go after what we do not know. Let us delete the recording and pray for him.” The matter is closed. <strong>One sentence is enough to break a chain of backbiting.</strong></p><p class="vz-not">This is an illustrative example; it is not presented as an event that actually took place.</p></aside>

<p class="vz-hitap">Dear brothers and sisters! Our Prophet has left us a scale by which to measure the maturity of faith.</p>

<figure class="vz-nass vz-hadis"><figcaption class="vz-nass-bas"><span class="vz-nass-tur">Hadith</span><span class="vz-nass-konu">Those most mature in faith</span></figcaption><p lang="ar" dir="rtl" class="vz-arapca">أَكْمَلُ الْمُؤْمِنِينَ إِيمَانًا أَحْسَنُهُمْ خُلُقًا، وَخِيَارُكُمْ خِيَارُكُمْ لِنِسَائِهِمْ خُلُقًا</p><p class="vz-meal">The most mature of the believers in faith is the one whose character is finest. And the best of you are those who are finest in character towards their wives.</p><p class="vz-kaynak">al-Tirmidhi, Rada‘, 11 (1162)</p></figure>

<p>In this hadith faith and character meet in a single sentence: the perfection of faith is measured by the beauty of character. The second sentence of the hadith shows where this scale is first set up: the home. The character of a person who is cheerful, polite and helpful outside, but harsh with his wife and children at home, has not yet reached perfection. <strong>The first test of character comes the moment we step in through our own front door.</strong></p>

<p>Our Prophet also summed up the purpose for which he was sent: “I was sent only to complete good character.” (Ibn Hanbal, II, 381) And our Lord addresses him, saying:</p>

<figure class="vz-nass vz-ayet"><figcaption class="vz-nass-bas"><span class="vz-nass-tur">Qur’anic verse</span><span class="vz-nass-konu">A sublime character</span></figcaption><p lang="ar" dir="rtl" class="vz-arapca">وَإِنَّكَ لَعَلَىٰ خُلُقٍ عَظِيمٍ</p><p class="vz-meal">You are surely of a sublime character.</p><p class="vz-kaynak">al-Qalam, 68:4</p></figure>

<p>When our mother Aisha was asked about our Prophet’s character, she answered, “His character was the Qur’an.” (Muslim, Musafirin, 139) A life that embodies every goodness the Qur’an commands and keeps away from every ugliness it forbids… The complete believer takes that life as his example. Our Lord shows us this example in these words: “For those among you who place their hope in Allah’s favour and in the Last Day, and who remember Allah often, there is, without doubt, a beautiful example in the Messenger of Allah.” (al-Ahzab, 33:21)</p>

<p>Good character means giving the greeting of peace with a smiling face, swallowing a hurtful word, showing respect to the elderly, tenderness to the young, and thanking those who serve us. These seem small; yet on the Day of Resurrection, one of the heaviest deeds on the Scale will be good character.</p>

<figure class="vz-nass vz-hadis"><figcaption class="vz-nass-bas"><span class="vz-nass-tur">Hadith</span><span class="vz-nass-konu">Who is a Muslim, and who is a believer?</span></figcaption><p lang="ar" dir="rtl" class="vz-arapca">الْمُسْلِمُ مَنْ سَلِمَ النَّاسُ مِنْ لِسَانِهِ وَيَدِهِ، وَالْمُؤْمِنُ مَنْ أَمِنَهُ النَّاسُ عَلَى دِمَائِهِمْ وَأَمْوَالِهِمْ</p><p class="vz-meal">A Muslim is one from whose tongue and hand people are safe. A believer is one whom people trust with regard to their lives and their property.</p><p class="vz-kaynak">al-Nasa’i, Iman, 8 (4998); with similar wording al-Tirmidhi, Iman, 12 (2627)</p></figure>

<p>My dear brothers and sisters! <strong>The word “believer” (mu’min) comes from the root of “trust”.</strong> One of Allah’s most beautiful names is al-Mu’min, which means the One who gives His servants assurance. So a believer is a person who trusts and who inspires trust. The complete believer is not feared for his hand or his tongue. He is free of deceit, his trade is honest, and he does not betray a trust. As he inspires trust in the prayer rows of the mosque, so he inspires trust outside the mosque.</p>

<p>Our Prophet also taught this trust through neighbourliness. Three times he said, “By Allah, he does not believe!” When asked, “Who, O Messenger of Allah?”, he said, “One whose neighbour is not safe from his harm.” (al-Bukhari, Adab, 29)</p>

<p>Here in Europe every Muslim, whether he wishes it or not, is a representative of Islam. One person’s lie may be charged to the whole community, a shopkeeper’s cheating to all Muslims. In the same way, a worker’s honesty, a student’s good manners and a neighbour’s smiling face say more than thousands of words of explanation. <strong>A neighbour who feels safe with us is a window opened onto Islam.</strong></p>

<figure class="vz-nass vz-ayet"><figcaption class="vz-nass-bas"><span class="vz-nass-tur">Qur’anic verse</span><span class="vz-nass-konu">Those who overcome their anger and pardon people</span></figcaption><p lang="ar" dir="rtl" class="vz-arapca">الَّذِينَ يُنفِقُونَ فِي السَّرَّاءِ وَالضَّرَّاءِ وَالْكَاظِمِينَ الْغَيْظَ وَالْعَافِينَ عَنِ النَّاسِ وَاللَّهُ يُحِبُّ الْمُحْسِنِينَ</p><p class="vz-meal">They spend in Allah’s way in prosperity and in hardship, they overcome their anger, and they pardon people. Allah loves those who do good with excellence.</p><p class="vz-kaynak">Al ‘Imran, 3:134</p></figure>

<p>Anger is a natural emotion that Allah has placed in human beings; the fault lies not in its existence but in its seizing the reins. Anger lasts a moment, but its consequence sometimes turns into a regret that can never be made good. We know how many homes have been destroyed, and how many friendships ended, by a moment of anger. Our Prophet said, “The strong person is not the one who defeats his opponent in wrestling; the truly strong is the one who controls himself at the moment of anger.” (al-Bukhari, Adab, 76; Muslim, Birr, 107) When anger rises, he advised seeking refuge in Allah, keeping silent, and sitting down if we are standing (al-Bukhari, Adab, 76; Ibn Hanbal, I, 239; Abu Dawud, Adab, 3); a narration has also been reported that advises extinguishing anger with ablution.</p>

<p>The believer who overcomes his anger takes one step further and forgives. Our Lord teaches that answering evil with an equal response is justice, while pardoning and putting things right is a virtue whose reward He Himself will give. (al-Shura, 42:40)</p>

<figure class="vz-sema"><figcaption><strong>Three steps from anger to goodness</strong>Al ‘Imran, 3:134</figcaption><ol class="vz-basamak"><li><span class="vz-basamak-no">1</span><strong>Overcoming anger</strong><em>kazm al-ghayz</em>Holding back tongue and hand in a moment of anger</li><li><span class="vz-basamak-no">2</span><strong>Forgiving</strong><em>‘afw</em>Letting go of resentment and putting things right</li><li><span class="vz-basamak-no">3</span><strong>Doing good</strong><em>ihsan</em>Being among those who do good with excellence</li></ol><p class="vz-sema-alt">Allah loves those who do good with excellence.</p></figure>

<figure class="vz-nass vz-hadis"><figcaption class="vz-nass-bas"><span class="vz-nass-tur">Hadith</span><span class="vz-nass-konu">Forgiveness increases honour</span></figcaption><p lang="ar" dir="rtl" class="vz-arapca">مَا نَقَصَتْ صَدَقَةٌ مِنْ مَالٍ، وَمَا زَادَ اللَّهُ عَبْدًا بِعَفْوٍ إِلَّا عِزًّا، وَمَا تَوَاضَعَ أَحَدٌ لِلَّهِ إِلَّا رَفَعَهُ اللَّهُ</p><p class="vz-meal">Charity never diminishes wealth. Allah adds nothing to a servant who forgives except honour. And whoever humbles himself for Allah’s sake, Allah raises him.</p><p class="vz-kaynak">Muslim, Birr, 69 (2588)</p></figure>

<p>Abu Bakr (may Allah be pleased with him) had sworn that he would no longer support his relative Mistah, who had joined in the slander against his daughter Aisha. Thereupon this verse was revealed: “Let them pardon and overlook. Do you not wish that Allah should forgive you?” (al-Nur, 24:22) Abu Bakr, without any hesitation, said, “Yes, by Allah, I do wish that Allah would forgive me,” and resumed his support for Mistah. (al-Bukhari, Tafsir, al-Nur, 6) Our Prophet too, at the conquest of Mecca, forgave those who had tormented him for years, and so gave the highest example of this character.</p>

<p>The complete believer does not stay estranged from his brother for more than three days, because our Prophet taught that this is not permissible, and that the better of two people who have fallen out is the one who is first to give the greeting of peace. (al-Bukhari, Adab, 62; Muslim, Birr, 25) <strong>Whoever wishes to be forgiven forgives; the complete believer does not hesitate to be the first to give the greeting of peace.</strong></p>

<figure class="vz-nass vz-hadis"><figcaption class="vz-nass-bas"><span class="vz-nass-tur">Hadith</span><span class="vz-nass-konu">Prayer is light, charity is proof, patience is radiant light (diya’)</span></figcaption><p lang="ar" dir="rtl" class="vz-arapca">وَالصَّلَاةُ نُورٌ، وَالصَّدَقَةُ بُرْهَانٌ، وَالصَّبْرُ ضِيَاءٌ</p><p class="vz-meal">Prayer is a light, charity is proof (of faith), and patience is a radiant light.</p><p class="vz-kaynak">Muslim, Tahara, 1 (223); excerpt from the narration</p></figure>

<p>The word <strong>diya’</strong>, used in the hadith for patience, describes a light that both illuminates and warms, like the light of the sun. Patience is like a lamp burning in a dark night: it does not remove hardship at once, but it lets us see our way. When a calamity strikes, the complete believer does not rebel, and does not complain to everyone he meets. He knows that every hardship he lives through is a test in this world, and he holds fast to the glad tidings our Lord gives, “Truly, Allah is with those who show patience” (al-Baqara, 2:153).</p>

<p>Patience is not merely enduring misfortune. Resisting what is forbidden, persevering in worship and standing firm in truthfulness are also patience. Surah al-‘Asr tells us that those saved from loss are those who urge one another to the truth and urge one another to patience. (al-‘Asr, 103:1–3) One of the finest kindnesses we can show a fellow Muslim is to remind him of patience on his difficult day.</p>

<figure class="vz-nass vz-hadis"><figcaption class="vz-nass-bas"><span class="vz-nass-tur">Hadith</span><span class="vz-nass-konu">How wonderful is the believer’s state</span></figcaption><p lang="ar" dir="rtl" class="vz-arapca">عَجَبًا لِأَمْرِ الْمُؤْمِنِ، إِنَّ أَمْرَهُ كُلَّهُ خَيْرٌ، وَلَيْسَ ذَاكَ لِأَحَدٍ إِلَّا لِلْمُؤْمِنِ، إِنْ أَصَابَتْهُ سَرَّاءُ شَكَرَ فَكَانَ خَيْرًا لَهُ، وَإِنْ أَصَابَتْهُ ضَرَّاءُ صَبَرَ فَكَانَ خَيْرًا لَهُ</p><p class="vz-meal">How wonderful is the state of the believer! All his affairs are good for him, and this is true of no one except the believer. If something pleasing happens to him, he gives thanks, and that is good for him. If something distressing befalls him, he is patient, and that is good for him too.</p><p class="vz-kaynak">Muslim, Zuhd, 64 (2999)</p></figure>

<p>Life here in Europe often tests both our patience and our gratitude: an illness, the loss of a job, a worry over a child, the distance from parents back home… Yet this same life gives us blessings too: lawful livelihood, our children’s education, the congregation of our mosque. <strong>The complete believer arms himself with patience in hardship and gratitude in plenty; like a bird flying on its two wings, he does not lose his way.</strong></p>

<figure class="vz-nass vz-ayet"><figcaption class="vz-nass-bas"><span class="vz-nass-tur">Qur’anic verse</span><span class="vz-nass-konu">A trade that will never end in loss</span></figcaption><p lang="ar" dir="rtl" class="vz-arapca">إِنَّ الَّذِينَ يَتْلُونَ كِتَابَ اللَّهِ وَأَقَامُوا الصَّلَاةَ وَأَنفَقُوا مِمَّا رَزَقْنَاهُمْ سِرًّا وَعَلَانِيَةً يَرْجُونَ تِجَارَةً لَّن تَبُورَ</p><p class="vz-meal">Those who recite Allah’s Book, who perform the prayer with care, and who spend on others, in secret and in the open, from the provision We have given them, may hope for a trade that will never end in loss.</p><p class="vz-kaynak">Fatir, 35:29</p></figure>

<p><strong>Infaq</strong> is to share some of the blessings Allah has given with those in need, for the sake of His good pleasure. The Qur’an mentions infaq right beside faith, because a person who can share his wealth shows that his heart is not tied to this world. Secret infaq protects against showing off, while open infaq sets an example for others. The aid parcels of our mosque associations, student scholarships, funeral funds, and the hands stretched out to earthquake victims are the branches of this trade here in Europe.</p>

<figure class="vz-sema"><figcaption><strong>Secret and open infaq</strong>Fatir, 35:29: Those who spend from their provision, in secret and in the open.</figcaption><div class="vz-denge"><div class="vz-kefe"><p class="vz-kefe-ad">Secret infaq</p><ul><li>Protects against showing off</li><li>Shows that the heart is not tied to this world</li></ul></div><div class="vz-terazi"><svg viewBox="0 0 24 24" fill="none" stroke="currentColor" stroke-width="1.8" stroke-linecap="round" stroke-linejoin="round" aria-hidden="true" focusable="false"><rect x="5.2" y="5.2" width="13.6" height="13.6"/><rect x="5.2" y="5.2" width="13.6" height="13.6" transform="rotate(45 12 12)"/><circle cx="12" cy="12" r="2.1" fill="currentColor" stroke="none"/></svg></div><div class="vz-kefe"><p class="vz-kefe-ad">Open infaq</p><ul><li>Sets an example for others</li><li>Aid parcels, student scholarships, funeral funds</li></ul></div></div><p class="vz-sema-alt">A trade that will never end in loss.</p></figure>

<figure class="vz-nass vz-hadis"><figcaption class="vz-nass-bas"><span class="vz-nass-tur">Hadith</span><span class="vz-nass-konu">Trust and covenant</span></figcaption><p lang="ar" dir="rtl" class="vz-arapca">لَا إِيمَانَ لِمَنْ لَا أَمَانَةَ لَهُ، وَلَا دِينَ لِمَنْ لَا عَهْدَ لَهُ</p><p class="vz-meal">There is no (complete) faith for one who does not honour a trust, and no (complete) religion for one who does not keep a covenant.</p><p class="vz-kaynak">Ibn Hanbal, III, 134</p></figure>

<p>Anas ibn Malik (may Allah be pleased with him) reports that our Prophet said these words in almost every address he gave us. <strong>Keeping one’s word</strong> means remaining faithfully bound to a promise given and an agreement made. Our Lord says, “You who believe! Fulfil your contracts” (al-Ma’ida, 5:1) and “Keep faith with your covenant, for a covenant entails responsibility” (al-Isra’, 17:34). Our Prophet counted lying when one speaks, breaking one’s word when one promises, and betraying a trust among the signs of a hypocrite. (al-Bukhari, Iman, 24; Muslim, Iman, 107)</p>

<p>Here in Europe, keeping one’s word covers a very wide field: honouring the employment contract and working hours, being faithful with the rent and with debts, arriving on time for appointments, paying taxes and insurance honestly, and abiding by the lawful order of the country in which we live. Every door we pass through with a residence permit, a work permit or citizenship is a contract. <strong>The complete believer stands behind his signature and his word.</strong></p>

<p><strong>Faith has its roots in the heart and its fruit in life; the complete believer is the same person in the mosque and in the marketplace.</strong> As we leave this Friday prayer, let us ask ourselves one question: which branch of my tree is waiting most urgently to bear fruit? My prayer, my tongue, my home, my word? This week, let us resolve to water that branch and turn to our Lord.</p>
