<p class="vz-hitap">Dear congregation! Our brothers and sisters, who know the person Allah has honoured by the heart and not by the body!</p>

<p>This supplication, which we so often recite as we ascend the pulpit, is the prayer of a prophet who had a knot in his tongue; the commentaries too explain that this knot was a hesitancy in Moses’ speech. Moses had been given the task of appearing before Pharaoh. A prophet for whom speaking was not easy opened his heart to his Lord without hiding his own limitation: “Appoint for me a helper from among my family.” (Ta-Ha 20:29) He named his brother Aaron: “My brother Aaron speaks more clearly and fluently than I do. Send him with me as a helper who will affirm me.” (al-Qasas 28:34)</p>

<p>Allah did not take the task back from Moses; He gave Aaron to stand at his side. The knot in his tongue was no barrier to his prophethood; it only showed his need for a companion on the way. These two sentences are the summary of the whole sermon: a limitation does not remove a duty; and being a helper is also our duty.</p>

<p>This week we shall also remember the dawn adhan. In Medina the dawn adhan was called by a Companion who could not see, ‘Abd Allah ibn Umm Maktum; according to what the narrators of hadith report, he would not begin the adhan until it was said to him, “Morning has come, morning has come!” He could not see the dawn; yet it was he who announced the morning to the whole city. We shall speak of the details in due course.</p>

<p>Next week, on 3 December, it is the International Day of Persons with Disabilities. But our subject is not the subject of one day, it is the subject of every day; our brothers and sisters with disabilities are not found on one day of the calendar, but within our rows, our tables and our streets.</p>

<p><strong>A scene from our life in Europe:</strong> On Friday, at the door of the mosque, an uncle leaning on his walking stick climbs the three steps, first holding the railing, then the arm of the young man beside him. Behind him, a mother carrying her small child in her arms crosses the same steps with twice the effort. In a corner, a young person who cannot hear the sermon watches the imam’s lips. The question on all three faces is the same: “Is this mosque ours too?” Our answer will be “yes”; but we must give this answer not only with words, but with our door.</p>

<p>Our main sentence: <strong>Allah looks not at our appearances but at our hearts; the limitation may be in the body, but the real barrier is a hard heart and a closed door: let us be eyes for those who cannot see, ears for those who cannot hear, a shoulder for those who cannot walk; our brother or sister with a disability is not our burden but a trust and a companion on the way.</strong></p>

<p class="vz-hitap">Our Lord did not count the knot in Moses’ tongue as a barrier to his mission; He gave him a helper at his side.</p>

<p>How we look at our brother or sister with a disability depends on how we look at the human being; so let us begin with the worth of the human being. Our Lord addresses all the children of Adam like this:</p>

<figure class="vz-nass vz-ayet"><figcaption class="vz-nass-bas"><span class="vz-nass-tur">Qur’anic verse</span><span class="vz-nass-konu">Honour for the human being; the most honoured</span></figcaption><p lang="ar" dir="rtl" class="vz-arapca">وَلَقَدْ كَرَّمْنَا بَنِي آدَمَ وَحَمَلْنَاهُمْ فِي الْبَرِّ وَالْبَحْرِ وَرَزَقْنَاهُم مِّنَ الطَّيِّبَاتِ وَفَضَّلْنَاهُمْ عَلَىٰ كَثِيرٍ مِّمَّنْ خَلَقْنَا تَفْضِيلًا</p><p lang="ar" dir="rtl" class="vz-arapca">إِنَّ أَكْرَمَكُمْ عِندَ اللَّهِ أَتْقَاكُمْ</p><p class="vz-meal"><em>We have truly given the children of Adam glory, honour and blessings; We carried them over land and sea, gave them good things as provision, and raised them above most of those We created.</em><br><em>In Allah’s sight, the most valued among you is the one who most guards against disobeying Him.</em></p><p class="vz-kaynak">al-Isra’ 17:70; al-Hujurat 49:13; excerpt from the verse</p></figure>

<p>The “glory, honour and blessings” of the verse, that is, the dignity a human being carries from birth, is expressed by the word karama. This dignity is given in equal measure to the strong and the weak, to the one born without impairment and to the one who comes to live with a disability later in life. The surah of at-Tin says: “We have indeed created the human being in the finest form.” (at-Tin 95:4) “The finest form”, that is ahsan taqwim, does not mean the flawlessness of the body; it is the whole of the human being’s finest creation, as a being with intellect, will and heart. The absence of an arm, an eye or an ear does not erase this honour. So from where does our Lord look at the human being? The Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him) said:</p>

<figure class="vz-nass vz-hadis"><figcaption class="vz-nass-bas"><span class="vz-nass-tur">Hadith</span><span class="vz-nass-konu">Not at appearances but at hearts</span></figcaption><p lang="ar" dir="rtl" class="vz-arapca">إِنَّ اللَّهَ لاَ يَنْظُرُ إِلَى صُوَرِكُمْ وَأَمْوَالِكُمْ وَلَكِنْ يَنْظُرُ إِلَى قُلُوبِكُمْ وَأَعْمَالِكُمْ</p><p class="vz-meal">Allah does not look at your appearances and your wealth, but looks at your hearts and your deeds.</p><p class="vz-kaynak">Muslim, Birr 34; Ibn Majah, Zuhd 9</p></figure>

<figure class="vz-sema"><figcaption><strong>Allah’s measure</strong>Muslim, Birr 34: Allah looks not at your appearances and your wealth but at your hearts and your deeds.</figcaption><div class="vz-denge"><div class="vz-kefe"><p class="vz-kefe-ad">Light pan</p><ul><li><strong>Appearance</strong></li><li><strong>Wealth</strong></li></ul></div><div class="vz-terazi"><svg viewBox="0 0 24 24" fill="none" stroke="currentColor" stroke-width="1.8" stroke-linecap="round" stroke-linejoin="round" aria-hidden="true" focusable="false"><path d="M12 3v17M8 21h8M5 7h14"/><path d="M5 7l-3 6a3 3 0 0 0 6 0z"/><path d="M19 7l-3 6a3 3 0 0 0 6 0z"/></svg></div><div class="vz-kefe"><p class="vz-kefe-ad">Heavy pan</p><ul><li><strong>Heart</strong></li><li><strong>Deeds</strong></li></ul></div></div><p class="vz-sema-alt">On the light pan of the scales stand appearance and wealth; on the heavy pan, heart and deeds.</p></figure>

<p>Appearance, that is, our face and our body, is a gift none of us chose. Our heart and our deeds, however, are in our hands. We often look hardest exactly where Allah does not look: at the body, at looks, at strength. The Qur’an too does not seek the real defect in the body: “The truth is that it is not eyes that go blind, but the hearts within the breasts that go blind.” (al-Hajj 22:46) Şeyh Gâlib speaks of the human being in these lines:</p>

<p class="vz-hitap"><em>Hoşça bak zâtına kim zübde-i âlemsin sen</em></p>

<p class="vz-hitap"><em>Merdüm-i dîde-i ekvân olan âdemsin sen</em></p>

<p class="vz-not">— Şeyh Gâlib, Tercî-i bend</p>

<p>“Look kindly upon yourself, for you are the essence of the world; you are the human being, the pupil of the eye of the universe.” We owe this way of looking both to ourselves and to our brother or sister with a disability.</p>

<p><strong>A scene from our life in Europe:</strong> When we open our phones, only bright faces and quick successes appear before us. In a job application and in a wedding hall alike, our eyes first ask, “What is this person good for?” Yet every body grows old one day, and measured by this yardstick it falls behind; we are all walking towards that day. What do we mostly look at in people: their face, or their heart?</p>

<p class="vz-hitap">Allah looks at our hearts, not at our appearance; let us too look at one another where He looks.</p>

<header class="vz-bolum" id="imtihan" tabindex="-1"><h2 class="vaaz-bolum-baslik"><span class="vz-no">II.</span> The world is the place of testing: everyone in proportion to their strength</h2><p class="vz-bolum-oz">Disability is not a punishment but a test; in that test every one of us has a share.</p></header>

<p>Why, then, is not everyone in this world in the same circumstances? Our Lord has set up life as a test:</p>

<figure class="vz-nass vz-ayet"><figcaption class="vz-nass-bas"><span class="vz-nass-tur">Qur’anic verse</span><span class="vz-nass-konu">Life is a test</span></figcaption><p lang="ar" dir="rtl" class="vz-arapca">الَّذِي خَلَقَ الْمَوْتَ وَالْحَيَاةَ لِيَبْلُوَكُمْ أَيُّكُمْ أَحْسَنُ عَمَلًا وَهُوَ الْعَزِيزُ الْغَفُورُ</p><p lang="ar" dir="rtl" class="vz-arapca">لَا يُكَلِّفُ اللَّهُ نَفْسًا إِلَّا وُسْعَهَا</p><p class="vz-meal"><em>It is He who created death and life, to try which of you is better in conduct. He is mighty and ever ready to forgive.</em><br><em>Allah does not charge anyone with what is beyond their capacity;</em></p><p class="vz-kaynak">al-Mulk 67:2; al-Baqara 2:286; excerpt from the verse</p></figure>

<p>The test comes upon each person differently: “It is He who made you successors on the earth and raised some of you above others in degrees, to test you in what He has given you.” (al-An‘am 6:165) Some are tested with wealth, some with want, some with health, some with illness. But the measure of the test is just: everyone is responsible as far as their capacity (wus‘) reaches, that is, their strength and means. Our Lord also wills ease: “Allah wants ease for you and does not want you to suffer hardship.” (al-Baqara 2:185) When misfortune comes, the believer’s words are clear: “We surely belong to Allah, and to Him we shall surely return.” (al-Baqara 2:156)</p>

<aside class="vz-olcu" aria-label="Disability is not a punishment"><span class="vz-olcu-ikon"><svg viewBox="0 0 24 24" fill="none" stroke="currentColor" stroke-width="1.8" stroke-linecap="round" stroke-linejoin="round" aria-hidden="true" focusable="false"><path d="M12 3v17M8 21h8M5 7h14"/><path d="M5 7l-3 6a3 3 0 0 0 6 0z"/><path d="M19 7l-3 6a3 3 0 0 0 6 0z"/></svg></span><p class="vz-kutu-tur">GUIDANCE FROM FIQH</p><p class="vz-kutu-baslik">Disability is not a punishment</p><p>Disability is not the repayment of anyone’s sin, neither that of the person with a disability nor that of their parents. The Qur’an states this plainly: “No guilty soul will bear the guilt of another.” (al-An‘am 6:164) To parents who ask, “What sin did we commit, that our child should be like this?”, the answer is tender and clear: no, this is not a punishment. Putting off the doctor, the treatment or the device because of the evil eye or sorcery is wrong; for our Prophet said: “Seek treatment. For Allah has created a cure for every illness He has created.” (Abu Dawud, Tibb 1; al-Tirmidhi, Tibb 2) The doctor, therapy, the device and prayer go together. Disability does not lower anyone’s worth in Allah’s sight.</p></aside>

<figure class="vz-sema"><figcaption><strong>The two faces of the test</strong>One test, two sides.</figcaption><div class="vz-denge"><div class="vz-kefe"><p class="vz-kefe-ad">Our brother or sister</p><ul><li><strong>Patience</strong></li></ul></div><div class="vz-terazi"><svg viewBox="0 0 24 24" fill="none" stroke="currentColor" stroke-width="1.8" stroke-linecap="round" stroke-linejoin="round" aria-hidden="true" focusable="false"><rect x="5.2" y="5.2" width="13.6" height="13.6"/><rect x="5.2" y="5.2" width="13.6" height="13.6" transform="rotate(45 12 12)"/><circle cx="12" cy="12" r="2.1" fill="currentColor" stroke="none"/></svg></div><div class="vz-kefe"><p class="vz-kefe-ad">The community</p><ul><li><strong>Mercy</strong></li><li><strong>Justice</strong></li><li><strong>Service</strong></li></ul></div></div><p class="vz-sema-alt">The test does not belong to one side only: our brother or sister is tested with patience, we with mercy, justice and service.</p></figure>

<p>Our Prophet counted health too as a test: most people are cheated in two blessings whose value they do not know: health and free time. (al-Bukhari, Riqaq 1) Those who are healthy will also be asked to account for the blessing. The test is not only that of our brother or sister with a disability, but also of those around them: they are tested with patience, we with mercy, justice and service.</p>

<p><strong>A scene from our life in Europe:</strong> In the maternity-ward corridor of a hospital in Liège, a father who has just learned his baby’s diagnosis leans against the wall; the mother in her bed asks, “Why us?” There is a young man who had an accident at work on a building site and never regained his former strength. An uncle who lost a foot because of diabetes now drinks his evening tea sitting down. They all ask the same question. The answer is this: every person is one accident, one illness or one old age away from living with a disability; no one is in anyone else’s place, but anyone may find themselves in another’s.</p>

<p class="vz-hitap">Disability is not a punishment but a test; in that test every one of us has a share.</p>

<header class="vz-bolum" id="peygamberlerin-sabri" tabindex="-1"><figure class="vz-bolum-sahne"><img class="vz-resim" src="/media/vaazlar/engelli-kardeslerimize-karsi-sorumluluklarimiz/pinar-tas-1600.webp" srcset="/media/vaazlar/engelli-kardeslerimize-karsi-sorumluluklarimiz/pinar-tas-480.webp 480w, /media/vaazlar/engelli-kardeslerimize-karsi-sorumluluklarimiz/pinar-tas-960.webp 960w, /media/vaazlar/engelli-kardeslerimize-karsi-sorumluluklarimiz/pinar-tas-1600.webp 1600w" sizes="(max-width: 640px) calc(100vw - 40px), (max-width: 1024px) calc(100vw - 64px), 736px" width="1600" height="893" alt="A clear spring rushing out between mossy rocks and pouring into a cool pool; green leaves all around and pebbles in the water." loading="lazy" decoding="async"><figcaption>Illustrative scene: A spring and a stone</figcaption></figure><h2 class="vaaz-bolum-baslik"><span class="vz-no">III.</span> The patience of the prophets: Jacob’s eyes, Job’s supplication</h2><p class="vz-bolum-oz">Opening one’s heart to Allah does not break patience; patience is bringing one’s sorrow to Allah.</p></header>

<p>The Qur’an shows us the finest examples of patience in the lives of the prophets. Joseph’s father Jacob (Ya‘qub) wept so much in longing for his son that, in the Qur’an’s words, “in the end his eyes turned white with grief” (Yusuf 12:84); that is, he lost his sight. But did he fall silent, or did he rebel? “All that is left for me is beautiful patience.” (Yusuf 12:83) This is called beautiful patience (sabr jamil): not silence without complaint, but endurance that opens one’s sorrow to Allah alone.</p>

<figure class="vz-bolum-sahne vz-ara-sahne"><img class="vz-resim" src="/media/vaazlar/engelli-kardeslerimize-karsi-sorumluluklarimiz/gomlek-ve-pencere-1600.webp" srcset="/media/vaazlar/engelli-kardeslerimize-karsi-sorumluluklarimiz/gomlek-ve-pencere-480.webp 480w, /media/vaazlar/engelli-kardeslerimize-karsi-sorumluluklarimiz/gomlek-ve-pencere-960.webp 960w, /media/vaazlar/engelli-kardeslerimize-karsi-sorumluluklarimiz/gomlek-ve-pencere-1600.webp 1600w" sizes="(max-width: 640px) calc(100vw - 40px), (max-width: 1024px) calc(100vw - 64px), 736px" width="1600" height="893" alt="A plain white shirt, folded, lies on an old wooden chest; soft light filters in from the window." loading="lazy" decoding="async"><figcaption>Illustrative scene: A shirt and a window</figcaption></figure>

<figure class="vz-nass vz-ayet"><figcaption class="vz-nass-bas"><span class="vz-nass-tur">Qur’anic verse</span><span class="vz-nass-konu">I open my sorrow to Allah alone</span></figcaption><p lang="ar" dir="rtl" class="vz-arapca">قَالَ إِنَّمَا أَشْكُو بَثِّي وَحُزْنِي إِلَى اللَّهِ</p><p lang="ar" dir="rtl" class="vz-arapca">وَأَيُّوبَ إِذْ نَادَىٰ رَبَّهُ أَنِّي مَسَّنِيَ الضُّرُّ وَأَنتَ أَرْحَمُ الرَّاحِمِينَ</p><p class="vz-meal"><em>And Jacob said: “It is only to Allah that I present my pain and my grief, and by knowledge from Allah I know what you do not know.”</em><br><em>And remember Job, when he called upon his Lord: “Hardship has befallen me, but You are the most merciful of those who show mercy.”</em></p><p class="vz-kaynak">Yusuf 12:86; excerpt from the verse; al-Anbiya’ 21:83</p></figure>

<p>Jacob’s sorrow came from Allah; but the door always stayed open. In the end, when his son’s shirt was laid on his face: “When the bearer of good news arrived, no sooner had he laid the shirt on his face than Jacob could see again.” (Yusuf 12:96) Now let us look at Job (Ayyub). The Qur’an tells of his state not with harsh judgements but with a supplication: “Hardship has befallen me, but You are the most merciful of those who show mercy.” Our Lord answered this: “We accepted his prayer and took away whatever hardship and distress he had.” (al-Anbiya’ 21:84) “Strike the ground with your foot (We told him): here is cool water to wash in and to drink!” (Sad 38:42) The Qur’an gives this witness for Job: “We truly found him steadfast in hardships. What an excellent servant he was! He always turned towards Allah.” (Sad 38:44) As we see, Job was patient, prayed and sought healing; the three walked together.</p>

<p>In the opening we recalled the knot in Moses’ tongue and Aaron, who was given to stand at his side. Three prophets, three lessons: Jacob opened his sorrow to Allah, Job prayed with patience, Moses asked for a helper. None of them is praised for having been silent; all are praised for having turned to their Lord. Complaining to Allah is not contrary to patience; weeping is not rebellion; seeking healing is not contrary to trust in Allah.</p>

<p><strong>A scene from our life in Europe:</strong> Our grandmother, whose eyes are growing weak, reads the Qur’an from a large-print mushaf; in the evenings she listens to a recording her grandchild plays for her. At another table, the phone of a friend who has fallen ill for a long time falls ever more silent, because everyone says, “Let us not disturb them.” Job’s patience is his own work; loyalty is ours. When did we last call a friend who has been ill for a long time?</p>

<figure class="vz-sema"><figcaption><strong>The patience of the prophets</strong>Three prophets, three lessons.</figcaption><ol class="vz-kartlar"><li><span class="vz-kart-ikon"><svg viewBox="0 0 24 24" fill="none" stroke="currentColor" stroke-width="1.8" stroke-linecap="round" stroke-linejoin="round" aria-hidden="true" focusable="false"><path d="M6 21V5a2 2 0 0 1 2-2h8a2 2 0 0 1 2 2v16"/><path d="M4 21h16"/><circle cx="14.6" cy="12.5" r=".95" fill="currentColor" stroke="none"/></svg></span><strong>Jacob</strong><span class="vz-kart-metin">Open your heart to Allah.</span></li><li><span class="vz-kart-ikon"><svg viewBox="0 0 24 24" fill="none" stroke="currentColor" stroke-width="1.8" stroke-linecap="round" stroke-linejoin="round" aria-hidden="true" focusable="false"><path d="M12 3.2c3.4 4.3 5.6 7.6 5.6 10.4a5.6 5.6 0 0 1-11.2 0c0-2.8 2.2-6.1 5.6-10.4z"/><path d="M9.3 14a2.8 2.8 0 0 0 2.5 2.9"/></svg></span><strong>Job</strong><span class="vz-kart-metin">Patience and supplication.</span></li><li><span class="vz-kart-ikon"><svg viewBox="0 0 24 24" fill="none" stroke="currentColor" stroke-width="1.8" stroke-linecap="round" stroke-linejoin="round" aria-hidden="true" focusable="false"><path d="M9 3L5 21M15 3l4 18"/><path d="M12 5v2.4M12 10.6V13M12 16.2v2.4"/></svg></span><strong>Moses</strong><span class="vz-kart-metin">A helper at his side.</span></li></ol><p class="vz-sema-alt">Three stops, one road: turning towards one’s Lord.</p></figure>

<p class="vz-hitap">Opening one’s heart to Allah does not break patience; patience is bringing one’s sorrow to Allah.</p>

<header class="vz-bolum" id="sabredene-mujde" tabindex="-1"><figure class="vz-bolum-sahne"><img class="vz-resim" src="/media/vaazlar/engelli-kardeslerimize-karsi-sorumluluklarimiz/fizik-tedavi-1600.webp" srcset="/media/vaazlar/engelli-kardeslerimize-karsi-sorumluluklarimiz/fizik-tedavi-480.webp 480w, /media/vaazlar/engelli-kardeslerimize-karsi-sorumluluklarimiz/fizik-tedavi-960.webp 960w, /media/vaazlar/engelli-kardeslerimize-karsi-sorumluluklarimiz/fizik-tedavi-1600.webp 1600w" sizes="(max-width: 640px) calc(100vw - 40px), (max-width: 1024px) calc(100vw - 64px), 736px" width="1600" height="893" alt="In a bright rehabilitation room, a man in a green tracksuit, seen from behind, walks between parallel bars; a physiotherapist in a blue tunic rests a supporting hand on his back." loading="lazy" decoding="async"><figcaption>Illustrative scene: The physiotherapy room</figcaption></figure><h2 class="vaaz-bolum-baslik"><span class="vz-no">IV.</span> Good news for those who are patient: the thorn, the two beloved ones, Umm Zufar</h2><p class="vz-bolum-oz">Our Lord, who gives recompense even for a thorn, counts no tear of the one who is patient as wasted.</p></header>

<p>Good news for those who are patient is great. The Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him) said: if a Muslim is struck by fatigue, illness, worry, sorrow, harm, distress, even a thorn that pricks, Allah covers some of their sins by it. (al-Bukhari, Marda 1; Muslim, Birr 52) This hadith does not say that the distress suffered is the penalty for sin; it brings the good news that our Lord turns even distress into a mercy. Another hadith praises the state of the believer: when joy comes, they give thanks; when distress comes, they are patient; both are good for them. (Muslim, Zuhd 64) In a hadith qudsi our Lord says:</p>

<figure class="vz-nass vz-hadis"><figcaption class="vz-nass-bas"><span class="vz-nass-tur">Hadith</span><span class="vz-nass-konu">Tested through the two beloved ones</span></figcaption><p lang="ar" dir="rtl" class="vz-arapca">إِذَا ابْتَلَيْتُ عَبْدِي بِحَبِيبَتَيْهِ فَصَبَرَ عَوَّضْتُهُ مِنْهُمَا الْجَنَّةَ</p><p class="vz-meal">Allah, the Exalted, said: “If I test My servant through his two beloved ones (his eyes) and he is patient, I give him Paradise in their place.”</p><p class="vz-kaynak">al-Bukhari, Marda 7</p></figure>

<p>This hadith does not say that losing one’s sight is a good thing; it tells the servant under test of the promise his Lord has given. The Prophet’s call to patience also shows that there was a choice. Ibn ‘Abbas (may Allah be pleased with him) narrates his conversation with a woman who had seizures:</p>

<aside class="vz-hikaye" aria-label="“I shall be patient, but…”"><p class="vz-kutu-tur">Umm Zufar</p><p class="vz-kutu-baslik">“I shall be patient, but…”</p><figure class="vz-nass vz-hadis"><p lang="ar" dir="rtl" class="vz-arapca">إِنِّي أُصْرَعُ، وَإِنِّي أَتَكَشَّفُ فَادْعُ اللَّهَ لِي قَالَ إِنْ شِئْتِ صَبَرْتِ وَلَكِ الْجَنَّةُ وَإِنْ شِئْتِ دَعَوْتُ اللَّهَ أَنْ يُعَافِيَكِ فَقَالَتْ أَصْبِرُ فَقَالَتْ إِنِّي أَتَكَشَّفُ فَادْعُ اللَّهَ أَنْ لاَ أَتَكَشَّفَ</p></figure><p>Ibn ‘Abbas (may Allah be pleased with him) pointed out to ‘Ata’ a woman leaning on the covering of the Kaaba and said, “Shall I show you a woman of Paradise?” This woman had come to the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him) and said, “I have seizures, and in a seizure I become uncovered; pray to Allah for me.” The Messenger of Allah said, “If you wish, be patient, and Paradise is yours; if you wish, I shall pray to Allah to grant you good health.” The woman said, “I shall be patient,” and then, “But I become uncovered; pray to Allah that I may not become uncovered.” Our Prophet prayed for her. <strong>He left the choice to her and respected her privacy.</strong></p><p class="vz-not">al-Bukhari, Marda 6; Muslim, Birr 54.</p></aside>

<p>In this narrative the woman made the decision herself: she chose patience and also asked for prayer that she might not be uncovered. Patience is not withdrawing into a shell, abandoning treatment or asking for nothing; our Lord gives those who are patient this good news: “Those who are patient will be given their reward without measure.” (az-Zumar 39:10)</p>

<figure class="vz-bolum-sahne vz-ara-sahne"><img class="vz-resim" src="/media/vaazlar/engelli-kardeslerimize-karsi-sorumluluklarimiz/park-yolu-1600.webp" srcset="/media/vaazlar/engelli-kardeslerimize-karsi-sorumluluklarimiz/park-yolu-480.webp 480w, /media/vaazlar/engelli-kardeslerimize-karsi-sorumluluklarimiz/park-yolu-960.webp 960w, /media/vaazlar/engelli-kardeslerimize-karsi-sorumluluklarimiz/park-yolu-1600.webp 1600w" sizes="(max-width: 640px) calc(100vw - 40px), (max-width: 1024px) calc(100vw - 64px), 736px" width="1600" height="893" alt="On a tree-lined park path covered with autumn leaves, a woman in a headscarf, seen from behind, moves forward pushing a small child in a wheelchair." loading="lazy" decoding="async"><figcaption>Illustrative scene: In the autumn park</figcaption></figure>

<p><strong>A scene from our life in Europe:</strong> On Eid morning, a young man who has come to the mosque courtyard in his wheelchair is brought in through the door with the help of two people; on his face the joy of Eid, in his eyes a question: “Who was waiting for me here?” And there is an uncle who, after a heart attack, is ashamed to pray on a chair; he sits alone in the back row. Yet what we should be ashamed of is not the chair, but the eyes that do not see him.</p>

<p class="vz-hitap">Our Lord, who gives recompense even for a thorn, counts no tear of the one who is patient as wasted.</p>

<header class="vz-bolum" id="abese-dersi" tabindex="-1"><figure class="vz-bolum-sahne"><img class="vz-resim" src="/media/vaazlar/engelli-kardeslerimize-karsi-sorumluluklarimiz/beyaz-baston-1600.webp" srcset="/media/vaazlar/engelli-kardeslerimize-karsi-sorumluluklarimiz/beyaz-baston-480.webp 480w, /media/vaazlar/engelli-kardeslerimize-karsi-sorumluluklarimiz/beyaz-baston-960.webp 960w, /media/vaazlar/engelli-kardeslerimize-karsi-sorumluluklarimiz/beyaz-baston-1600.webp 1600w" sizes="(max-width: 640px) calc(100vw - 40px), (max-width: 1024px) calc(100vw - 64px), 736px" width="1600" height="893" alt="A white cane leans against a wooden shoe rack where shoes are lined up at the mosque entrance; behind it a tiled wall border and a carpeted hall can be seen." loading="lazy" decoding="async"><figcaption>Illustrative scene: A white cane at the mosque entrance</figcaption></figure><h2 class="vaaz-bolum-baslik"><span class="vz-no">V.</span> Because the blind man came: the lesson of Surah ‘Abasa</h2><p class="vz-bolum-oz">We turn our face from no one; the person we speak to is our brother or sister in front of us, in person.</p></header>

<p>Let us now begin on the Messenger of Allah’s relationship with his Companions with disabilities with a gentle reproof in the Qur’an. According to what the sources of Qur’anic commentary and of the Prophet’s biography (sira) report, while our Prophet was speaking of Islam to the leaders of Mecca, Ibn Umm Maktum came running to him and asked him to teach him from what Allah had taught him. Surah ‘Abasa was revealed on this occasion (DİA, “Abese Sûresi”, I, 305–⁠306):</p>

<figure class="vz-nass vz-ayet"><figcaption class="vz-nass-bas"><span class="vz-nass-tur">Qur’anic verse</span><span class="vz-nass-konu">‘Abasa: “Because the man who could not see came to him”</span></figcaption><p lang="ar" dir="rtl" class="vz-arapca">عَبَسَ وَتَوَلَّىٰ ۝ أَن جَاءَهُ الْأَعْمَىٰ ۝ وَمَا يُدْرِيكَ لَعَلَّهُ يَزَّكَّىٰ ۝ أَوْ يَذَّكَّرُ فَتَنفَعَهُ الذِّكْرَىٰ</p><p class="vz-meal">He frowned and turned away. Because the man who could not see came to him. But (O Prophet!) how could you know, perhaps he would have purified himself. Or he would have taken advice, and that advice would have benefited him.</p><p class="vz-kaynak">‘Abasa 80:1–⁠4</p></figure>

<p>This reproof does not lower our Prophet’s worth; on the contrary, it shows that our Lord honours every human being, even in front of His most beloved servant. As the verses continue, they also say: “You pay no heed to the one who comes running to you with awe of Allah in his heart!” (‘Abasa 80:8–⁠10) The Messenger of Allah accepted this reproof, and afterwards he always showed honour to Ibn Umm Maktum. According to a narration whose chain is not known, whenever he saw him he would say, “Welcome to the one on whose account my Lord warned me!” (al-Qurtubi, commentary on ‘Abasa; DİA, “Abese Sûresi”, I, 305–⁠306) The lesson of the verse is this: we turn our face from no one for the sake of someone thought more important. The person we speak to is our brother or sister in front of us, not the one beside them.</p>

<p><strong>A scene from our life in Europe:</strong> An uncle who sees a young person who uses a wheelchair at the mosque door turns not to the young person but to the father beside them and says, “What does this one want?” The young person is able to answer in their own voice; what is missing is a face that turns to look at them. At the municipal counter, while an autistic young person tries to form a sentence, the official looks at their mother and waits for an answer. Yet to wait patiently for them to finish their sentence is the greatest courtesy. When did we last speak at eye level with a brother or sister who uses a wheelchair?</p>

<p class="vz-hitap">We turn our face from no one; the person we speak to is our brother or sister in front of us, in person.</p>

<p>Now let us return to the dawn adhan we recalled at the beginning of the sermon. Our Prophet said:</p>

<figure class="vz-nass vz-hadis"><figcaption class="vz-nass-bas"><span class="vz-nass-tur">Hadith</span><span class="vz-nass-konu">The muezzin who calls the dawn adhan</span></figcaption><p lang="ar" dir="rtl" class="vz-arapca">إِنَّ بِلاَلاً يُؤَذِّنُ بِلَيْلٍ، فَكُلُوا وَاشْرَبُوا حَتَّى يُنَادِيَ ابْنُ أُمِّ مَكْتُومٍ</p><p lang="ar" dir="rtl" class="vz-arapca">وَكَانَ رَجُلاً أَعْمَى لاَ يُنَادِي حَتَّى يُقَالَ لَهُ أَصْبَحْتَ أَصْبَحْتَ</p><p class="vz-meal"><em>The Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him) said: “Bilal calls the adhan at night; so eat and drink until Ibn Umm Maktum calls the adhan.”</em><br><em>The narrator of the hadith adds: “He was a blind man; he would not call the adhan until it was said to him, ‘Morning has come, morning has come!’” This last sentence is not our Prophet’s, but the narrator’s words.</em></p><p class="vz-kaynak">al-Bukhari, Adhan 11</p></figure>

<p>The one who can see tells him morning has come, and he makes the adhan heard across the whole city. That is, help does not go in one direction only: we become his eyes, and he becomes our voice. On thirteen occasions when the Messenger of Allah left Medina he appointed him in his place; he led the prayer for those who remained behind. (DİA, “İbn Ümmü Mektûm”, XX, 434–⁠435) It is reported, as the occasion of revelation, that the clause “except those who have a valid excuse” in Surah an-Nisa’ was also revealed upon his words; that is, the Qur’an answered the voice of a Companion with a disability. (al-Bukhari, Jihad 31; Tafsir, an-Nisa’ 18)</p>

<figure class="vz-nass vz-hadis"><figcaption class="vz-nass-bas"><span class="vz-nass-tur">Hadith</span><span class="vz-nass-konu">Do you hear the adhan?</span></figcaption><p lang="ar" dir="rtl" class="vz-arapca">رَجُلٌ أَعْمَى فَقَالَ يَا رَسُولَ اللَّهِ إِنَّهُ لَيْسَ لِي قَائِدٌ يَقُودُنِي إِلَى الْمَسْجِدِ فَسَأَلَ رَسُولَ اللَّهِ صلى الله عليه وسلم أَنْ يُرَخِّصَ لَهُ فَيُصَلِّيَ فِي بَيْتِهِ فَرَخَّصَ لَهُ فَلَمَّا وَلَّى دَعَاهُ فَقَالَ هَلْ تَسْمَعُ النِّدَاءَ بِالصَّلاَةِ فَقَالَ نَعَمْ قَالَ فَأَجِبْ</p><p class="vz-meal">A blind man came to our Prophet and asked for a concession to pray in his house; he granted it. When the man turned to go, he called him back: “Do you hear the adhan?” “Yes.” “Then respond.”</p><p class="vz-kaynak">Muslim, Masajid 255</p></figure>

<p>This hadith should not be taken to mean that a person with a visual impairment is obliged to come to the mosque. In the narration of Abu Dawud it is reported that this Companion was Ibn Umm Maktum and that his house was far away. (Abu Dawud, Salat 46; Ahmad ibn Hanbal, Musnad, III, 423–⁠424) Alongside the concession, the words “Then respond” also remind us of this: the real question is whether we have opened that road.</p>

According to the ilmihal, for a person with a visual impairment who has a guide, the Friday prayer is obligatory in the view of Abu Yusuf and Imam Muhammad, and not obligatory in the view of Abu Hanifa; that it is not obligatory for one who has no guide is a matter of agreement. One who cannot stand prays sitting, and one who cannot do even that prays lying down by gesture; our brother or sister who can pray sitting on a chair prays so. Praying on a chair is no barrier to joining the congregation: one joins the row, and the chair stands in line with the row. (İlmihal, “Hasta ve Engellilerin Namazı”; “Cemaatle Namaz”)</p></aside>

<p><strong>A scene from our life in Europe:</strong> On Friday morning a young person knocks on the door of the uncle on the third floor and brings him to the mosque door. Inside, at the end of the row, two chairs stand in line with the row, and there is a grab rail in the ablution room. None of this happened by itself.</p>

<p>The mosque opens its door to the one who can come; to the one who cannot, it goes itself. ‘Itban ibn Malik (may Allah be pleased with him) of Medina, though his eyes had grown weak, used to lead his people in prayer. What did he do on rainy days when he could not go to his mosque? He said to the Messenger of Allah:</p>

<aside class="vz-hikaye" aria-label="Two rak‘as in the house of ‘Itban"><p class="vz-kutu-tur">A LESSON FROM HISTORY</p><p class="vz-kutu-baslik">Two rak‘as in the house of ‘Itban</p><figure class="vz-nass vz-hadis"><p lang="ar" dir="rtl" class="vz-arapca">قَدْ أَنْكَرْتُ بَصَرِي، وَأَنَا أُصَلِّي لِقَوْمِي، فَإِذَا كَانَتِ الأَمْطَارُ سَالَ الْوَادِي الَّذِي بَيْنِي وَبَيْنَهُمْ، لَمْ أَسْتَطِعْ أَنْ آتِيَ مَسْجِدَهُمْ فَأُصَلِّيَ بِهِمْ، وَوَدِدْتُ يَا رَسُولَ اللَّهِ أَنَّكَ تَأْتِينِي فَتُصَلِّيَ فِي بَيْتِي، فَأَتَّخِذَهُ مُصَلًّى</p></figure><p>‘Itban (may Allah be pleased with him) said: “O Messenger of Allah! My eyes do not see well, and I lead my people in prayer. When it rains, the valley between me and them flows, and I cannot go to their mosque to lead them in prayer. I would like you to come to my house and pray in some place in it, so that I may take that place as my prayer-place.” The Messenger of Allah said, “If Allah wills, I shall.” The next day, when the sun had risen, the Messenger of Allah and Abu Bakr arrived. The Messenger of Allah asked, “Where in your house would you like me to pray?” ‘Itban pointed out the place. The Messenger of Allah said the takbir, the people lined up behind him, he led two rak‘as and ended with the salam. <strong>The mosque came to the house of the one who could not come to its door.</strong></p><p class="vz-not">al-Bukhari, Salat 46; Adhan 40.</p></aside>

<p>Then there was ‘Amr ibn al-Jamuh, the chief of the Banu Salima; he had trouble with his foot and found walking difficult. Although he was not required to go out to war, he wished to go to Uhud. When his sons, thinking of his condition, wished to stop him, he took his wish to the Messenger of Allah; the Messenger of Allah gave him permission. “O Messenger of Allah! If I fight in the way of Allah and am killed as a martyr, shall I walk on this foot of mine, sound, in Paradise?” he asked; our Prophet said, “Yes.” He fell as a martyr at Uhud together with his relatives; and the Messenger of Allah said, “I seem to see you walking in Paradise on that very foot of yours, whole and sound.” (Ahmad ibn Hanbal, Musnad, Arna’ut–Risala edition no. 22553, its chain is good (hasan); DİA, “Amr b. Cemûh”, III, 82–⁠83) As we see, the decision was his; the door was only opened for him.</p>

<figure class="vz-nass vz-ayet"><figcaption class="vz-nass-bas"><span class="vz-nass-tur">Qur’anic verse</span><span class="vz-nass-konu">No constraint</span></figcaption><p lang="ar" dir="rtl" class="vz-arapca">لَّيْسَ عَلَى الْأَعْمَىٰ حَرَجٌ وَلَا عَلَى الْأَعْرَجِ حَرَجٌ وَلَا عَلَى الْمَرِيضِ حَرَجٌ</p><p class="vz-meal">No constraint is laid on the blind, no constraint on the lame, no constraint on the sick.</p><p class="vz-kaynak">al-Fath 48:17; excerpt from the verse</p></figure>

<p>The word “lame” in the verse meaning describes, in today’s wording, our brother or sister with a mobility disability. This verse grants them a concession; a concession is not a prohibition but a door: whoever wishes may enter, and there is no compulsion on the one who does not wish. The surah of at-Tawba gives the same measure: “There is no responsibility on the weak, on the sick, and on those who have nothing to spend, as long as they remain loyal to Allah and His Messenger.” (at-Tawba 9:91) Jabir ibn ‘Abd Allah, Ka‘b ibn Malik, Sa‘d ibn Abi Waqqas, ‘Abd Allah ibn Abi Awfa, ‘Abbas ibn ‘Abd al-Muttalib, Abu Usayd and many other Companions lost their eyesight towards the end of their lives. (DİA, relevant entries)</p>

<p><strong>A scene from our life in Europe:</strong> To go with the congregation to the door of a brother or sister who cannot leave their home, and to pray one of the prayers together, brings a gain both to them and to us. For imams and teachers, visiting the sick and our brothers and sisters with disabilities is a duty. Giving a young person who uses a wheelchair a role in the mosque opens another door: treasurer, Qur’an teacher, assistant muezzin; whoever it may be, they will do more than we expect.</p>

<p class="vz-hitap">Going to the one who cannot come is also what a congregation is; the mosque goes to the door of the one who cannot come to its own.</p>

<p>The surah of an-Nur teaches the etiquette of the table together with our brothers and sisters with disabilities; it is reported that some Companions hesitated to eat at the same table as their brothers and sisters with disabilities, and the verse removed this hesitation.</p>

<figure class="vz-nass vz-ayet"><figcaption class="vz-nass-bas"><span class="vz-nass-tur">Qur’anic verse</span><span class="vz-nass-konu">There is room at the table for everyone</span></figcaption><p lang="ar" dir="rtl" class="vz-arapca">لَّيْسَ عَلَى الْأَعْمَىٰ حَرَجٌ وَلَا عَلَى الْأَعْرَجِ حَرَجٌ وَلَا عَلَى الْمَرِيضِ حَرَجٌ</p><p class="vz-meal">There is no harm for the blind, no harm for the lame, and no harm for the sick.</p><p class="vz-kaynak">an-Nur 24:61; excerpt from the verse</p></figure>

<p>Let us not keep the table divided; at the wedding table and at the Eid table let there always be a place for them: “There is no sin on you whether you eat together or separately.” (an-Nur 24:61) When our Prophet noticed that Sa‘d ibn Abi Waqqas regarded himself as above those beneath him, he said:</p>

<figure class="vz-nass vz-hadis"><figcaption class="vz-nass-bas"><span class="vz-nass-tur">Hadith</span><span class="vz-nass-konu">Through your weak ones</span></figcaption><p lang="ar" dir="rtl" class="vz-arapca">هَلْ تُنْصَرُونَ وَتُرْزَقُونَ إِلاَّ بِضُعَفَائِكُمْ</p><p class="vz-meal">Sa‘d (may Allah be pleased with him) thought that he had a superiority over those beneath him. Upon this the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him) said: “You are only helped and given provision because of your weak ones, is it not so?”</p><p class="vz-kaynak">al-Bukhari, Jihad 76</p></figure>

<p>Among those we regard as weak and powerless are also our brothers and sisters with disabilities; through them we learn to show mercy, to share, to learn. A woman with a condition affecting her mind also had a request of the Messenger of Allah:</p>

<aside class="vz-hikaye" aria-label="At the corner of the street"><p class="vz-kutu-tur">A LESSON FROM HISTORY</p><p class="vz-kutu-baslik">At the corner of the street</p><figure class="vz-nass vz-hadis"><p lang="ar" dir="rtl" class="vz-arapca">امْرَأَةً، كَانَ فِي عَقْلِهَا شَىْءٌ فَقَالَتْ يَا رَسُولَ اللَّهِ إِنَّ لِي إِلَيْكَ حَاجَةً فَقَالَ يَا أُمَّ فُلاَنٍ انْظُرِي أَىَّ السِّكَكِ شِئْتِ حَتَّى أَقْضِيَ لَكِ حَاجَتَكِ فَخَلاَ مَعَهَا فِي بَعْضِ الطُّرُقِ حَتَّى فَرَغَتْ مِنْ حَاجَتِهَا</p></figure><p>A woman with a condition affecting her mind said: “O Messenger of Allah! I have a request of you.” The Messenger of Allah said: “O mother of so-and-so! Choose whichever street you like; I shall stand with you until you have seen to your need.” And he drew aside with her to a corner of the road and stayed beside her until she had finished. <strong>Our Prophet saw her too as a person worth listening to.</strong></p><p class="vz-not">Muslim, Fada’il 76.</p></aside>

<p>Our Prophet also gave tasks to his Companions with disabilities: he left Ibn Umm Maktum in charge in Medina; he sent Mu‘adh ibn Jabal, who had a problem with his foot, to Yemen as envoy, collector of zakat and judge. (DİA, “Muâz b. Cebel”, XXX, 336–⁠338) ‘Ata’ ibn Abi Rabah, the foremost authority on the knowledge of the pilgrimage (hajj), also lost his sight at the end of his life; it is reported that the caliph Sulayman ibn ‘Abd al-Malik came with his sons to ask him about the rites of the hajj, and sat beside him and waited while ‘Ata’ was praying. (DİA, “Atâ b. Ebû Rebâh”, IV, 35–⁠36) In the hadith, service is also counted as charity: guiding the one who cannot see, explaining to the one who cannot hear or speak until they understand, and supporting the weak with one’s arm are among the doors of the charity a person gives on behalf of their own self. (Ahmad ibn Hanbal, Musnad, V, 168–⁠169, Abu Dharr)</p>

<figure class="vz-bolum-sahne vz-ara-sahne"><img class="vz-resim" src="/media/vaazlar/engelli-kardeslerimize-karsi-sorumluluklarimiz/kabartma-mushaf-1600.webp" srcset="/media/vaazlar/engelli-kardeslerimize-karsi-sorumluluklarimiz/kabartma-mushaf-480.webp 480w, /media/vaazlar/engelli-kardeslerimize-karsi-sorumluluklarimiz/kabartma-mushaf-960.webp 960w, /media/vaazlar/engelli-kardeslerimize-karsi-sorumluluklarimiz/kabartma-mushaf-1600.webp 1600w" sizes="(max-width: 640px) calc(100vw - 40px), (max-width: 1024px) calc(100vw - 64px), 736px" width="1600" height="893" alt="The fingers of two hands move across the page of a book in raised dots (Braille) lying on a carved Qur’an stand." loading="lazy" decoding="async"><figcaption>Illustrative scene: The Braille mushaf</figcaption></figure>

<p><strong>A scene from our life in Europe:</strong> At the entrance of the wedding hall there are three steps; the uncle who uses a wheelchair waits at the door, with the sound of drums coming from inside. An autistic child who was left off the guest list of a birthday party goes quietly to school the next day. At a job application there is a door that closes at the sight of a wheelchair. When a school decides on “special education” or inclusive education, not leaving the family alone and standing beside them in their meetings with the support team is also our task.</p>

<p class="vz-hitap">Those we regard as weak are a means of help and provision; the table is blessed through them.</p>

<header class="vz-bolum" id="engelliye-engel-olmayalim" tabindex="-1"><figure class="vz-bolum-sahne"><img class="vz-resim" src="/media/vaazlar/engelli-kardeslerimize-karsi-sorumluluklarimiz/kaldirim-rampasi-1600.webp" srcset="/media/vaazlar/engelli-kardeslerimize-karsi-sorumluluklarimiz/kaldirim-rampasi-480.webp 480w, /media/vaazlar/engelli-kardeslerimize-karsi-sorumluluklarimiz/kaldirim-rampasi-960.webp 960w, /media/vaazlar/engelli-kardeslerimize-karsi-sorumluluklarimiz/kaldirim-rampasi-1600.webp 1600w" sizes="(max-width: 640px) calc(100vw - 40px), (max-width: 1024px) calc(100vw - 64px), 736px" width="1600" height="893" alt="On a rainy evening, an empty, unobstructed pavement ramp in front of a pedestrian crossing, with the reflections of street lamps on the wet ground." loading="lazy" decoding="async"><figcaption>Illustrative scene: A pavement ramp</figcaption></figure><h2 class="vaaz-bolum-baslik"><span class="vz-no">IX.</span> Let us not be a barrier to our brothers and sisters with disabilities: the ramp, the parking space, our words</h2><p class="vz-bolum-oz">Even two minutes of parking that blocks a ramp is a matter of other people’s rights; let us not be a barrier to our brothers and sisters with disabilities.</p></header>

<p>The barrier in front of our brother or sister with a disability is, more often than not, not in their body but in what we leave there: the car in front of the ramp, the bicycle on the pavement, the bin left in front of the lift door. Our Prophet taught that the road too has a right. To the Companions who sat on the roads he said, “Give the road its right,” and listed this right as follows:</p>

<figure class="vz-nass vz-hadis"><figcaption class="vz-nass-bas"><span class="vz-nass-tur">Hadith</span><span class="vz-nass-konu">The right of the road</span></figcaption><p lang="ar" dir="rtl" class="vz-arapca">فَأَعْطُوا الطَّرِيقَ حَقَّهَا</p><p lang="ar" dir="rtl" class="vz-arapca">غَضُّ الْبَصَرِ، وَكَفُّ الأَذَى، وَرَدُّ السَّلاَمِ، وَأَمْرٌ بِالْمَعْرُوفِ، وَنَهْىٌ عَنِ الْمُنْكَرِ</p><p class="vz-meal">Our Prophet said: “Since you will not give up sitting there, then at least give the road its right.” When the Companions said, “What is the right of the road, O Messenger of Allah?”, he said: “Not looking at what is forbidden, not disturbing those who pass by, returning the greeting, urging what is good and preventing what is wrong.”</p><p class="vz-kaynak">al-Bukhari, Mazalim 22; Muslim, Libas 114</p></figure>

<p>A branch of faith is also removing from the road whatever causes harm. (Muslim, Iman 58) It is also reported that whoever misleads our brother or sister who cannot see from their way is cursed. (Ahmad ibn Hanbal, Musnad, I, 217, 317) Our tongue and our screen have their road too: “Do not belittle one another, and do not give one another bad names.” (al-Hujurat 49:11)</p>

<aside class="vz-olcu" aria-label="The rights of others that seem small"><span class="vz-olcu-ikon"><svg viewBox="0 0 24 24" fill="none" stroke="currentColor" stroke-width="1.8" stroke-linecap="round" stroke-linejoin="round" aria-hidden="true" focusable="false"><path d="M12 3v17M8 21h8M5 7h14"/><path d="M5 7l-3 6a3 3 0 0 0 6 0z"/><path d="M19 7l-3 6a3 3 0 0 0 6 0z"/></svg></span><p class="vz-kutu-tur">GUIDANCE FROM FIQH</p><p class="vz-kutu-baslik">The rights of others that seem small</p><p>Parking “for two minutes” in an accessible parking space, blocking the front of a ramp or the pavement with a car, a bicycle or a bin, and occupying the lift without need are each a matter of the rights of others. Insults such as “Are you blind?”, “Handicapped!” and, in the schoolyard, the French « handicapé ! » are also a matter of the rights of others. Secretly filming a person with a disability and making others laugh at them on social media is the same. For each of these one must seek forgiveness and be reconciled (halal); the right of another person does not lapse without the forgiveness of the one who holds that right.</p></aside>

<p><strong>A scene from our life in Europe:</strong> On Friday, outside the mosque, cars parked up on the pavement are blocking on one side a neighbour’s garage door and on the other the pavement ramp of the uncle who uses a wheelchair. We all leave our cars only for two minutes. When we come to the Friday prayer, where do we leave our car? At home too we must explain to our child that imitating a friend with a disability and laughing is not a game, that it opens a wound in a friend’s heart. Let us stand in the way of those who put barriers in front of our brothers and sisters with disabilities; but let us begin the work with ourselves.</p>

<p class="vz-hitap">Even two minutes of parking that blocks a ramp is a matter of other people’s rights; let us not be a barrier to our brothers and sisters with disabilities.</p>

<p>Beside our brother or sister with a disability there is often someone keeping watch whom we do not see: their mother, father, sibling or spouse. Our Lord sees their weariness. In a hadith qudsi it is said:</p>

<figure class="vz-nass vz-hadis"><figcaption class="vz-nass-bas"><span class="vz-nass-tur">Hadith</span><span class="vz-nass-konu">Allah’s nearness to the one who visits the sick</span></figcaption><p lang="ar" dir="rtl" class="vz-arapca">إِنَّ اللَّهَ عَزَّ وَجَلَّ يَقُولُ يَوْمَ الْقِيَامَةِ يَا ابْنَ آدَمَ مَرِضْتُ فَلَمْ تَعُدْنِي قَالَ يَا رَبِّ كَيْفَ أَعُودُكَ وَأَنْتَ رَبُّ الْعَالَمِينَ قَالَ أَمَا عَلِمْتَ أَنَّ عَبْدِي فُلاَنًا مَرِضَ فَلَمْ تَعُدْهُ أَمَا عَلِمْتَ أَنَّكَ لَوْ عُدْتَهُ لَوَجَدْتَنِي عِنْدَهُ</p><p class="vz-meal">On the Day of Resurrection Allah, the Exalted, will say: “Son of Adam, I fell ill and you did not visit Me.” He will say: “My Lord, how could I visit You, when You are the Lord of the worlds?” Allah, the Exalted, will say: “Do you not remember that such-and-such a servant of Mine fell ill and you did not visit him? Know that had you visited him, you would have found Me beside him.”</p><p class="vz-kaynak">Muslim, Birr 43</p></figure>

<figure class="vz-nass vz-hadis"><figcaption class="vz-nass-bas"><span class="vz-nass-tur">Hadith</span><span class="vz-nass-konu">Whoever helps their brother or sister</span></figcaption><p lang="ar" dir="rtl" class="vz-arapca">مَنْ نَفَّسَ عَنْ مُؤْمِنٍ كُرْبَةً مِنْ كُرَبِ الدُّنْيَا نَفَّسَ اللَّهُ عَنْهُ كُرْبَةً مِنْ كُرَبِ يَوْمِ الْقِيَامَةِ وَمَنْ يَسَّرَ عَلَى مُعْسِرٍ يَسَّرَ اللَّهُ عَلَيْهِ فِي الدُّنْيَا وَالآخِرَةِ وَمَنْ سَتَرَ مُسْلِمًا سَتَرَهُ اللَّهُ فِي الدُّنْيَا وَالآخِرَةِ وَاللَّهُ فِي عَوْنِ الْعَبْدِ مَا كَانَ الْعَبْدُ فِي عَوْنِ أَخِيهِ</p><p class="vz-meal">Whoever relieves a believer of one of the distresses of this world, Allah will relieve him of one of the distresses of the Day of Resurrection. Whoever eases things for one in difficulty, Allah will ease things for him in this world and the Hereafter. Whoever covers a Muslim’s fault, Allah will cover his fault in this world and the Hereafter. Allah is at the help of His servant for as long as the servant is helping his brother.</p><p class="vz-kaynak">Muslim, Dhikr 38; Abu Dawud, Adab 60</p></figure>

<p>Today there are also parents of ours who live with a limitation; for them too the Qur’an addresses us: “If one or both of them grow old with you, do not even say ‘Ugh!’ to them. Do not scold them!” (al-Isra’ 17:23) We are not angry with parents who want to hide their child out of the thought “What will people say?”; but we correct that thought together: a child is not hidden, but held close to our hearts. Making use of the support services of the school, the municipality and the region is a right and no shame. Consulting a doctor before marriage is also a wise precaution for the coming generations.</p>

<aside class="vz-olcu" aria-label="As far as your strength allows"><span class="vz-olcu-ikon"><svg viewBox="0 0 24 24" fill="none" stroke="currentColor" stroke-width="1.8" stroke-linecap="round" stroke-linejoin="round" aria-hidden="true" focusable="false"><path d="M12 3v17M8 21h8M5 7h14"/><path d="M5 7l-3 6a3 3 0 0 0 6 0z"/><path d="M19 7l-3 6a3 3 0 0 0 6 0z"/></svg></span><p class="vz-kutu-tur">GUIDANCE FROM FIQH</p><p class="vz-kutu-baslik">As far as your strength allows</p><p>“Pray standing; if you are not able, pray sitting; and if you are not able even to do that, pray lying on your side.” (al-Bukhari, Taqsir al-salat 19; ‘Imran ibn Husayn) The ilmihal gives these measures: if the injured limb is bandaged, one wipes over the bandage; for a prosthesis fitted in place of a missing limb, it is not necessary to wash or wipe it, it is enough that it is clean; if water cannot be used, one performs dry ablution (tayammum). Recitation falls away from one who cannot memorise. Our brother or sister with a hearing or speech impairment prays by performing the fard, wajib and sunna acts other than reciting the Qur’an and supplications. Our brother or sister with a severe intellectual disability is not religiously responsible; to look after them, however, is our act of worship. (İlmihal, “Hasta ve Engellilerin Namazı”)</p></aside>

<p><strong>A scene from our life in Europe:</strong> A mother who puts her child on the school transport at seven in the morning keeps watch through the night for the times of the medicine; her other children sometimes fall silent over the question “Who will look after me?” That evening there is a knock at the door: the neighbour says, “This Saturday afternoon let me look after the child; you rest a little.” When did we last ask the mother of the child with a disability next door, “Do you need anything?”</p>

<p class="vz-hitap">Whoever lightens the burden of a family living with disability will find our Lord beside that servant.</p>

<p>Let us sum up our talk with a statement of the Qur’an: “The truth is that it is not eyes that go blind, but the hearts within the breasts that go blind.” (al-Hajj 22:46) The real lack is not in the limits of the body, but in the heart closing itself to the truth. The Messenger of Allah told ‘Amr ibn al-Jamuh that he would walk in Paradise on his foot, perfectly sound; the mercy of our Lord encompasses our shortcomings and our tears alike. Let us also recall the dawn adhan from the beginning of the sermon: the one who could see told him morning had come, and he made the adhan heard across the whole city. This is not a scene that happened once and was over; this is the order of a congregation.</p>

<p><strong>A scene from our life in Europe:</strong> This evening we hang the pledge plaque by the front door of the house, at eye level. Our child comes and asks, “What is this?” And we read the first article aloud to them: “I will look at every person as a servant whom Allah has honoured.” Tomorrow morning, as we walk to the door with the key in hand, our eyes catch the plaque again. A barrier-free community is built from small words seen every day.</p>

<p><strong>Allah looks not at our appearances but at our hearts; the limitation may be in the body, but the real barrier is a hard heart and a closed door: let us be eyes for those who cannot see, ears for those who cannot hear, a shoulder for those who cannot walk; our brother or sister with a disability is not our burden but a trust and a companion on the way.</strong> As we leave this Friday prayer let us ask ourselves three questions: <strong>Whose barrier did I remove today? Whom did I speak to face to face? Which family did I give a moment to breathe?</strong> When we go home this evening, let us do one small thing: knock on a neighbour’s door, make a phone call, offer a greeting, draw a chair into line with the row. A barrier begins to fall with a small step.</p>
