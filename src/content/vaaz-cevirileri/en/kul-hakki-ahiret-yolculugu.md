<p class="vz-hitap">Dear congregation! My dear brothers and sisters, who here in Europe are in pursuit of a lawful (halal) livelihood, a clean name and hearts that are not broken!</p>

<p>After the funeral prayer (janaza), standing before the musalla stone, all of us hear the same question: <strong>“What was the deceased like, as you knew him? Do you release him from what he owed you?”</strong> The congregation answers with one voice, “Helâl olsun” (“May it be released”). At that moment no one speaks of the deceased’s account, title or car; only this is asked: <strong>Is any person’s right still outstanding against this brother of ours?</strong> Let us ask ourselves the same question today, while we are still on our feet.</p>

<figure class="vz-nass vz-ayet"><figcaption class="vz-nass-bas"><span class="vz-nass-tur">Qur’anic verse</span><span class="vz-nass-konu">An atom’s weight</span></figcaption><p lang="ar" dir="rtl" class="vz-arapca">فَمَن يَعْمَلْ مِثْقَالَ ذَرَّةٍ خَيْرًا يَرَهُ ۝ وَمَن يَعْمَلْ مِثْقَالَ ذَرَّةٍ شَرًّا يَرَهُ</p><p class="vz-meal">Whoever has done an atom’s weight of good will see it (its recompense). And whoever has done an atom’s weight of evil will see it (its recompense).</p><p class="vz-kaynak">az-Zalzala, 99:7–⁠8</p></figure>

<p>A human being is on a journey in this worldly life. This journey begins with birth and does not end with death; death is the gate of an eternal life. This world is passing, the Hereafter lasting. For this reason the believer lives every act as a trust for which an account will be asked in the Hereafter. Our Lord tells us that nothing we do will go unrequited. A person forgets some of what they have done, and people forget wrongs in time; but nothing is hidden from Allah’s knowledge.</p>

<p><strong>The rights of others</strong> are the material and moral rights that people hold over one another. Taking someone’s property unjustly, not paying a debt, betraying a trust, withholding a worker’s labour, cheating in trade: each of these violates the material rights of others. Belittling a person, mocking, backbiting and slandering, damaging their honour and reputation: each of these violates their moral rights. So the rights of others are not only “consuming someone’s money”; they are a wide responsibility that embraces all our relationships, from family life to trade, from the neighbourhood to the workplace, from friendship to social media.</p>

<p>Today we shall speak of the foundation of the rights of others, of their material and moral sides, and of the responsibility that rests on the tongue, on social media and on public property; we shall see the reckoning of the Hereafter in the hadith of the bankrupt, and learn the way out, settling accounts and seeking release. Our key sentence is this: <strong>A right owed to another is not lifted until it is returned to its owner or the owner releases us from it; whoever does not settle it today will pay with their good deeds on the day when dinar and dirham are of no use.</strong></p>

<figure class="vz-nass vz-ayet"><figcaption class="vz-nass-bas"><span class="vz-nass-tur">Qur’anic verse</span><span class="vz-nass-konu">Render trusts to those entitled to them</span></figcaption><p lang="ar" dir="rtl" class="vz-arapca">إِنَّ اللَّهَ يَأْمُرُكُمْ أَن تُؤَدُّوا الْأَمَانَاتِ إِلَىٰ أَهْلِهَا وَإِذَا حَكَمْتُم بَيْنَ النَّاسِ أَن تَحْكُمُوا بِالْعَدْلِ</p><p class="vz-meal">Allah commands you to render trusts to those entitled to them, and, when you judge between people, to judge with justice.</p><p class="vz-kaynak">an-Nisa’, 4:58; excerpt from the verse</p></figure>

<p>The verse shows the two pillars of the rights of others: <strong>the trust</strong> and <strong>justice</strong>. The trust is not only a belonging that someone leaves with us. Every duty, office, profession, piece of knowledge and talent given to us; our family, our health, our word and our secrets are also a trust. A person is bound to use the means given to them in a way that pleases Allah. (Kur’an Yolu, vol. 2, pp. 81–⁠83)</p>

<p>The Qur’an describes the weight of the trust like this: “We offered the trust to the heavens, the earth and the mountains, but they refused to bear it and were afraid of it, and the human being bore it.” (al-Ahzab, 33:72) The human being, who shouldered this burden from which even the mountains shrank, is bound to do it justice. Our Prophet also names one of the marks of the hypocrite in these words: <strong>“When he is entrusted, he betrays.”</strong> (al-Bukhari, Iman, 24; Muslim, Iman, 107)</p>

<aside class="vz-hikaye" aria-label="The trusts that remained in Mecca"><p class="vz-kutu-tur">A LESSON FROM HISTORY</p><p class="vz-kutu-baslik">The trusts that remained in Mecca</p><p>Even the polytheists of Mecca, who tormented our Prophet and in the end drove him from his homeland, used to call him “al-Amin”, the Trustworthy, and left their valuables with him. When our Prophet left his house on the night of the hijra, he left those trusts with ‘Ali so that they would reach their owners, asking him to hand them over and only then to follow. ‘Ali, with his own life in danger, returned the trusts to their owners one by one, and then migrated to Medina. <strong>Even an enemy’s trust is a trust.</strong></p><p class="vz-not">The Prophet’s biography (sira); TDV İslâm Ansiklopedisi, “Hicret”, “Ali”; a narration from the biography, in summary.</p></aside>

<header class="vz-bolum vz-devam" id="adalet" tabindex="-1"><h3 class="vz-ara-baslik">Justice: Wanting for our brother what we want for ourselves</h3></header>

<figure class="vz-nass vz-hadis"><figcaption class="vz-nass-bas"><span class="vz-nass-tur">Hadith</span><span class="vz-nass-konu">What you wish for yourself</span></figcaption><p lang="ar" dir="rtl" class="vz-arapca">لا يُؤْمِنُ أَحَدُكُمْ حَتَّى يُحِبَّ لأَخِيهِ مَا يُحِبُّ لِنَفْسِهِ</p><p class="vz-meal">According to the narration of Anas (may Allah be pleased with him), our Prophet said: “None of you has truly believed until he wants for his brother what he wants for himself.”</p><p class="vz-kaynak">al-Bukhari, Iman, 7 (13); Muslim, Iman, 71 (45)</p></figure>

<p>Justice is not to be sought only in the courtroom: a father must be just towards his child, a spouse towards a spouse, an employer towards a worker, a trader towards a customer, a neighbour towards a neighbour. The measure is clear: to want for our brother what we want for ourselves. Our servanthood to Allah must also show in how we treat Allah’s servants.</p>

<figure class="vz-nass vz-ayet"><figcaption class="vz-nass-bas"><span class="vz-nass-tur">Qur’anic verse</span><span class="vz-nass-konu">Hatred towards a people</span></figcaption><p lang="ar" dir="rtl" class="vz-arapca">وَلَا يَجْرِمَنَّكُمْ شَنَآنُ قَوْمٍ عَلَىٰ أَلَّا تَعْدِلُوا اعْدِلُوا هُوَ أَقْرَبُ لِلتَّقْوَىٰ</p><p class="vz-meal">Let not hatred for any people lead you to act unjustly. Be just; that is closer to taqwa (piety).</p><p class="vz-kaynak">al-Ma’ida, 5:8; excerpt from the verse</p></figure>

<p>This measure does not hold only among Muslims. The right of our Belgian neighbour, of our employer, of our landlord, even of the state, is also a right; the verse says that not even hatred can be an excuse for injustice. <strong>The rights of others recognise no religion, language or colour.</strong> When our Prophet passed away, even his armour was pledged with a Jewish man against a debt of barley. (al-Bukhari, Jihad, 89 (2916))</p>

<figure class="vz-nass vz-hadis"><figcaption class="vz-nass-bas"><span class="vz-nass-tur">Hadith</span><span class="vz-nass-konu">I have forbidden wrongdoing</span></figcaption><p lang="ar" dir="rtl" class="vz-arapca">يَا عِبَادِي إِنِّي حَرَّمْتُ الظُّلْمَ عَلَى نَفْسِي وَجَعَلْتُهُ بَيْنَكُمْ مُحَرَّمًا فَلاَ تَظَالَمُوا</p><p class="vz-meal">According to Abu Dharr (may Allah be pleased with him), who reports from our Prophet, who reports from his Lord, Allah says: “O My servants! I have forbidden wrongdoing for Myself, and I have made it forbidden among you as well; so do not wrong one another.”</p><p class="vz-kaynak">Muslim, Birr, 55 (2577); hadith qudsi (sacred hadith)</p></figure>

<figure class="vz-sema"><figcaption><strong>The two faces of rights</strong>A right of others lapses by a different route from a right of Allah.</figcaption><div class="vz-denge"><div class="vz-kefe"><p class="vz-kefe-ad">The rights of Allah (huququllah)</p><ul><li>Debts of worship such as prayer, fasting and zakat</li><li>Between servant and Lord</li><li>Forgiveness is hoped for through sincere repentance and seeking forgiveness (istighfar)</li></ul></div><div class="vz-terazi"><svg viewBox="0 0 24 24" fill="none" stroke="currentColor" stroke-width="1.8" stroke-linecap="round" stroke-linejoin="round" aria-hidden="true" focusable="false"><path d="M12 3v17M8 21h8M5 7h14"/><path d="M5 7l-3 6a3 3 0 0 0 6 0z"/><path d="M19 7l-3 6a3 3 0 0 0 6 0z"/></svg></div><div class="vz-kefe"><p class="vz-kefe-ad">The rights of others (huquq al-‘ibad)</p><ul><li>Property, labour and debt; honour, dignity and reputation</li><li>Between one person and another; Allah too is witness</li><li>In addition to repentance, one must return what is owed or settle accounts and seek release</li></ul></div></div><p class="vz-sema-alt">Whoever tramples another’s right tramples both that right and Allah’s command.</p></figure>

<p>Failings against Allah are hoped to be forgiven through sincere repentance; a right of another person, by contrast, lapses only by being paid to its owner or by obtaining the owner’s consent. The conditions of repentance are to abandon the sin, to feel regret and to resolve not to return to it; if the sin is a violation of another person’s right, one must also return what is owed or ask for release (helallik). (DİA, “Kul Hakkı”, “Tövbe”)</p>

<header class="vz-bolum" id="mal-ve-ticaret" tabindex="-1"><figure class="vz-bolum-sahne"><img class="vz-resim" src="/media/vaazlar/kul-hakki-ahiret-yolculugu/bakkal-terazi-1600.webp" srcset="/media/vaazlar/kul-hakki-ahiret-yolculugu/bakkal-terazi-480.webp 480w, /media/vaazlar/kul-hakki-ahiret-yolculugu/bakkal-terazi-960.webp 960w, /media/vaazlar/kul-hakki-ahiret-yolculugu/bakkal-terazi-1600.webp 1600w" sizes="(max-width: 640px) calc(100vw - 40px), (max-width: 1024px) calc(100vw - 64px), 736px" width="1600" height="900" alt="In a greengrocer’s shop, the shopkeeper in an apron has put his hand on the needle of a scale with tomatoes in its pan; on the left a woman in a red headscarf arranges the shelves, on the right an elderly woman with a basket, in front of a rainy window, looks at the scale; crates of apples, peppers and lemons stand in the foreground." loading="lazy" decoding="async"><figcaption>Illustrative scene: At the scale</figcaption></figure><h2 class="vaaz-bolum-baslik"><span class="vz-no">II.</span> Material rights of others: property and trade</h2><p class="vz-bolum-oz">Let us not look for the measure only on the scale: Is our earning lawful (halal)?</p></header>

<figure class="vz-nass vz-ayet"><figcaption class="vz-nass-bas"><span class="vz-nass-tur">Qur’anic verse</span><span class="vz-nass-konu">Do not consume your wealth unjustly</span></figcaption><p lang="ar" dir="rtl" class="vz-arapca">وَلَا تَأْكُلُوا أَمْوَالَكُم بَيْنَكُم بِالْبَاطِلِ</p><p class="vz-meal">Do not consume your wealth among yourselves unjustly.</p><p class="vz-kaynak">al-Baqara, 2:188; excerpt from the verse</p></figure>

<p>The material rights of others show themselves most plainly in property: taking property unjustly, not paying a debt, not handing back a trust, underpaying a worker, hiding the defect in defective goods. The Qur’an draws particular attention to injustice in measure and weight:</p>

<figure class="vz-nass vz-ayet"><figcaption class="vz-nass-bas"><span class="vz-nass-tur">Qur’anic verse</span><span class="vz-nass-konu">Woe to those who give short measure</span></figcaption><p lang="ar" dir="rtl" class="vz-arapca">وَيْلٌ لِّلْمُطَفِّفِينَ ۝ الَّذِينَ إِذَا اكْتَالُوا عَلَى النَّاسِ يَسْتَوْفُونَ ۝ وَإِذَا كَالُوهُمْ أَو وَّزَنُوهُمْ يُخْسِرُونَ</p><p class="vz-meal">Woe to those who give short measure! Those who, when they take a measure from people, take it in full; but when they measure or weigh for others, they give less (they diminish).</p><p class="vz-kaynak">al-Mutaffifin, 83:1–⁠3</p></figure>

<p>Right after the warning the Day of the Hereafter is recalled: “Do they not think that they will be raised up for that great Day — the Day when people will stand before the Lord of the worlds?” (al-Mutaffifin, 83:4–⁠6) So injustice in trade is a responsibility for which an account will be given in the Hereafter. (Kur’an Yolu, vol. 5, pp. 573–⁠574)</p>

<figure class="vz-sema"><figcaption><strong>The faces of the scale today</strong>Six behaviours that spoil the measure.</figcaption><div class="vz-mizan"><div class="vz-terazi"><svg viewBox="0 0 24 24" fill="none" stroke="currentColor" stroke-width="1.8" stroke-linecap="round" stroke-linejoin="round" aria-hidden="true" focusable="false"><path d="M12 3v17M8 21h8M5 7h14"/><path d="M5 7l-3 6a3 3 0 0 0 6 0z"/><path d="M19 7l-3 6a3 3 0 0 0 6 0z"/></svg></div><ul class="vz-mizan-liste"><li>Selling faulty goods as sound</li><li>Giving poor service and charging in full</li><li>Neglecting one’s work</li><li>Delaying a worker’s wages</li><li>Exploiting a customer’s distraction</li><li>Deceiving with fake reviews and ratings</li></ul></div><p class="vz-sema-alt">al-Mutaffifin, 83:1–⁠6: Those who give short measure forget the Day of Reckoning.</p></figure>

<figure class="vz-nass vz-hadis"><figcaption class="vz-nass-bas"><span class="vz-nass-tur">Hadith</span><span class="vz-nass-konu">The wet grain</span></figcaption><p lang="ar" dir="rtl" class="vz-arapca">أَفَلاَ جَعَلْتَهُ فَوْقَ الطَّعَامِ كَىْ يَرَاهُ النَّاسُ مَنْ غَشَّ فَلَيْسَ مِنِّي</p><p class="vz-meal"><em>Abu Hurayra (may Allah be pleased with him) reports: The Messenger of Allah put his hand into a heap of wheat, and his fingers became wet. “Owner of the food, what is this?” he asked. When the man said, “The rain wetted it,” he said: “Then why did you not put it on top of the food, so that people could see it?</em> <strong><em>Whoever deceives is not one of us.”</em></strong></p><p class="vz-kaynak">Muslim, Iman, 164 (102)</p></figure>

<figure class="vz-bolum-sahne vz-ara-sahne"><img class="vz-resim" src="/media/vaazlar/kul-hakki-ahiret-yolculugu/araba-ayip-1600.webp" srcset="/media/vaazlar/kul-hakki-ahiret-yolculugu/araba-ayip-480.webp 480w, /media/vaazlar/kul-hakki-ahiret-yolculugu/araba-ayip-960.webp 960w, /media/vaazlar/kul-hakki-ahiret-yolculugu/araba-ayip-1600.webp 1600w" sizes="(max-width: 640px) calc(100vw - 40px), (max-width: 1024px) calc(100vw - 64px), 736px" width="1600" height="900" alt="At a second-hand car market, a crouching seller points out a dent and a scratch on the door of a silver car; beside him a young woman in a headscarf and a young man in a jacket look on attentively." loading="lazy" decoding="async"><figcaption>Illustrative scene: Stating the defect</figcaption></figure>

<p>Let us not look for the measure only on the scale: selling faulty goods as sound, charging the full fee for deficient service and deceiving with false reviews also spoil the measure. The believer’s question is not only “How much did I earn?” but <strong>“Is my earning lawful (halal)?”</strong> Taking advantage of the distraction of one of our brothers or sisters also falls, in conscience, under the rights of others.</p>

<header class="vz-bolum vz-devam" id="borc-ve-alin-teri" tabindex="-1"><figure class="vz-bolum-sahne"><img class="vz-resim" src="/media/vaazlar/kul-hakki-ahiret-yolculugu/borc-odeme-1600.webp" srcset="/media/vaazlar/kul-hakki-ahiret-yolculugu/borc-odeme-480.webp 480w, /media/vaazlar/kul-hakki-ahiret-yolculugu/borc-odeme-960.webp 960w, /media/vaazlar/kul-hakki-ahiret-yolculugu/borc-odeme-1600.webp 1600w" sizes="(max-width: 640px) calc(100vw - 40px), (max-width: 1024px) calc(100vw - 64px), 736px" width="1600" height="900" alt="On a rainy evening in a kitchen, at a wooden table with tea glasses, a plate of simit (sesame bread rings) and an open notebook, a man holds out a white envelope to a bearded man who has put his hand on his chest; in the background a woman in a headscarf prepares tea." loading="lazy" decoding="async"><figcaption>Illustrative scene: Paying the debt</figcaption></figure><h3 class="vz-ara-baslik">Debt and the sweat of one’s brow</h3></header>

<figure class="vz-nass vz-hadis"><figcaption class="vz-nass-bas"><span class="vz-nass-tur">Hadith</span><span class="vz-nass-konu">The wealthy person who delays repaying a debt</span></figcaption><p lang="ar" dir="rtl" class="vz-arapca">مَطْلُ الْغَنِيِّ ظُلْمٌ</p><p class="vz-meal">According to the narration of Abu Hurayra (may Allah be pleased with him), our Prophet said: “For a person who has the means, to put off repaying a debt is wrongdoing.”</p><p class="vz-kaynak">al-Bukhari, Hawalat, 1 (2287); Muslim, Musaqat, 33 (1564)</p></figure>

<p>Debt is a right that is made light of in this world but weighs heavily in the Hereafter. Our Prophet said: <strong>“The soul of the believer remains bound to its debt until it is paid.”</strong> (al-Tirmidhi, Janaiz, 76 (1078)) He declared that even a martyr’s sins are all forgiven except debt. (Muslim, Imara, 119 (1886)) At the funeral of a man in debt, he first said, “Pray over your companion yourselves,” and led the prayer himself only after Abu Qatada took the debt upon himself. (al-Bukhari, Kafala, 3 (2295)) On the other hand, Allah makes it easy to repay for the one who borrows with the intention of repaying. (al-Bukhari, Istiqrad, 2 (2387)) <strong>Saying “He has surely forgotten it by now” is no excuse in the Hereafter.</strong></p>

<figure class="vz-nass vz-hadis"><figcaption class="vz-nass-bas"><span class="vz-nass-tur">Hadith</span><span class="vz-nass-konu">I am the adversary of three</span></figcaption><p lang="ar" dir="rtl" class="vz-arapca">قَالَ اللَّهُ ثَلاَثَةٌ أَنَا خَصْمُهُمْ يَوْمَ الْقِيَامَةِ، رَجُلٌ أَعْطَى بِي ثُمَّ غَدَرَ، وَرَجُلٌ بَاعَ حُرًّا فَأَكَلَ ثَمَنَهُ، وَرَجُلٌ اسْتَأْجَرَ أَجِيرًا فَاسْتَوْفَى مِنْهُ، وَلَمْ يُعْطِ أَجْرَهُ</p><p class="vz-meal">Abu Hurayra (may Allah be pleased with him) reports: Allah said: “There are three people against whom I shall stand as adversary on the Day of Resurrection: one who gives his word in My name and then breaks it; one who sells a free person and consumes the price; and one who hires a worker, has the work fully done, and then does not pay the wage.”</p><p class="vz-kaynak">al-Bukhari, Buyu‘, 106 (2227); Ijara, 10 (2270); hadith qudsi</p></figure>

<figure class="vz-bolum-sahne vz-ara-sahne"><img class="vz-resim" src="/media/vaazlar/kul-hakki-ahiret-yolculugu/santiye-ucret-1600.webp" srcset="/media/vaazlar/kul-hakki-ahiret-yolculugu/santiye-ucret-480.webp 480w, /media/vaazlar/kul-hakki-ahiret-yolculugu/santiye-ucret-960.webp 960w, /media/vaazlar/kul-hakki-ahiret-yolculugu/santiye-ucret-1600.webp 1600w" sizes="(max-width: 640px) calc(100vw - 40px), (max-width: 1024px) calc(100vw - 64px), 736px" width="1600" height="900" alt="At a construction site at sunset, a foreman in an orange vest and hard hat shakes hands with a young worker in a muddy hoodie; in his other hand is a white envelope; behind them a crane, scaffolding, piles of bricks, a concrete mixer and the city skyline." loading="lazy" decoding="async"><figcaption>Illustrative scene: Paying the wage before the sweat dries</figcaption></figure>

<p>A worker’s wage is also a right of another: the employer must pay wages on time and in full. It is narrated that our Prophet said, “Pay the worker his wage before his sweat dries”; the chain of this narration contains weakness, but its meaning is supported by other authentic narrations. (Ibn Majah, Ruhun, 4 (2443)) The worker, too, must do the job properly; doing justice to the wage one receives is also a responsibility.</p>

<p>The rights of others begin not only in the wallet but also on the tongue. In his Farewell Sermon our Prophet said: <strong>“(O people!) Just as this (Dhu’l-Hijja) month of yours, in this (Mecca) city of yours, on this (‘Arafa) day of yours is sacred, so too are your blood, your property and your honour (dignity and chastity) sacred.”</strong> (al-Bukhari, ‘Ilm, 9; Muslim, Qasama, 30)</p>

<figure class="vz-nass vz-ayet"><figcaption class="vz-nass-bas"><span class="vz-nass-tur">Qur’anic verse</span><span class="vz-nass-konu">Do not mock one another</span></figcaption><p lang="ar" dir="rtl" class="vz-arapca">لَا يَسْخَرْ قَوْمٌ مِّن قَوْمٍ عَسَىٰ أَن يَكُونُوا خَيْرًا مِّنْهُمْ وَلَا نِسَاءٌ مِّن نِّسَاءٍ عَسَىٰ أَن يَكُنَّ خَيْرًا مِّنْهُنَّ وَلَا تَلْمِزُوا أَنفُسَكُمْ وَلَا تَنَابَزُوا بِالْأَلْقَابِ</p><p class="vz-meal">Let no people ridicule another people, for they may be better than they. Nor let women ridicule other women, for those ridiculed may be better than those who ridicule. Do not belittle one another, and do not call one another by offensive names.</p><p class="vz-kaynak">al-Hujurat, 49:11; excerpt from the verse</p></figure>

<p>The verse shows the place of human honour and dignity in Islamic ethics. (Kur’an Yolu, vol. 5, pp. 94–⁠95) Without touching a person’s property we can still violate their right: with a word we can break their heart, with gossip cause unrest in their family, with a slander bring about harm that lasts for years, and by telling a secret destroy their trust. <strong>Guarding our tongue is among the most important ways of protecting ourselves from violating the rights of others.</strong></p>

<figure class="vz-nass vz-hadis"><figcaption class="vz-nass-bas"><span class="vz-nass-tur">Hadith</span><span class="vz-nass-konu">The one from whose hand and tongue people are safe</span></figcaption><p lang="ar" dir="rtl" class="vz-arapca">الْمُسْلِمُ مَنْ سَلِمَ الْمُسْلِمُونَ مِنْ لِسَانِهِ وَيَدِهِ وَالْمُؤْمِنُ مَنْ أَمِنَهُ النَّاسُ عَلَى دِمَائِهِمْ وَأَمْوَالِهِمْ</p><p class="vz-meal">According to the narration of Abu Hurayra (may Allah be pleased with him), our Prophet said: “A Muslim is one from whose hand and tongue other Muslims are safe. A believer is one whom people trust with their lives and their property.”</p><p class="vz-kaynak">al-Tirmidhi, Iman, 12 (2627); al-Nasa’i, Iman, 8; the first sentence: al-Bukhari, Iman, 4 (10); Muslim, Iman, 65 (41)</p></figure>

<p>The tongue can both raise a person and bring them down. Our Prophet tells us that a servant may fall into Hell because of a careless word that earns Allah’s wrath, and may rise in rank with Allah through a good word spoken just as carelessly. (al-Bukhari, Riqaq, 23 (6478)) So our measure is this: <strong>“Whoever believes in Allah and the Last Day, let him say what is good or else keep silent.”</strong> (al-Bukhari, Adab, 31 (6018); Muslim, Iman, 74 (47))</p>

<header class="vz-bolum vz-devam" id="giybet-ve-iftira" tabindex="-1"><figure class="vz-bolum-sahne"><img class="vz-resim" src="/media/vaazlar/kul-hakki-ahiret-yolculugu/tuy-ruzgar-1600.webp" srcset="/media/vaazlar/kul-hakki-ahiret-yolculugu/tuy-ruzgar-480.webp 480w, /media/vaazlar/kul-hakki-ahiret-yolculugu/tuy-ruzgar-960.webp 960w, /media/vaazlar/kul-hakki-ahiret-yolculugu/tuy-ruzgar-1600.webp 1600w" sizes="(max-width: 640px) calc(100vw - 40px), (max-width: 1024px) calc(100vw - 64px), 736px" width="1600" height="900" alt="On the slope of a hill, feathers from a torn white pillow are scattered in every direction by the wind; below, a village of red-tiled houses, fields and a cloudy sky." loading="lazy" decoding="async"><figcaption>Illustrative scene: Scattered feathers</figcaption></figure><h3 class="vz-ara-baslik">Backbiting and slander</h3></header>

<figure class="vz-nass vz-ayet"><figcaption class="vz-nass-bas"><span class="vz-nass-tur">Qur’anic verse</span><span class="vz-nass-konu">The flesh of a dead brother</span></figcaption><p lang="ar" dir="rtl" class="vz-arapca">وَلَا يَغْتَب بَّعْضُكُم بَعْضًا أَيُحِبُّ أَحَدُكُمْ أَن يَأْكُلَ لَحْمَ أَخِيهِ مَيْتًا فَكَرِهْتُمُوهُ</p><p class="vz-meal">Do not backbite one another. Would any of you like to eat the flesh of his dead brother? You would loathe it!</p><p class="vz-kaynak">al-Hujurat, 49:12; excerpt from the verse</p></figure>

<figure class="vz-nass vz-hadis"><figcaption class="vz-nass-bas"><span class="vz-nass-tur">Hadith</span><span class="vz-nass-konu">What is backbiting?</span></figcaption><p lang="ar" dir="rtl" class="vz-arapca">أَتَدْرُونَ مَا الْغِيبَةُ قَالُوا اللَّهُ وَرَسُولُهُ أَعْلَمُ قَالَ ذِكْرُكَ أَخَاكَ بِمَا يَكْرَهُ قِيلَ أَفَرَأَيْتَ إِنْ كَانَ فِي أَخِي مَا أَقُولُ قَالَ إِنْ كَانَ فِيهِ مَا تَقُولُ فَقَدِ اغْتَبْتَهُ وَإِنْ لَمْ يَكُنْ فِيهِ فَقَدْ بَهَتَّهُ</p><p class="vz-meal"><em>Abu Hurayra (may Allah be pleased with him) reports: The Messenger of Allah asked, “Do you know what backbiting is?” The Companions said, “Allah and His Messenger know best.” He said, “It is to mention your brother with something he dislikes.” Someone asked, “What if what I say is actually true of my brother?” He replied, “If what you say is true of him, you have backbitten him; and if it is not, you have</em> <strong><em>slandered</em></strong> <em>him.”</em></p><p class="vz-kaynak">Muslim, Birr, 70 (2589)</p></figure>

<p>Backbiting is often committed with the words “but I am telling the truth”; our Prophet answered this objection: if the fault mentioned is in the person, it is backbiting; if not, it is <strong>buhtan</strong>, that is, slander. On the night of the Mi‘raj our Prophet came upon a group who were tearing at their faces and chests with nails of copper; Gabriel said, “These are the ones who eat people’s flesh and attack their honour and dignity.” (Abu Dawud, Adab, 35 (4878))</p>

<figure class="vz-nass vz-ayet"><figcaption class="vz-nass-bas"><span class="vz-nass-tur">Qur’anic verse</span><span class="vz-nass-konu">The ifk: when you heard that slander</span></figcaption><p lang="ar" dir="rtl" class="vz-arapca">لَّوْلَا إِذْ سَمِعْتُمُوهُ ظَنَّ الْمُؤْمِنُونَ وَالْمُؤْمِنَاتُ بِأَنفُسِهِمْ خَيْرًا وَقَالُوا هَٰذَا إِفْكٌ مُّبِينٌ</p><p class="vz-meal">When you heard it, should not the believing men and women have thought well of one another and said, “This is a clear slander”?</p><p class="vz-kaynak">an-Nur, 24:12</p></figure>

<p>The Qur’an recounts the gravest example of slander in the incident of the ifk: “Those who brought forth the slander are a group among you… Each of them will bear the sin he has earned; and for the one among them who took upon himself the greater part of it, there is a great punishment.” (an-Nur, 24:11) This slander cast against our mother ‘A’isha (may Allah be pleased with her) harmed not only her but also the household of our Prophet and the climate of trust in society. The verses also condemn passing slander from tongue to tongue: “You passed it on with your tongues and spoke with your mouths what you had no knowledge of; you thought it a small matter, while with Allah it is a grave one.” (an-Nur, 24:15) A baseless word can affect a person’s family, work and reputation for years. That is why slander is likened to a murder committed with the tongue.</p>

<header class="vz-bolum" id="sosyal-medya" tabindex="-1"><figure class="vz-bolum-sahne"><img class="vz-resim" src="/media/vaazlar/kul-hakki-ahiret-yolculugu/telefon-paylas-1600.webp" srcset="/media/vaazlar/kul-hakki-ahiret-yolculugu/telefon-paylas-480.webp 480w, /media/vaazlar/kul-hakki-ahiret-yolculugu/telefon-paylas-960.webp 960w, /media/vaazlar/kul-hakki-ahiret-yolculugu/telefon-paylas-1600.webp 1600w" sizes="(max-width: 640px) calc(100vw - 40px), (max-width: 1024px) calc(100vw - 64px), 736px" width="1600" height="900" alt="On an evening in a living room, a young man in socks sits on the sofa looking at the phone in his hand, his hood up; in the armchair beside him a white-bearded old man with an open book on his lap watches him with a smile; at the back a bookcase, a lampshade and the lights of the neighbouring houses through the window." loading="lazy" decoding="async"><figcaption>Illustrative scene: Before sharing</figcaption></figure><h2 class="vaaz-bolum-baslik"><span class="vz-no">IV.</span> The rights of others apply on social media too</h2><p class="vz-bolum-oz">A change of medium does not change responsibility: what we would not say to someone’s face, let us not say in a comment either.</p></header>

<p class="vz-hitap">Dear brothers and sisters! Especially our young people, whose phones never leave their hands, our dear children!</p>

<p>In our age we meet new faces of the rights of others: sharing a person’s photo without permission, spreading a piece of information about their private life, passing on news whose truth has not been checked, insulting people in comments, circulating images that humiliate someone: these are serious responsibilities. A change of medium does not change responsibility: what we would not say to someone’s face, let us not say in a comment either.</p>

<figure class="vz-nass vz-ayet"><figcaption class="vz-nass-bas"><span class="vz-nass-tur">Qur’anic verse</span><span class="vz-nass-konu">Verify the news</span></figcaption><p lang="ar" dir="rtl" class="vz-arapca">يَا أَيُّهَا الَّذِينَ آمَنُوا إِن جَاءَكُمْ فَاسِقٌ بِنَبَإٍ فَتَبَيَّنُوا أَن تُصِيبُوا قَوْمًا بِجَهَالَةٍ فَتُصْبِحُوا عَلَىٰ مَا فَعَلْتُمْ نَادِمِينَ</p><p class="vz-meal">So that you do not harm people unknowingly and then regret what you have done, when a transgressor brings you news, verify it.</p><p class="vz-kaynak">al-Hujurat, 49:6</p></figure>

<p>According to what the commentators report, this verse was revealed when the official sent to collect zakat from the Banu Mustaliq turned back before reaching the tribe and brought the unfounded report that they refused to pay zakat; when the report was investigated, it proved untrue. (Kur’an Yolu, al-Hujurat 49:6) Our Prophet also said: <strong>“It is enough lying for a person to relate everything he hears.”</strong> (Muslim, Muqaddima, 5; Abu Dawud, Adab, 80) The fatwa sources likewise ask that privacy, accurate information and avoidance of slander, backbiting and prying be respected in online spaces. <strong>A lie that is shared is a violation of the rights of others that multiplies with the number of those who share it.</strong></p>

<figure class="vz-sema"><figcaption><strong>Four questions before pressing the Share button</strong>Four questions to be asked in order before sharing.</figcaption><ol class="vz-cerceve-orta vz-sira"><li><span class="vz-cerceve-no">1</span><strong>Is it true?</strong><em>Have I checked the report’s source? (al-Hujurat, 49:6)</em></li><li><span class="vz-cerceve-no">2</span><strong>Is it permitted?</strong><em>Photo, voice, secret: with the owner’s consent?</em></li><li><span class="vz-cerceve-no">3</span><strong>Does it hurt?</strong><em>Does it humiliate someone or break a heart?</em></li><li><span class="vz-cerceve-no">4</span><strong>Can I answer for it?</strong><em>Every word spoken is recorded. (Qaf, 50:18)</em></li></ol><p class="vz-sema-alt">If even one is “no”: do not share.</p></figure>

<figure class="vz-nass vz-ayet"><figcaption class="vz-nass-bas"><span class="vz-nass-tur">Qur’anic verse</span><span class="vz-nass-konu">Every word spoken is recorded</span></figcaption><p lang="ar" dir="rtl" class="vz-arapca">مَّا يَلْفِظُ مِن قَوْلٍ إِلَّا لَدَيْهِ رَقِيبٌ عَتِيدٌ</p><p class="vz-meal">He utters not a word without a most attentive watcher beside him!</p><p class="vz-kaynak">Qaf, 50:18</p></figure>

<p>The same measure applies to our own posts: insults in the comments, children’s photos posted without permission, a secret or a fault spread in family WhatsApp groups… The Qur’an warns: “Do not pursue what you have no knowledge of! Surely hearing, sight and the heart, all of these will be questioned about it.” (al-Isra’, 17:36) <strong>Even behind a phone screen, Allah sees us.</strong></p>

<header class="vz-bolum" id="muflis" tabindex="-1"><figure class="vz-bolum-sahne"><img class="vz-resim" src="/media/vaazlar/kul-hakki-ahiret-yolculugu/mahser-ovasi-1600.webp" srcset="/media/vaazlar/kul-hakki-ahiret-yolculugu/mahser-ovasi-480.webp 480w, /media/vaazlar/kul-hakki-ahiret-yolculugu/mahser-ovasi-960.webp 960w, /media/vaazlar/kul-hakki-ahiret-yolculugu/mahser-ovasi-1600.webp 1600w" sizes="(max-width: 640px) calc(100vw - 40px), (max-width: 1024px) calc(100vw - 64px), 736px" width="1600" height="900" alt="Footprints stretching towards the horizon across an endless plain of cracked red earth; beams of light descend to the ground between the clouds on the horizon." loading="lazy" decoding="async"><figcaption>Illustrative scene: The day of reckoning</figcaption></figure><h2 class="vaaz-bolum-baslik"><span class="vz-no">V.</span> True bankruptcy in the Hereafter: the hadith of the bankrupt</h2><p class="vz-bolum-oz">The bankrupt, who comes with prayer, fasting and zakat but loses his good deeds to those entitled.</p></header>

<p>The hadith that describes most strikingly the weight of the rights of others in the Hereafter is the hadith of the bankrupt.</p>

<figure class="vz-nass vz-hadis"><figcaption class="vz-nass-bas"><span class="vz-nass-tur">Hadith</span><span class="vz-nass-konu">Do you know who the bankrupt is?</span></figcaption><p lang="ar" dir="rtl" class="vz-arapca">أَتَدْرُونَ مَا الْمُفْلِسُ</p><p lang="ar" dir="rtl" class="vz-arapca">قَالُوا الْمُفْلِسُ فِينَا مَنْ لاَ دِرْهَمَ لَهُ وَلاَ مَتَاعَ</p><p lang="ar" dir="rtl" class="vz-arapca">إِنَّ الْمُفْلِسَ مِنْ أُمَّتِي يَأْتِي يَوْمَ الْقِيَامَةِ بِصَلاَةٍ وَصِيَامٍ وَزَكَاةٍ</p><p class="vz-meal">Abu Hurayra (may Allah be pleased with him) reports: The Messenger of Allah asked, “Do you know who the bankrupt is?” The Companions said, “For us the bankrupt is the one who has no money or goods.” Our Prophet then said: “The bankrupt of my community is the one who comes on the Day of Resurrection with prayer, fasting and zakat, but who has also insulted this one, slandered that one, consumed the property of another, shed the blood of another and beaten another. The reward of his good deeds is given to this one and that one. If his good deeds run out before the rights owed by him are paid, some of the sins of those entitled are taken and placed on him; then he is thrown into Hell.”</p><p class="vz-kaynak">Muslim, Birr, 59 (2581); al-Tirmidhi, Qiyama, 2 (2418)</p></figure>

<figure class="vz-sema"><figcaption><strong>The bankrupt of the Hereafter</strong>Muslim, Birr, 59 (2581): The one whose good deeds are distributed to those entitled.</figcaption><div class="vz-denge"><div class="vz-kefe"><p class="vz-kefe-ad">What they bring</p><ul><li>Prayer</li><li>Fasting</li><li>Zakat</li></ul></div><div class="vz-terazi"><svg viewBox="0 0 24 24" fill="none" stroke="currentColor" stroke-width="1.8" stroke-linecap="round" stroke-linejoin="round" aria-hidden="true" focusable="false"><path d="M4 12h14M13 7l5 5-5 5"/></svg></div><div class="vz-kefe"><p class="vz-kefe-ad">Rightful owners</p><ul><li>Insulted</li><li>Slandered</li><li>Property consumed</li><li>Blood shed</li><li>Beaten</li></ul></div></div><p class="vz-sema-alt">The good deeds are given to them; if the good deeds run out, their sins are loaded on the bankrupt.</p></figure>

<p>A person may pray, fast and give zakat; but if they wrong people, take their property unjustly or hurt them with the tongue, they face a great danger in the Hereafter. Acts of worship are not worthless; but they must make a person upright and just: prayer should keep us from evil, fasting should strengthen the will and zakat generosity, and the Qur’an should teach justice and compassion.</p>

<figure class="vz-nass vz-hadis"><figcaption class="vz-nass-bas"><span class="vz-nass-tur">Hadith</span><span class="vz-nass-konu">The right of the hornless sheep</span></figcaption><p lang="ar" dir="rtl" class="vz-arapca">لَتُؤَدُّنَّ الْحُقُوقَ إِلَى أَهْلِهَا يَوْمَ الْقِيَامَةِ حَتَّى يُقَادَ لِلشَّاةِ الْجَلْحَاءِ مِنَ الشَّاةِ الْقَرْنَاءِ</p><p class="vz-meal">According to the narration of Abu Hurayra (may Allah be pleased with him), our Prophet said: “On the Day of Resurrection rights will certainly be returned to their owners, so that even the hornless sheep will have its right taken from the horned sheep.”</p><p class="vz-kaynak">Muslim, Birr, 60 (2582)</p></figure>

<p>On a day when rights are observed even among animals, the wrong done by one person to another is not forgotten. <strong>A debt that could be paid today with a wallet will be paid there with prayers, fasts and zakat.</strong></p>

<header class="vz-bolum" id="aile-komsu-is" tabindex="-1"><figure class="vz-bolum-sahne"><img class="vz-resim" src="/media/vaazlar/kul-hakki-ahiret-yolculugu/hediye-esit-1600.webp" srcset="/media/vaazlar/kul-hakki-ahiret-yolculugu/hediye-esit-480.webp 480w, /media/vaazlar/kul-hakki-ahiret-yolculugu/hediye-esit-960.webp 960w, /media/vaazlar/kul-hakki-ahiret-yolculugu/hediye-esit-1600.webp 1600w" sizes="(max-width: 640px) calc(100vw - 40px), (max-width: 1024px) calc(100vw - 64px), 736px" width="1600" height="900" alt="In a living room, a father kneeling on the carpet holds out two identical gift packages to his son and daughter at the same moment; the children smile, and on the sofa their mother in a headscarf watches them; autumn trees through the window." loading="lazy" decoding="async"><figcaption>Illustrative scene: Justice among children</figcaption></figure><h2 class="vaaz-bolum-baslik"><span class="vz-no">VI.</span> The rights of others in the family, the neighbourhood and working life</h2><p class="vz-bolum-oz">A right does not lapse through closeness: the rights of those closest to us are rights too.</p></header>

<p>Most often we violate the rights of those closest to us: saying “after all, it is family,” we make light of the rights of our spouse, our children, our parents and our siblings. Yet <strong>a right does not lapse through closeness:</strong> being a sibling does not permit taking property unjustly, being a spouse does not permit wrongdoing, being a child does not permit breaking someone’s dignity.</p>

<figure class="vz-nass vz-hadis"><figcaption class="vz-nass-bas"><span class="vz-nass-tur">Hadith</span><span class="vz-nass-konu">Be just among your children</span></figcaption><p lang="ar" dir="rtl" class="vz-arapca">فَاتَّقُوا اللَّهَ، وَاعْدِلُوا بَيْنَ أَوْلاَدِكُمْ</p><p class="vz-meal"><em>al-Nu‘man ibn Bashir (may Allah be pleased with him) reports: His father wanted to give him a gift, and his mother had asked that our Prophet be a witness. Our Prophet asked, “Have you given all your other children the like of this?” When the answer was “No,” he said,</em> <strong><em>“Fear Allah, and be just among your children.”</em></strong> <em>In Muslim’s version he also says, “Then do not make me a witness; I do not bear witness to injustice.”</em></p><p class="vz-kaynak">al-Bukhari, Hiba, 13 (2587); Muslim, Hibat (1623)</p></figure>

<p>Favouring one child unjustly leads to jealousy and hurt among siblings and damages the child’s dignity; that is, it gives rise to a violation of the rights of others within the family.</p>

<figure class="vz-nass vz-ayet"><figcaption class="vz-nass-bas"><span class="vz-nass-tur">Qur’anic verse</span><span class="vz-nass-konu">Men have a share, and so do women</span></figcaption><p lang="ar" dir="rtl" class="vz-arapca">لِّلرِّجَالِ نَصِيبٌ مِّمَّا تَرَكَ الْوَالِدَانِ وَالْأَقْرَبُونَ وَلِلنِّسَاءِ نَصِيبٌ مِّمَّا تَرَكَ الْوَالِدَانِ وَالْأَقْرَبُونَ مِمَّا قَلَّ مِنْهُ أَوْ كَثُرَ نَصِيبًا مَّفْرُوضًا</p><p class="vz-meal">For men there is a share of what parents and relatives leave; and for women there is a share of what parents and relatives leave, be it little or much — a fixed share.</p><p class="vz-kaynak">an-Nisa’, 4:7</p></figure>

<figure class="vz-bolum-sahne vz-ara-sahne"><img class="vz-resim" src="/media/vaazlar/kul-hakki-ahiret-yolculugu/miras-kardesler-1600.webp" srcset="/media/vaazlar/kul-hakki-ahiret-yolculugu/miras-kardesler-480.webp 480w, /media/vaazlar/kul-hakki-ahiret-yolculugu/miras-kardesler-960.webp 960w, /media/vaazlar/kul-hakki-ahiret-yolculugu/miras-kardesler-1600.webp 1600w" sizes="(max-width: 640px) calc(100vw - 40px), (max-width: 1024px) calc(100vw - 64px), 736px" width="1600" height="900" alt="At a table with tea cups, a file and a bunch of keys, a man in a jacket pushes the file towards two women in headscarves sitting opposite him; the women look at it attentively." loading="lazy" decoding="async"><figcaption>Illustrative scene: At the inheritance table</figcaption></figure>

<p>A wrong frequently met with here in Europe is inheritance: the field, house or shop back in the home country; sisters talked out of their share with the words “You married; your share is in your husband’s house”; “voluntary” signatures obtained through shame or pressure. Allah Himself has fixed the shares and has called them “the limits set by Allah.” (an-Nisa’, 4:13) <strong>To deprive a sister of her share without her free consent is a violation of the rights of others committed against those closest to us.</strong></p>

<figure class="vz-sema"><figcaption><strong>A right does not lapse through closeness</strong>At no degree of closeness does a right lapse.</figcaption><ol class="vz-halka"><li><strong>Me</strong></li><li><strong>Spouse, children</strong></li><li><strong>Parents and siblings</strong></li><li><strong>Neighbour, colleague</strong></li><li><strong>Society, public</strong></li></ol><p class="vz-sema-alt">A believer is just to the nearest and the farthest.</p></figure>

<p>The neighbour’s right is also our responsibility. Our Prophet said three times, “By Allah, he does not believe!” and when asked, “Who, O Messenger of Allah?” he said, “The one whose neighbour is not safe from his harm!” (al-Bukhari, Adab, 29 (6016)) Noise at night, parking that blocks the neighbour’s door, leaving shared areas neglected: these fall under this right. At work, neglecting one’s hours, obtaining a sick note one has not earned, using the employer’s materials for private jobs are also violations of the rights of others. <strong>“Each of you is a shepherd, and each of you is responsible for those in your charge.”</strong> (al-Bukhari, Jumu‘a, 11 (893); Muslim, Imara, 20 (1829))</p>

<figure class="vz-nass vz-ayet"><figcaption class="vz-nass-bas"><span class="vz-nass-tur">Qur’anic verse</span><span class="vz-nass-konu">No prophet misappropriates</span></figcaption><p lang="ar" dir="rtl" class="vz-arapca">وَمَا كَانَ لِنَبِيٍّ أَن يَغُلَّ وَمَن يَغْلُلْ يَأْتِ بِمَا غَلَّ يَوْمَ الْقِيَامَةِ</p><p class="vz-meal">No prophet would ever misappropriate the due of those who fought. Whoever commits such an injustice will come on the Day of Resurrection carrying what he misappropriated.</p><p class="vz-kaynak">Al ‘Imran, 3:161; excerpt from the verse</p></figure>

<figure class="vz-nass vz-hadis"><figcaption class="vz-nass-bas"><span class="vz-nass-tur">Hadith</span><span class="vz-nass-konu">A camel groaning on his neck</span></figcaption><p lang="ar" dir="rtl" class="vz-arapca">لاَ أُلْفِيَنَّ أَحَدَكُمْ يَجِيءُ يَوْمَ الْقِيَامَةِ عَلَى رَقَبَتِهِ بَعِيرٌ لَهُ رُغَاءٌ يَقُولُ يَا رَسُولَ اللَّهِ أَغِثْنِي فَأَقُولُ لاَ أَمْلِكُ لَكَ شَيْئًا قَدْ أَبْلَغْتُكَ</p><p class="vz-meal">Abu Hurayra (may Allah be pleased with him) reports: The Messenger of Allah, stressing the gravity of betrayal over the spoils of war (ghulul), said: “Let me not find any of you on the Day of Resurrection coming to me with a camel groaning on his neck, saying, ‘O Messenger of Allah, help me!’ and I shall say, ‘I can do nothing for you; I conveyed the message to you.’”</p><p class="vz-kaynak">Muslim, Imara, 24 (1831); similar narration: al-Bukhari, Jihad, 189 (3073)</p></figure>

<p>Public property is not ownerless; it is a shared trust. <strong>Ghulul</strong> is taking from the spoils of war without permission; using public resources for personal gain is also understood within this scope. When Ibn al-Lutbiyya, who collected zakat, said, “This was given to me as a gift,” our Prophet said, “Why did he not sit in his father’s house and see whether a gift would be given to him?” (al-Bukhari, Hiba, 17 (2597); Muslim, Imara, 26 (1832)) What is taken by reason of office is not a gift but ghulul — even if it is a needle. (Muslim, Imara, 30 (1833))</p>

<p>When we notice our fault, there is a way out. Our Prophet says:</p>

<figure class="vz-nass vz-hadis"><figcaption class="vz-nass-bas"><span class="vz-nass-tur">Hadith</span><span class="vz-nass-konu">Let him settle accounts and seek release today</span></figcaption><p lang="ar" dir="rtl" class="vz-arapca">مَنْ كَانَتْ لَهُ مَظْلَمَةٌ لِأَخِيهِ مِنْ عِرْضِهِ أَوْ شَيْءٌ فَلْيَتَحَلَّلْهُ مِنْهُ الْيَوْمَ قَبْلَ أَنْ لَا يَكُونَ دِينَارٌ وَلَا دِرْهَمٌ</p><p class="vz-meal">Abu Hurayra (may Allah be pleased with him) reports: Our Prophet said: “Whoever has wronged his brother in respect of his honour or his property, let him settle accounts and seek release from him today, before the day when there will be neither dinar nor dirham. Otherwise, in proportion to his wrongdoing, some of his good deeds will be taken and given to the one he wronged; and if he has no good deeds, some of the other’s sins will be taken and placed on him.”</p><p class="vz-kaynak">al-Bukhari, Mazalim, 10 (2449)</p></figure>

<figure class="vz-sema"><figcaption><strong>Four steps out of the burden of the rights of others</strong>al-Bukhari, Mazalim, 10 (2449).</figcaption><ol class="vz-sube"><li><span class="vz-sube-ust">Step 1</span><strong>Give up</strong>Stop the wrongdoing at once.</li><li><span class="vz-sube-ust">Step 2</span><strong>Feel regret</strong>Repent sincerely, resolve never to repeat it.</li><li><span class="vz-sube-ust">Step 3</span><strong>Repay</strong>Return it to its owner, make good the harm.</li><li><span class="vz-sube-ust">Step 4</span><strong>Seek release</strong>Win their heart, ask for release; if you cannot reach them: prayer and charity.</li></ol></figure>

<p>First we must give up the wrong, feel regret and return what is owed: give back the property, pay the debt, make good the damage, make up the underpaid wage, and apologise to the heart we have broken. If the owner has died, the right is handed to the heirs; if the owner cannot be reached, its value is given to the poor on the owner’s behalf; in the case of backbiting, where this is not possible, there are repentance, a good prayer and good deeds done on the person’s behalf. The details are in the guide to settling accounts and seeking release below.</p>

<figure class="vz-sozler" id="yedi-soz"><figcaption><span>Let us keep it in our wallets and hang it on the inside of our doors</span><strong>The pledge of settling accounts and seeking release</strong></figcaption><ol class="vz-soz-liste"><li><strong>I will write down my debt and pay it on time.</strong> If I cannot pay, I will go to my creditor without running away, explain the situation and ask for a new deadline.</li><li><strong>I will keep my scale, my invoice and my word true.</strong> I will state the defect of goods, not hide the deficiency of a service, and not take advantage of a customer’s distraction.</li><li><strong>I will pay wages before the sweat of the brow has dried, and do justice to the wage I receive.</strong> I will not delay my worker’s wage, and I will not neglect my working hours and my work.</li><li><strong>I will not speak behind people’s backs, and I will not share what I hear without verifying it.</strong> I will gently warn a gathering where backbiting is going on, and I will not spread photos or secrets without permission.</li><li><strong>I will be just in my home.</strong> I will treat my spouse and children equally and with affection, and give my sisters their inheritance rights in full.</li><li><strong>I will regard public property as a trust.</strong> I will validate my ticket, make my declaration truthfully, and not take assistance I do not deserve.</li><li><strong>This week I will settle accounts and seek release with one person.</strong> I will win back a heart I have broken, pay a debt I had forgotten, and call a relative with whom I have fallen out.</li></ol><p class="vz-sozler-alt"><a href="/vaazlar/en/the-rights-of-others-a-heavy-responsibility.pdf#page=13">Printable version: PDF, page 13</a></p></figure>

<p><strong>A right owed to another is not lifted until it is returned to its owner or the owner releases us from it; whoever does not settle it today will pay with their good deeds on the day when dinar and dirham are of no use.</strong> As we leave this Friday prayer, let us ask three questions: <strong>Whose right is on me? Whose heart have I broken? Whose debt have I forgotten?</strong> Let us call the first name that comes to mind today.</p>

<figure class="vz-nass vz-hadis"><figcaption class="vz-nass-bas"><span class="vz-nass-tur">Hadith</span><span class="vz-nass-konu">The account of one who dies in debt</span></figcaption><p lang="ar" dir="rtl" class="vz-arapca">مَنْ مَاتَ وَعَلَيْهِ دِينَارٌ أَوْ دِرْهَمٌ قُضِيَ مِنْ حَسَنَاتِهِ لَيْسَ ثَمَّ دِينَارٌ وَلَا دِرْهَمٌ</p><p class="vz-meal">According to the narration of Ibn ‘Umar (may Allah be pleased with him), our Prophet said: “Whoever dies owing a dinar or a dirham, that debt will be paid from his good deeds, for there is neither dinar nor dirham there.”</p><p class="vz-kaynak">Ibn Majah, Sadaqat, 12 (2414)</p></figure>

<p>This hadith is the essence of our key sentence: the debt we make light of and postpone today will be paid there from our most precious capital, our good deeds. So instead of postponing, let us take a small step this week: a phone call, an apology, a payment.</p>

<p>Every day that a matter of others’ rights is postponed makes it heavier. Let us get the first right we remember to its owner this week; if we cannot reach them, let us give charity on their behalf and pray. <strong>Our Lord does not leave alone the one who intends to put a right back in its place.</strong></p>

<figure class="vz-kavramlar"><figcaption><strong>Seven key concepts</strong>Words to keep in mind while reading and listening to the sermon.</figcaption><dl class="vz-kavram-liste"><div><dt>The rights of others (huquq al-‘ibad)</dt><dd>People’s material and moral rights, such as their life, property, honour and dignity; they lapse only when paid to their owner or when the owner’s consent is obtained.</dd></div><div><dt>The trust (amana)</dt><dd>Everything left in our care in confidence: belongings, duty, word, secrets, health, children and public resources.</dd></div><div><dt>Backbiting and slander</dt><dd>Backbiting (ghiba) is mentioning a person in their absence with a trait they would dislike; slander (buhtan) is attributing to them what they do not have.</dd></div><div><dt>Ghulul</dt><dd>Secretly setting aside for oneself a part of the common property one holds by virtue of a duty; today, reaching for public resources.</dd></div><div><dt>Public right</dt><dd>Property and resources that belong to the community: taxes, transport, social benefits, shared spaces.</dd></div><div><dt>The bankrupt (muflis)</dt><dd>Not a person without money, but one whose good deeds are distributed to those entitled and who is loaded with their sins.</dd></div><div><dt>Settling accounts and seeking release (helallesme)</dt><dd>Paying what is owed to its owner, or bringing the owner to agree willingly and release the right.</dd></div></dl></figure>
